# Research Note — The FSB Fontaine Scale Drawing

## Archive ID: MDM-RESEARCH-FONTAINE-DRAWING

## Recorded: June 2026, from the 30 Jan 1971 letter and conversation with the son of SGT Marvin Miller

---

### Overview

In his letter home dated **30 January 1971**, written just after FSB Silver was torn down
and the company moved to a new base (later named **FSB Fontaine**), Marvin reports, in a single
matter-of-fact sentence:

> "I had to draw a scaled down model of the new base for the Colonel."

The drawing itself does not survive, and there is no known recollection from anyone who saw or
used it. This note records what the archive can and cannot establish about it, so the open
threads are preserved should a 2/8 Cav veteran ever surface to answer them. **Nothing here is
asserted beyond what the sources support; the identifications below are inference and possibility,
not record.**

---

### Background — why Marvin was likely the one asked

Marvin had formal arts training before the war: one semester at the **Art Institute of
Pittsburgh** (fall 1969), cut short by the December 1969 draft lottery (see
`MDM-FAMILY-BACKGROUND`). By family account he was a genuinely capable artist. That training is
the most plausible reason he, rather than another soldier, was asked to render the new base to
scale.

**Open question:** how the Army came to know of his art training. It does not appear to have
been a formally recorded skill, and the family does not know how it surfaced — a mention to a
sergeant or officer, a sketch someone saw, or simply word getting around. He reports the task
without explanation.

---

### Which "Colonel"?

"The Colonel" was almost certainly the **battalion commander** of the 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry.
Troops routinely called the 2/8 CO "the Colonel" even though a battalion is a Lieutenant Colonel
(LTC) billet rather than a full colonel's command.

The battalion command timeline the archive establishes:

- **LTC Michael J. Conrad** — commanding in 1970 (Dog's Head campaign, March 1970).
- **LTC W.G. "Gary" Bacon** — attested in command through **April–May 1971** (Neal and Bacon
  accounts of 20–22 Apr; Operation Thunder, late May). Sometimes misreported "Willis Bacon."
- **LTC Thomas "Tom" Blagg** — assumed command **14 July 1971**.

The drawing dates to **late January 1971**, which falls *before* Bacon's first firm attestation
in the records held here. Bacon is therefore **plausible but unconfirmed** as the commander who
made the request; there may have been a CO between Conrad (1970) and Bacon (April 1971). LTC
Bacon is deceased, so a direct recollection from him is no longer possible. **The late-January
1971 battalion commander is not pinned down in the archive.**

---

### Who would have seen or kept it, and where it was made

A scaled base layout is principally an **operations (S-3)** product — defensive geometry, fields
of fire, siting of the TOC/FDC and crew-served weapons — more than an intelligence (S-2) one,
though both shops would plausibly have seen it. The archive names **Maj. Charles Kinsey** as the
battalion S-3 (attested April 1971) and elsewhere references a battalion intelligence officer.
A drawing of this kind would most likely have lived at the **battalion CP/TOC** and passed across
the S-3 shop as a planning aid for laying out the new base. (A related thread: firebase geometry
became more deliberate under Blagg — FSB Fanning was later rebuilt at Jeffries "along more modern
geometric lines.")

**Where Marvin physically made it** is unknown. The Silver CP during teardown is a reasonable
guess, but because the drawing depicted the *new* base, it could equally have been done forward
at Fontaine or at the battalion CP. The letter does not say.

---

### Why only once? — the "not standard practice" question

Marvin mentions this task exactly once across roughly 41 surviving letters and never refers to
drawing another. Two readings, pointing the same way:

1. **It was a one-off, not a billet.** Firebases were normally sited and built by engineers
   working from real survey plans, not by a recon rifleman with a pencil. If this were a routine,
   rotating duty there would likely be a designated role doing it each time; if Marvin were the
   unit's standing artist, one would expect a string of such jobs over a year — and plausibly a
   second mention. Its single, flat appearance reads as ad hoc: a new base going up, a quick
   scaled rendering needed, and someone who knew the Cat Platoon trooper could draw.

2. **Opportunity was rare.** A layout drawing is only needed when a base is *established or
   redesigned*, which was infrequent. The company spent the tour rotating among bases that
   already existed (Silver, Mace, Fontaine, Fanning, King, Jeffries). Fontaine in late January
   was one of the few genuine stand-ups where Marvin's platoon was the teardown-and-move crew.
   Few occasions, one drawing.

**Caveat.** Marvin's letters are a thin evidence base for any military specific — the archive
notes repeatedly how little he says about his actual duties (the danger, as a rule, goes
unmentioned). Absence of a second mention is therefore not proof there was no second drawing.
But that he troubled to mention *this* one, when he omits so much else, is itself a small tell:
it stuck with him because it was out of the ordinary.
