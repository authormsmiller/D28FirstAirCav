## What this document teaches about the 1967 gap generally

1. **The 30–31 May cluster is the strongest circumstantial lead surfaced in the 1967
   backfill so far** — an exact-date, gridded, division-recognized action (An Qui, BS 6811,
   96 enemy KIA) that the division's own historians tagged to 1st Brigade specifically for
   this date range — but it stops short of a battalion match, and it would be a mistake to
   treat "1st Brigade" as shorthand for "2/8 Cav" without a source that actually narrows it
   down among 1/8, 2/8, and 1/12 Cav.
2. **2/8 Cav's brigade assignment for May–Jul 1967 combat operations is now an open
   question, not a known fact carried over from April.** This document contains no task-org
   table at all, unlike AD0385642's clean TAB A. Any future pass should not assume the
   February–April Pershing OPCON swap (2/8 to 3rd Brigade) either continued or reverted by
   late May without a source that says so directly.
3. **A new OPCON fact for the timeline (Task #11):** the division's *own* 3rd Brigade was
   itself chopped OPCON to the 4th Infantry Division for Operation GREELEY, 24 June–25 July
   1967, in Kontum Province — the mirror image of April's finding (2/8 OPCON'd *to* 3rd
   Brigade). OPCON swaps this year are cascading across multiple echelons, not a one-time
   arrangement — exactly the kind of detail that would trip up a NARA record request that
   assumed a single fixed brigade designation for the whole year.
4. **Verdict:** a step up in yield from AD0385642 for the KIA question (a real date/place
   match exists here, even if not a battalion match) but a step down for task-organization
   clarity (no table at all). Both documents converge on the same lesson: division-level
   ORLLs can locate a KIA cluster in time and space, and can rule out or partially support
   a brigade-level tie, but resolving to D Company specifically requires the 2/8 Cav Daily
   Staff Journal or an equivalent unit-level record.
