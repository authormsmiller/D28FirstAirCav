## April 20, 1971 — Assault, Contact, Withdrawal

---

### [Morning — approx. 0800–0900] Air assault insertion near Gia Ray

D Company "Angry Skipper," 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry (Airmobile), 1st Cavalry Division — approximately forty men with full gear — lifted and air-assaulted into an area near Gia Ray, Long Khanh Province, approximately 12 kilometers southeast of Gia Ray village and 2 kilometers north of National Highway 1 (QL 1).

**Command:** Company Commander CPT William D. Neal.

**Platoons on the ground:**
- Range Platoon (2nd Platoon) — lead element
- Cat Platoon (3rd / "Wild Cat") — south bank

**Not present:**
- Skull Platoon (1st / "White Skull") — per Neal, operating on a separate assignment nearby (location not specified). Skull is NOT part of the initial insertion or the engagement. They are choppered in as a late-afternoon reinforcement after the firefight.

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey); Neal account (Maxey); Bacon deposition; Neal (Skull separate assignment).*

---

### [Morning] Movement along truck road; preliminary creek crossing

The company moved out along a truck road. Range Platoon crossed a small stream — boots got wet. This is a separate water feature from the Suoi Gia Ui; the crossing is noted by Dillon as a routine movement marker, not a tactical obstacle.

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey).*

---

### [~Noon] Range Platoon sets up perimeter for lunch — north bank, Suoi Gia Ui

The three squads established a perimeter along the north bank of the Suoi Gia Ui. A military working dog (German Shepherd) assigned for booby-trap detection was operating with Range. The company set up for a midday rest.

Neal's diagram ("Positions just after noon") shows Range north of the stream, Cat and HQS south, and a unit labeled "Skull" in column south of the stream. **Note:** Skull was not present — per Neal they were on a separate assignment. The "Skull" label in the Maxey diagram is a misidentification; the unit shown is almost certainly Cat's trail squad or another Cat element. See oq-08.

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey); Neal account / diagram (Maxey); Neal (Skull separate assignment).*

---

### [Early afternoon — approx. 1200–1300] Second squad spots enemy near the creek

Second squad moved down the creek and encountered a group of enemy soldiers — described by Dillon as "Viet Cong taking a bath in the creek." The contact was reported up.

Stan Sargent's squad was ordered to go down and investigate. The German Shepherd ran ahead of the platoon as it moved east along the north bank.

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey).*

---

### [Early-to-mid afternoon] Range Platoon moves east along north bank; initial contact

Range moved east along the north bank of the Suoi Gia Ui and walked into the fortified headquarters of the **83rd NVA rear service unit** and its security detachment.

The base camp was extremely well constructed: double interlocking fires throughout, with six feet and more of mud and log overhead cover. An NVA rear service unit is equivalent to a US brigade headquarters; the security detachment consisted of seasoned fighters on rotation from front-line duties.

Neither Neal, Dillon, nor LT Bill Bott (Range Six, platoon leader) believed before the contact that they had encountered a prepared base camp. Neal later wrote: *"The one consolation I have is that both Stan Dillon and Bill Bott also believed we had come across a moving unit, and not one dug into a very well defended base camp."*

*Source: Neal written account (Maxey); Dillon interview (Maxey).*

---

### [Contact] Chicom claymores detonate; Cardwell and Drinkard killed

At least two command-detonated Chicom claymore mines exploded in the tree canopy. CPL James Melvin Cardwell and CPL Danny George Drinkard, walking point, caught the full blast. Both were killed instantly.

Intense automatic weapons fire — full automatic, both sides — erupted immediately from the bunker complex.

*Source: Neal account (Maxey); Maxey footnote 3.*

---

### [Firefight] SSG Dillon wounded; Hall killed; Stan takes over the M-60

The volume of fire from the bunkers was immediate and sustained. SSG Stan Dillon, Range Platoon Sergeant, was shot three times and struck by claymore shrapnel — including wounds to his collarbone, arm, kneecap, lung, and eardrum. One bullet was stopped by a Bible in his shirt pocket; it came to rest at 2nd Corinthians 5:7: *"for we walk by faith, not by sight."*

CPL Joseph Lindsey Hall moved forward from the rear of the column to assist Dillon, who had been badly hit. Hall was killed by enemy rifle fire.

Stan Sargent, the assistant machine gunner, took over Hall's M-60. A second machine gun continued firing simultaneously. In the face of dead and wounded falling around him, Stan held his position and provided sustained covering fire.

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey); Neal account (Maxey); Maxey footnote 3.*

---

### [Firefight] Cat pinned on south bank; Neal observes M-60

Cat Platoon, on the south bank of the Suoi Gia Ui, was pinned by intensifying fire from across the creek. The north bank at the firefight location is significantly higher than the south bank — a result of centuries of drainage off Núi Chứa Chan building elevated ground to the north. The 83rd NVA had exploited this terrain, constructing their bunkers into the elevated reverse slope. Cat's fire hit the bank face rather than the bunkers.

CPT Neal, from the south bank, remembered: *"… somebody on the north side was raising royal hell with an M-60 machine gun. I hoped it was one of ours. I remember thinking that maybe it wouldn't be too bad if we still had an M-60 engaged, though the volume of M-16 fire had become really weak."*

*Source: Neal account (Maxey).*

---

### [Firefight] Withdrawal of Range; Cat trail squad flanking action

Two M-60s sustained fire through the withdrawal. Range Platoon crossed back over the Suoi Gia Ui and joined the main US force.

**Skull label in Maxey corrected — oq-08 (partially resolved).** The Maxey account and diagrams attribute the flanking movement (moving west along the stream, crossing, coming in behind Range) to "Skull Platoon." Neal confirms Skull was not present — they were on a separate assignment nearby. The unit that executed this movement was Cat's trail squad or a Cat element. The "Skull" label in the Maxey diagrams and narrative is a misidentification. Location of Skull's separate assignment is not stated by Neal.

*Source: Neal account (Maxey); Neal (Skull on separate assignment); Davis account; terrain analysis (see event index).*

---

### [Firefight — concurrent] Medevac; Stan evacuated unconscious

Stan Sargent was struck by shrapnel from a Chicom claymore mine. He was medevacked by helicopter. SSG Dillon, also medevacked, remembered: *"I remember flying out on the helicopter with Stan — he wasn't conscious."*

LT Bill Bott (Range Six) was wounded; per Davis's account, he was the last man to cross the stream.

PFC Gary Collins took over the second M-60 from SGT Steve Schneck (who had continued firing despite shrapnel wounds across his back) and helped carry Dillon across the stream. Doc Harvey Brothers, Range Platoon medic, treated Dillon at the stream bank.

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey); Davis account; event index.*

---

### [Afternoon] Artillery, ARA, and air support engaged

Direct support and general support artillery (including 8-inch guns from Xuan Loc), aerial rocket artillery (ARA), and Air Cav gunships were brought to bear on the bunker complex throughout the afternoon.

Six USAF F-100s dropped 500-lb bombs on the complex in the late afternoon.

*Source: Neal account; event index.*

---

### [Late afternoon] Skull Platoon choppered in as reinforcement

Skull Platoon was inserted as a late-afternoon reinforcement to reconstitute D Company's strength and establish the defensive perimeter. LT Michael Martin (Skull Platoon leader) took command of perimeter defense following the reinforcement.

*Source: event index; Neal account.*

---

### [Evening] Three KIA remain at contact site; defensive perimeter established

CPL Cardwell, CPL Drinkard, and CPL Hall could not be recovered from the ambush site under enemy fire. They were left with reluctance. D Company established a defensive perimeter east of the bunker complex.

*Source: event index; Neal account.*

---

## Night of April 20–21, 1971

---

### [Night — continuous] Artillery through the night

CPT Neal and acting FO SGT Steve Kahnke directed continuous artillery fire through the night onto the bunker complex.

Frank Guidara, logistics officer, organized ammunition resupply choppered in during or after the fight.

*Source: Neal account; event index.*

---

## April 21, 1971

---

### CPL Stanton Gerald Sargent dies of wounds

CPL Stanton Gerald Sargent, 21 years old, 112 days in-country, died of wounds received in the previous day's engagement. SSG Dillon, who had been medevacked alongside him: *"He died the next day."*

*Source: Dillon interview (Maxey); Martin oral history (ASU, 2018); VVMF records.*

---

## April 23, 1971 (corroborated — McGrew calendar)

---

### Combat assault to recover the three KIA

A combat assault was conducted to recover the remains of CPL Cardwell, CPL Drinkard, and CPL Hall from the ambush site. McGrew's calendar places a CA to Fontaine on April 23. No source explicitly documents the recovery mission, but it is strongly implied. See oq-01.

*Source: McGrew calendar (corroborating); event index.*

---

## April 24, 1971

---

### UH-1H crash; FSB renamed Fanning

A helicopter crash four days after the contact killed pilot CW2 Martin Fanning. The fire support base was subsequently renamed in his honor. Neal's deposition uses "FSB Fanning" when describing April 20 — almost certainly a retrospective slip; the working name at the time of the contact was Fontaine. See oq-07.

*Source: event index; crash-fsb-fontaine-1971-04-24 record.*
