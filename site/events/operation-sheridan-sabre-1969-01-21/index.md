## January 21, 1969 — D Company loses one man (attached)

> **Built from a single firsthand source.** CPT Henry Colavita's memoir is, so far, the only
> account of this loss anywhere in this archive. Cause of death was disputed even at the time and
> was never resolved — this page records what is known rather than asserting a conclusion.

On the night of January 20–21, 1969, while **D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** held the
perimeter at **[FSB Rita](/locations/fsb-rita/)**, **SP4 Elliot Velez-Rodriguez** was killed in an
explosion inside the base.

### A one-night assignment

Velez-Rodriguez was a newly arrived replacement bound for **A Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th
Cavalry** — not organic to Delta Company. He had arrived on Rita while Alpha Company was out in
the field and Delta was holding the base's perimeter defense, and was waiting for the next
resupply flight to carry him out to his own unit. Per Colavita's account, a Wild Cat (2nd Platoon)
squad leader, **Sergeant Terry Shoopman**, was short-handed for the night's perimeter watch and
added Velez-Rodriguez to the line rather than leave him unassigned.

### What happened

A single explosion in the middle of the firebase that night sympathetically set off stacked mortar
rounds stored in a nearby ammunition pit. No ground attack followed. A headcount the next morning
confirmed all of Delta Company's own troopers present, but a further check of the perimeter turned
up Velez-Rodriguez's remains near the ammunition storage area — he had not survived the blast.

The precise cause was never established, and Colavita's account is candid that it split opinion
among the men at the time. Some believed the explosion was accidental, or possibly
self-inflicted. Others believed the enemy had thrown or launched a satchel charge onto the LZ from
outside the wire, using a bent-over tree as a kind of sling — a theory that gained some support
when patrols later found signs of enemy activity nearby. The Army's official classification for
his cause of death is recorded as "Artillery, rocket, or mortar." This page does not take a
position between these accounts; no source since has resolved it.

### "He absolutely does count"

Because Velez-Rodriguez was administratively still assigned to Alpha Company, Colavita writes that
he briefly reasoned the loss shouldn't be counted against Delta's own toll — before rejecting that
thought outright:

> "He absolutely does count as a soldier who died on my watch and is included on the Honor Roll
> with the other KIA members of Delta Company, 2/8 Cavalry."

This mirrors the same reasoning this archive already applies to **[SP4 Raymond
Ahern](/soldiers/ahern-raymond/)** — a man who served and died with D Company despite an
administrative attachment elsewhere. He is remembered on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, **Panel
34W, Line 54**.

### One week before a harder-fought loss

D Company held Rita for another week after this — see **[the January 28, 1969
cluster](/events/operation-sheridan-sabre-1969-01-28/)**, in which SP4 Carl Pipher and CPL Warren
Eskridge were killed during a search-and-destroy patrol away from the base.
