## Operation Pershing — D Company's year in Binh Dinh

> **Context page — operation level (umbrella).** Built from the standard published histories of
> Pershing, with the 2/8 Cavalry firebase records confirming the battalion's continuous presence in
> the area of operations. It frames **D Company's entire 1967 in Binh Dinh Province** — the year that
> produced the largest run of losses in the company's record. The operation's shape is well
> established; the company's day-by-day actions, and the specific contacts behind most of the 1967
> casualties, await the battalion staff journal and are documented on their own event pages as they
> are built.

### What it was

Operation Pershing (12 February 1967 – 19 January 1968) was the **1st Cavalry Division's dominant
operation of 1967** — a year-long search-and-destroy and pacification campaign across **Binh Dinh
Province** on the central coast. It began the day **Operation Thayer II ended**, continuing the same
fight on the same ground: the **Bong Son Plain** — a rich agricultural flatland of nearly 100,000
people, hemmed by mountains on three sides and the South China Sea on the fourth — and the valleys
behind it, the **An Lao** and **Kim Son ("Crow's Foot")**, later extending north into the **Song Re
Valley** and lower Quang Ngai Province.

The division's object, under MG John Norton (and later MG John J. Tolson), was to **trap and destroy
the PAVN 3rd ("Sao Vang") Division** and dismantle the Viet Cong infrastructure in the province,
working alongside the **3rd Brigade, 25th Infantry Division**, the **ARVN 22nd Division**, and the
**ROK Capital ("Tiger") Division**. Over the full operation the division reported roughly **5,400
enemy killed and more than 2,000 captured**, at a cost of **852 U.S. dead**; by year's end over
eighty percent of Binh Dinh's population was assessed as free of insurgent control.

### Where 2/8 Cavalry — and D Company — fit

2/8 Cavalry fought Pershing as a **1st Brigade** battalion. The battalion's firebase records place
its companies at **dozens of landing zones across the Pershing area of operations through 1967** —
the Bong Son Plain, the An Lao and Kim Son valleys, the **Song Re Valley** in August (where A Company
took a hot LZ at Hill 450 / LZ Pat on 9 August: eleven killed, twenty-seven wounded, three aircraft
down), and the **Tam Quan** area in December. D Company appears repeatedly in that record, from the
Kim Son LZs of the winter to the Tam Quan landing zones of December.

That continuous presence is why the year matters here: after the **28 January 1967 Kim Son cluster**
(Keller and Yates) that closed out Thayer II, **nearly every D Company loss of 1967 falls inside
Pershing.** The company's 1967 dead — eighteen organic and two attached — run in clusters and singles
from March through December:

- **1 March** — SGT William Burton Jr. and CPL Robert E. Johnson
- **12 March** — PFC Robert Van Gieson
- **18 March** — PFC Michael N. Johnson and SSG Ralph Woodall Jr. (with SSG John Willis and SP4 Clayton Middleton, attached)
- **2 April** — PFC Vance Williams
- **23 May** — SP4 Robert Bohmer
- **30 May** — PFC Daniel Nelson and PFC George Sutt
- **31 May** — PFC Charles Krueger
- **1 June** — CPL Lonney Ehlers · **5 June** — SP4 Terry McComb · **13 June** — PFC Clyde Houser Jr.
- **26 September** — PFC Michael Santoroski
- **11 December** — SP4 Allan Follett and SP4 John Paulson Jr. (in the **Battle of Tam Quan**)

The best-documented of these is the **December cluster**, which falls inside the **[Battle of Tam
Quan](/events/battle-of-tam-quan-1967/)** (6–20 December 1967) — the 1st Brigade's large fight
against the PAVN 22d Regiment near Bong Son, and the one 1967 engagement with a full operational
record. The spring and early-summer clusters are not yet matched to specific actions; those Tier 1
event pages are queued, and will wire to this frame as they are built.

### What's still open

Pershing's shape is well documented at division and brigade level, but the **specific contacts behind
most of D Company's 1967 losses are not** — the firebase data gives the landing zones, but the
day-of maneuver and the enemy units await the **2/8 Cav daily staff journal (NARA RG 472)**. Building
the remaining Tier 1 cluster pages (and the soldier profiles behind them) is the next step; this page
is the frame they attach to.
