## March 15, 1968 — LZ Betty, Quang Tri Province

> **Research stub.** The casualty record and unit history confirm date, mechanism, and
> command succession. The firebase name is disputed between two sources. This page holds
> what is known and marks the gap explicitly.

**LTC Howard Palma Petty**, commanding officer of the 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry, was killed in action on the night of **March 15, 1968**, at **LZ Betty**, Quang Tri Province. He had commanded the battalion for 33 days. He was 37 years old. He is inscribed on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, **Panel 44E, Line 61**.

### What is known

A mortar and rocket attack struck LZ Betty during the night of March 15. A mortar round landed on the tent occupied by LTC Petty and his Battalion Operations Officer, **Major Barney Darby**. Petty was killed immediately; Darby was evacuated with serious wounds. **LTC John V. Gibney** assumed command of the battalion the following day, March 16.

L.J. Adams, who served as Petty's S-3 NCO, later wrote that a church tower spotter directed the mortar team, and that the team was killed in the follow-up action. This detail is not corroborated by other available sources but is consistent with VC tactics in the Quang Tri area.

LTC Petty had assumed command in early February 1968, succeeding **LTC John Stannard**, who was promoted to Colonel and took command of the 1st Brigade. He was a Regular Army officer with 18 years of service at the time of his death.

### The location dispute

The Eager Arms Bravo Company history — a unit-level narrative — places the battalion at **LZ Betty**, described as "a newly established base … to the west of Quang Tri." Adams, in his Wall of Faces remembrance, names **LZ Sharon**. The most likely explanation is that Adams' header ("Co A 2/8th Cav 1st Cav LZ Sharon") describes his own company's home position, not the battalion TOC where Petty was killed. LZ Betty is the preferred location; the NARA daily staff journal would settle the question.

### Next source needed

The **2/8 Cav daily staff journal** (NARA RG 472, 15 March 1968) is the primary outstanding source. It should confirm the firebase, the nature of the attack, and whether any other casualties were recorded that night.
