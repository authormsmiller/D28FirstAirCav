## 1965 — The First Team arrives

> **Context page — division level.** The opening of D Company, 2/8 Cav's war: how the unit got to
> Vietnam. Drawn from the official Army record (CMH *Airmobility*, the division's Interim Report,
> and the 2/8 Cav Lineage and Honors). The exact DA General Order numbers are flagged to pull.

On **1 July 1965**, at Fort Benning, Georgia, the Army activated the **1st Cavalry Division
(Airmobile)** — the "First Team." It was built from the assets of the **11th Air Assault Division
(Test)**, which had spent two years proving the airmobile concept, and elements of the **2nd
Infantry Division**; the 1st Cavalry Division colors were brought over from Korea to carry the new
formation. With roughly **428 organic helicopters**, it was the Army's **first airmobile
division** — a division that would ride to battle by helicopter.

### Into Vietnam

Reorganized and equipped in a matter of weeks, the division shipped to the Republic of Vietnam by
sea that summer. Its **2nd Brigade landed at Qui Nhon on 14 September 1965**, and the division
established its base at **An Khe (Camp Radcliff)** in the Central Highlands. The 1st Cavalry
Division was the **first fully committed U.S. division of the Vietnam War**, and within weeks it
was in combat — the **Pleiku Campaign** of October–November 1965, for which it became the **first
division of the war to earn the Presidential Unit Citation**.

### Where 2/8 Cav — and D Company — fit

The **8th Cavalry** had been a regiment of the 1st Cavalry Division since **1921**; the **2nd
Battalion, 8th Cavalry** in its modern form was assigned to the division on **1 September 1963**.
It deployed as an organic battalion and was in country by **September 1965**. Its first major
action came almost immediately: the pursuit phase of the Pleiku Campaign (see
**Operation All the Way**), and D Company's first recorded losses near **Hill 732 on 4 November
1965** — SGT Coffey, PFC Hamill, and CPL Hill, among the division's earliest combat deaths.

This is where the company's war begins. It would end nearly seven years later with the same
battalion still in the field — see **Departure — The Last Team Out (1972)**.
