## Operation Overlord — the operation frame

> **Operation-level page.** This establishes what **2/8 Cav** (the US battalion in Overlord) and the wider task force were doing in June 1971 — drawn from a 1 ATF operation account, McGrew's calendar, Garvin's photos, and the firebase gazetteer. It is **not** a day-by-day record of D Company's actions, which await the 2/8 Cav journal (NARA RG 472).

**Operation Overlord** was a brigade-size "search and clear" operation launched by the **1st Australian Task Force (1 ATF)** in **Long Khánh Province** in June 1971. The task force commander named it after the 1944 Normandy landings, intending it to open on the same date 27 years later — and the operation's three areas of operation were even named for the invasion beachheads, **Gold, Juno, and Omaha** (the US sector lay in **AO Gold**). Intelligence had located the **3rd Battalion, 33rd NVA Regiment (3/33)** and the **D445 VC Battalion** in fortified bunker complexes **east of Route 2**, along the Phuoc Tuy / Long Khánh border roughly **30 km north of Nui Dat**. The plan was a classic **hammer and anvil**: blocking forces would seal the flanks while 3 RAR, supported by Centurion tanks, drove the enemy onto them from the northwest.

Deployment began before dawn on **5 June 1971**. The main fighting — the **Battle of Long Khánh, 6–7 June** — began when 3 RAR's lead platoon was pinned at point-blank range on the edge of a bunker system built to survive B-52 strikes. Centurion tanks of C Squadron, 1st Armoured Regiment ground forward to break the enemy hold, crushing and grenading bunkers in close support; at the decisive moment a **US Army Cobra gunship**, called in over the radio, laid rockets and machine-gun fire just **50 metres ahead of the tanks**. Unable to mass enough combat power to trap the defenders, the task force saw the bulk of the VC/PAVN force withdraw intact, probably with heavy casualties. On **12 June**, near the end of the operation, the **274 Regional Force VC Regiment** ambushed a 1 ATF headquarters element on armoured personnel carriers southwest of the area — an RPG detonated a load of Claymore mines and killed seven, the worst single-incident Australian loss of the war. **Ten Australians were killed** across Overlord, which ended on **14 June** with 1 ATF's return to Nui Dat. Australian accounts record it as **the last joint US–Australian battalion-size operation of the war**.

## Where 2/8 Cav fit

The **US battalion** in Overlord was **2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** (3rd Brigade, 1st Cav Div) — the parent unit of D Company "Angry Skipper," the focus of this archive. According to the 1 ATF account:

> "2/8 Battalion 3 Brigade (US) 1 Cavalry Division (Air Mobile), deployed in blocking positions along the **Suoi Luc** with three companies, with **Headquarters 2/8 Battalion remaining at FSB Fanning** and a new US artillery **FSB Hall** established to support the operation."

So 2/8 Cav formed part of the **anvil**: headquarters at **[FSB Fanning](/locations/fsb-fanning/)** — the battalion's primary firebase, as the Stars and Stripes clipping from Jean "Dizzy" Blais also calls it — three companies blocking along the Suoi Luc, and fresh artillery at the newly built **FSB Hall**.

The Australian task-force commander, **Col Peter Scott — then CO of 3 RAR** — later laid out the full order of battle, and **2/8 Cavalry stands in it as one of three infantry battalions**, alongside **3 RAR** and **4 RAR/NZ**, all under **Brigadier Bruce McDonald MC**: more than **2,500 Australian and American soldiers and airmen** deploying at once, out of the Task Force Maintenance Area at Nui Dat. The American contribution went beyond the riflemen on the Suoi Luc. Scott's support list names a single US **155mm** battery — **Battery C, 5th Battalion, 42nd Artillery** — almost certainly the guns of the newly built **FSB Hall**, and a match for the 155mm pieces D Company had photographed at **[FSB Judy](/locations/fsb-judy/)** on 4 June. And the **Cobra gunships** that turned the Battle of Long Khánh flew from a **US Army Assault Helicopter Company**. Wounded from the day were lifted out to the Australian Field Hospital at **Vung Tau** — the same Vung Tau that turns up on McGrew's calendar on 21 June.

One note for memory's sake: in Scott's account the "**D Company**" that swept the bunkers with the tanks is **3 RAR's** D Company — **not** D Co, 2/8 Cav. The American D Company's place in Overlord was the blocking line and the firebases, not the Long Khánh assault itself.

## The local sources line up

Howard McGrew's personal field calendar tracks the same days from inside the unit, and matches the operation account closely:

- **5 June — "build FSB Hall"** (Overlord D-Day, date-for-date).
- **7 June — Fanning** (the battalion's base during the operation).
- **9, 13, 16 June — combat assaults** to mission areas / new locations.
- **21 June — CA to Fanning, then Vung Tau.**

The day **before** D-Day, **4 June**, D Company had combat-assaulted to **[FSB Judy](/locations/fsb-judy/)** — a **155mm** heavy-artillery base (Garvin's photos and McGrew's "CA to FB Julie" both date it 4 Jun). The timing and the heavy guns suggest Judy was **artillery pre-positioning for Overlord**, occupied on the operation's eve.

After Overlord closed on 14 June, the company's June tempo continued: the **21 June** move staged back through Fanning, and on **24 June** D Co fought the **[Firefight on the Mountain](/events/firefight-mountain-1971-06-24/)** and the **[Bunker Complex Sweep](/events/bunker-complex-1971-06-24/)** southeast of FSB Fontaine — separate actions reached through the same June tempo, not part of Overlord itself.
