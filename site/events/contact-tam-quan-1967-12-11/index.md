## December 11, 1967 — a night ambush south of Tam Quan

> **Confirmed action, sourced to the after-action report.** Two men of D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th
> Cavalry were killed on 11 December 1967 during the Battle of Tam Quan. The 1st Brigade's Combat
> Operations After Action Report records the D Company action of that night — a 22:15 ambush of PAVN
> troops escaping south — and both men's casualty records match it. What is *not* in the AAR is the
> man-by-man detail: which of the two fell to small arms, which to the explosive device, and whether
> others were wounded.

By the second week of December 1967, the **Battle of Tam Quan** had turned into a pursuit. After the
1st Brigade broke the PAVN **22nd Regiment's** headquarters position at Dai Dong on 6–7 December, the
regimental commander slipped his command south toward the Cay Giep Mountains, leaving his battalions
to fight and exfiltrate as best they could. The U.S. and ARVN forces spent the following days trying
to seal the escape routes south across the Bong Son Plain.

**D Company, 2/8 Cavalry** had air-assaulted to **BS 925996** on **10 December** to block the enemy's
escape routes into the **Cay Giep Mountains**. The next night it set ambushes at **BS 926048**. The
1st Brigade Combat Operations After Action Report records what followed: **"Ambush elements of D 2-8
Cav located at BS 926048 had contact with an unknown sized enemy force at 2215 hours and 2245 hours.
The enemy was moving from north to south attempting to escape when contact was made with the ambush
elements."** It was in that night action that the company lost two men:

- **SP4 Allan Eugene Follett**, 23, of Independence, Missouri — killed by **small-arms fire** — Wall Panel 31E, Line 79
- **SP4 John Paul Paulson Jr.**, 20, of Neenah, Wisconsin — killed by an **explosive device** — Wall Panel 31E, Line 82

Their names sit three lines apart on the Wall, and they had arrived in Vietnam a day apart in May
1967. The two different causes — small arms and an explosive device — fit the shape of a close night
ambush (rifle and machine-gun fire alongside a mine, grenade, or RPG) rather than a single shared
blast. See their profiles: [Allan Follett](/soldiers/follett-allan/) and
[John Paulson](/soldiers/paulson-john/).

### The unit and its commanders

2/8 Cavalry fought Tam Quan as a **1st Brigade** battalion under **LTC John Stannard**, who days
earlier, on 7 December, had personally taken command of the battalion's C Company when its commander
was killed crossing a rice paddy. The brigade commander, **Col. Donald V. Rattan**, had placed
**LTC Christian F. Dubia** (CO of the 1/8 Cavalry) in tactical charge of the battle; the division was
commanded by **MG John J. Tolson**. D Company would fight on through the battle — holding blocking
positions on 15 December and making the air assault that opened the final fight at **An Nghiệp** on
19 December — before the operation closed on 20 December.

### What's still open

The after-action report fixes the ambush in place and time — BS 926048, contacts at 2215 and 2245 —
but it does not name the men or break out friendly casualties. So **which of the two fell at which
contact, and whether other D Company soldiers were wounded**, still await the **2/8 Cav daily staff
journal (NARA RG 472)**. Two further threads remain: confirming **LTC John Stannard** as the 2/8 Cav
commander against the AAR distribution list (the name here comes from a 2/8 Cav veterans' account —
the AAR itself is signed by the brigade commander, Col. Rattan), and verifying a possible **Silver
Star for SP4 Paulson** (listed by Honor States, no citation located).
