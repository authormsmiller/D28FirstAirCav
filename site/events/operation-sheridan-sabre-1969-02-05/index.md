## February 5, 1969 — D Company loses two men

> **Research page — updated 2026-07-22.** CPT Henry Colavita's memoir has
> upgraded this page from a casualty-record-only research stub to a firsthand
> narrated account. Confirmed: a single creek-crossing engagement, later
> nicknamed "Colavita Creek" by the company, killed both men on February 5,
> 1969. The exact grid and enemy unit are still not confirmed against a
> unit-level record.

On February 5, 1969, **D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** lost two men at
a creek crossing in its area of operations near FSB/LZ Saint Barbara:

- **SP4 Chester Jon Kmit**, 23, of Williamsburg, Massachusetts — Wall Panel
  33W, Line 65
- **CPL James Thomas Edmonds**, 20, of Burlington, North Carolina — Wall Panel
  33W, Line 62

Both names sit on **Panel 33W** — the same panel that carries Pipher and
Eskridge from one week earlier. **[Chester Kmit](/soldiers/kmit-chester/)**
had arrived in May 1968 and was nearing the end of his tour.
**[James Edmonds](/soldiers/edmonds-james/)** had been in country since
October 1968.

### Operation Sheridan Sabre and FSB Saint Barbara

By early February 1969, D Company appears to have shifted from the 2nd
Brigade's Fishhook AO (FSB Rita, through late January) to the **1st
Brigade's** Tay Ninh Province / War Zone C AO, operating off **[FSB Saint
Barbara](/locations/fsb-st-barbara/)** — a former French fort the 25th
Infantry Division had used earlier in the war, part of a string of firebases
along the Cambodian border kept within mutual 105mm howitzer range of each
other to counter NVA supply caching. Colavita's memoir places the company at
Saint Barbara through February and most of March 1969, and the base's own
grid (XT275684) is now confirmed via the firebase gazetteer. **Operation
Sheridan Sabre** (7 November 1968 – 4 April 1969) remains the operational
frame for this window; see "An unresolved brigade question, now resolved"
below for why this page now treats Tay Ninh Province as correct rather than
disputed.

### The commander's account

CPT Colavita's memoir describes a smaller contact the night before — a single
NVA encountered on February 4 — followed the next day by a downhill approach
to a creek crossing below that position. As the company crossed, it took
intense fire from a broad front on the far bank. Kmit, walking point, was
killed instantly. Several others were wounded. Colavita called battalion
("Stone Mountain 6" — LTC Richard Wood, who had just taken command from LTC
Frank Henry) for gunship support, and the company pulled back to higher
ground under covering fire while the two most seriously wounded were staged
for urgent medevac.

### Edmonds's evacuation

The landing zone remained too hot for a helicopter to land, so a jungle-
penetrator lift was arranged to pull Edmonds out by cable. During the lift,
continued enemy fire led the medevac crew to cut the cable, and Edmonds fell.
A Medical Service Corps colonel formally investigated the incident afterward;
Colavita testified that the cut had not been necessary and has said he
believes Edmonds may already have died of his wounds before the fall. A
second medevac later lifted out the other wounded once the situation allowed.

### Kmit's recovery

Kmit's body could not be recovered immediately — first because of the
ongoing situation, then because of a stay-in-place order tied to the
cable-cutting investigation. He was recovered two nights later, once a secure
landing zone could be established.

### An unresolved brigade question, now resolved

This page previously flagged an unresolved tension: a single Wall of Faces
remembrance for Edmonds (posted by Dennis Wriston) states he served under the
"2nd Brigade," while Tay Ninh Province — the province of record for both
men — was documented as the 1st Brigade's AO during Sheridan Sabre. Colavita's
account of D Company operating off Saint Barbara, combined with the existing
Operation Montana Scout page (which places Saint Barbara among the 1st
Brigade's firebases in the months immediately following), makes it very
likely that 2/8 Cav had already shifted to the 1st Brigade's Tay Ninh AO by
this date. On that reading, "Tay Ninh Province" is simply correct here — the
opposite pattern from the Dec 4, Jan 21, and Jan 28 clusters, where Tay Ninh
was very likely a casualty-record error for the 2nd Brigade's actual Binh
Long AO. The Wriston remembrance is most likely an outdated holdover from 2/8
Cav's earlier Rita-era assignment. This is an inference from strong
circumstantial fit, not a confirmation from a task-organization table or unit
journal — neither has been located for this specific date.
