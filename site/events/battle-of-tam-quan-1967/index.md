## The Battle of Tam Quan — December 1967

> **Context page — operation level.** Built from the published accounts of the Battle of Tam Quan and
> the 2/8 Cavalry firebase records, which place the battalion — A, B, C, and D Companies — in the
> fight. It frames the engagement in which **D Company lost SP4 Allan Follett and SP4 John Paulson on
> 11 December 1967.** The battle's shape is well documented; D Company's exact 11 December action
> awaits the battalion staff journal.

### What it was

The Battle of Tam Quan (6–20 December 1967) was the **largest named engagement of Operation Pershing**
for the 1st Cavalry Division's 1st Brigade — a two-week fight against the **PAVN 22d Regiment** of the
3rd ("Sao Vang") Division near **Tam Quan** and the village of **Dai Dong** on the northern Bong Son
Plain. In late November, U.S. intelligence detected the 22d Regiment preparing to operate in the Bong
Son area; on **6 December** the 1st Brigade located its headquarters near Dai Dong, and aero-scouts of
**A Troop, 1/9 Cavalry** drew fire and were pinned down. The **1/8 Cavalry** was ordered in that
evening and linked up with an armored cavalry platoon of the **1/50 Infantry** that had pushed up from
**LZ English**.

By **09:15 on 7 December**, after the contact site was worked over with aerial rocket and tube
artillery, **Company A, 1/8 Cavalry and Companies B and C, 2/8 Cavalry** had air-assaulted in and,
with the **ARVN 40th Regiment**, sealed a perimeter around the area. On **10 December** the PAVN
**8th Battalion, 22d Regiment** attacked the ARVN positions and was thrown back by gunships and
artillery. The encircled regiment, pinned between the allied cordon and the sea, was badly mauled
before search-and-destroy operations closed the battle out on **20 December** — one of the sharpest
defeats dealt to the 3rd Division during Pershing.

The division's own airmobility history (written by its commander, Gen. Tolson) holds up Tam Quan as
the model of airmobile **"piling on"**: the fortuitous discovery of an enemy radio antenna, a small
force inserted within hours, and then battalion after battalion — plus the 1/50 Mechanized Infantry
and the 40th ARVN Regiment — flown in to fix and surround the enemy before he could slip away. Tolson
judged that the battle, by shattering the 22d Regiment in early December, **pre-empted the enemy's Tet
offensive** in northern Binh Dinh.

### Where 2/8 Cavalry — and D Company — fit

2/8 Cavalry was in the fight from the start, under battalion commander **LTC John Stannard** (the 1st
Brigade was led by **Col. Donald V. Rattan**, who put **LTC Christian F. Dubia** of the 1/8 Cavalry in
tactical charge of the battle). **B Company air-assaulted to LZ Dai Dong and C Company to LZ Thien
Chanh on 7 December** — and that afternoon C Company was pinned down crossing a rice paddy and its
commander killed, until Stannard personally took command and got them out. The battalion's **A and D
Companies entered the battle on 10 December**; A and D held blocking positions on 15 December and
joined the pursuit on 16 December, and on **19 December D Company made the air assault that opened the
battle's final fight at An Nghiệp**.

It was in that span, on the night of **11 December 1967**, that D Company lost two men. The 1st
Brigade's after-action report records that **"at 22:15, Company D 2/8th Cavalry ambushed several PAVN
units as they attempted to escape the area to the South"** — and the two D Company dead of that date
fit the action:

- **SP4 Allan Eugene Follett**, of Independence, Missouri — killed by small-arms fire — Wall Panel 31E, Line 79
- **SP4 John Paul Paulson Jr.**, of Neenah, Wisconsin — killed by an explosive device — Wall Panel 31E, Line 82

Their Wall lines sit three apart, and the two causes — small arms and an explosive device — fit a
close night ambush. That action is documented on its own page:
[D Company's night ambush, 11 December 1967](/events/contact-tam-quan-1967-12-11/). Both men now have
profiles: [Allan Follett](/soldiers/follett-allan/) and [John Paulson](/soldiers/paulson-john/).

### What's still open

The battle is well documented at brigade and battalion level, and the after-action report names the
D Company 11 December ambush — but it does not break out casualties, so **which man fell to which
cause, the exact ambush grid, and any wounded** are still open. The man-level record awaits the
**2/8 Cav daily staff journal (NARA RG 472)**. Two threads also remain: confirming **LTC John
Stannard** as the 2/8 Cav commander against the AAR distribution list, and verifying a possible
**Silver Star for SP4 Paulson** (listed by Honor States, no citation located).
