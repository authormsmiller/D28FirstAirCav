## Operation Bolling — D Company's context

> **Context page — operation level.** Primary sources: 1st Cavalry Division ORLL (AD0387543,
> period ending 31 Oct 1967) and 173rd Airborne Brigade ORLL (AD0394510, period ending 31 Jul
> 1968). B Company, 2/8 Cavalry narrative (eagerarms.com) provides the unit-level account.
> D Company's specific actions during Bolling are not yet documented.

### What it was

In mid-September 1967, after returning from the Song Re Valley operation, 2nd Battalion, 8th
Cavalry received orders to redeploy south. IFFORCEV had directed the **173rd Airborne Brigade**
to counter an NVA build-up west of **Tuy Hoa** — the coastal city in **Phu Yen Province** that
was home to the **31st Tactical Fighter Wing** of the U.S. Air Force. Those aircraft provided
air support across the northern provinces, and an NVA build-up in the hills to the west
threatened to disrupt those operations. The 173rd was assigned **two battalions** for the
mission: its own **4th Battalion, 503d Infantry**, and 2/8 Cav reinforcing from the 1st Cav Div.

On **September 17–18**, the battalion moved from **LZ English** — its home base on the Bong Son
Plain — to a staging area at a **Special Forces camp west of Qui Nhon**. The 1st Cavalry
Division officially released **Task Force 2/8 Cav OPCON to the 173rd Airborne Brigade**
effective **17 September 1967**. On **September 19**, companies were combat-assaulted into
landing zones in the rolling hills west of Tuy Hoa.

### What happened

The operation produced only scattered contacts. The anticipated major engagement did not
materialize — the **95th NVA Regiment** (the primary enemy force in Phu Yen Province, operating
under the 5th NVA Division) avoided a pitched battle, and B Company veterans noted the enemy
"seemed to fade away." The most notable single incident was the capture of an **NVA Finance
Officer** who had become separated from his unit and wandered into a U.S. position.

The 1st Cavalry Division ORLL records the results for the entire Bolling operation — 26 days of
patrolling west of Tuy Hoa — as: **Enemy KIA 21, 12 prisoners of war, 8 small arms weapons
captured; Friendly KIA 0, WIA 7.** The zero-KIA figure for 2/8 Cav is confirmed: no D Company
soldier died during the Bolling window. On **October 14**, the battalion returned to LZ English
and resumed Pershing operations on the Bong Son Plain.

### Where 2/8 Cavalry — and D Company — fit

The battalion went to Tuy Hoa as a unit; all companies, including D Company, were part of the
deployment. Specific landing zone names and grid coordinates for 2/8 Cav's positions in AO
Bolling are not recorded at the division ORLL level — they would require the 2/8 Cav battalion
staff journal (NARA RG 472). No D Company KIA falls within the Bolling window; the division
ORLL's "Friendly KIA 0" for the entire operation closes that question definitively.

### Operation Bolling II

After 2/8 Cav returned to the Pershing AO in October 1967, the **4th Battalion, 503d Infantry**
remained in Phu Yen Province and continued operations against the 95th NVA Regiment under the
name **Operation Bolling II** — a designation the 173rd Airborne Brigade carried forward into
1968. The 173d ORLL for May–July 1968 records the same AO (west of Tuy Hoa), the same enemy
unit (95th NVA Regiment), and the same mission. The regiment had withdrawn to the jungle west
of Tuy Hoa after taking heavy casualties from ROK and ARVN forces in April 1968, and was
reorganizing through mid-1968. The 2/8 Cav's brief attachment was the opening act of a
sustained 173rd campaign that ran more than a year.

### The fall 1967 frame

Bolling fills the central gap in 2/8 Cavalry's fall 1967 operational record. After Song Re
closed on August 19, the battalion returned to the Pershing AO briefly before Bolling ran
September 19 through October 14. Following the return to LZ English, the battalion conducted
Bong Son operations through October, then deployed to **Dak To** in November — where B Company
fought a major engagement at Hill 1034 on November 16–20. The battalion then moved into the
**Battle of Tam Quan** in December, the largest named engagement of its 1967 campaign.
