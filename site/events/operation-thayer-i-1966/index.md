## Operation Thayer I — the Kim Son Valley search-and-destroy

> **Context page — operation level.** Built from the 1st Cavalry Division's own Operational Report —
> Lessons Learned for the quarter ending 22 November 1966, which narrates Thayer I at the division
> level, with a corroborating Staff Historian entry from the following quarter's report. It frames
> the operation that **D Company, 2/8 Cavalry fought through in September 1966** — the operation in
> which **2LT Michael Derosier was killed near Bong Son on 19 September** and LTC Tackaberry earned
> his second Distinguished Service Cross. Division- and brigade-level movements are well documented;
> D Company's hour-by-hour positions await the battalion staff journal.

### What it was

Operation Thayer I (13 September – 1 October 1966) was a 1st Cavalry Division **search-and-destroy
operation in Binh Dinh Province**, launched in response to an **enemy build-up in the Kim Son
("Crow's Foot") Valley** (vicinity grid BR1480). It was the first phase of a long sequence that ran
through that autumn and winter: **Thayer I → Irving (2–24 Oct) → Thayer II (24 Oct – 12 Feb 1967)**,
all on the same Binh Dinh ground.

The division's scheme, in its own words, was to **attack the enemy bases and facilities with two
brigades while squeezing his forces eastward toward the seacoast**. In the initial assault, **five
battalions were air-assaulted onto high-ground landing zones around the Kim Son**, then worked the
ridge lines and fingers down to the valley floors. **Contact was sporadic, but the division met
intense anti-aircraft fire throughout the area of operations.** On **20 September three battalions
were air-assaulted into the valley east of the Kim Son** (vic BR8580) to exploit reports of an enemy
build-up there. On **23 September** the enemy struck back at the division's **HAMMOND logistics base**
with mortars, recoilless rifles, and small arms, causing light damage and casualties. When Thayer I
terminated on **1 October**, its logistics elements were already positioned to roll straight into
**Operation Irving** the next day.

### Where 2/8 Cavalry — and D Company — fit

2/8 Cavalry fought Thayer I as a **1st Brigade** battalion under **LTC Thomas H. Tackaberry**, who
had taken command on 30 July 1966. The single best-documented D Company moment of the operation is
**19 September 1966**, when a **fifteen-man D Company patrol** — 2LT Michael Derosier's platoon —
was caught by a **reinforced North Vietnamese company** and pinned down. Derosier, the platoon
leader, was killed; Tackaberry landed his unarmed command helicopter beside the action, ran through
the fire, took personal command, and led the assault that broke the encirclement — the action of his
second Distinguished Service Cross. That day is documented in full on the
[19 September 1966 contact page](/events/contact-bong-son-1966-09-19/).

A note on location: Tackaberry's DSC citation places the action **"near Bong Son,"** while the
division Staff Historian's record — prepared with retired BG **S.L.A. Marshall**, who documented
1st Cavalry actions in this period — logs the **2/8 Cavalry action of 19 September 1966 in the
"506 Valley" under Thayer I**. Both lie in the Bong Son / Kim Son area of Binh Dinh; the archive
keeps both labels and flags the discrepancy rather than forcing a single grid.

### What's still open

The division ORLL is written at division and brigade level, so D Company's specific daily positions
within Thayer I — and the exact grid of the 19 September action — are not in it. Those await the
**2/8 Cav daily staff journal (NARA RG 472)** and the Thayer I after-action report.
