## January 25, 1970 — PFC Kenneth Frierson

> **Contact page.** Primary sources: Virtual Wall and VVMF casualty records; firsthand
> account from Lt. Glen Beasley (Cat 6, D Company, 2/8 Cav, 1969–70), who was with
> Frierson when he was killed. The specific circumstances — position, enemy unit, and the
> nature of Frierson's actions — are not fully established in available sources.

On **January 25, 1970**, **PFC Kenneth Frierson** was killed by small arms fire in **Hau
Nghia Province**. He was 20 years old, a rifleman with D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th
Cavalry, and he had been in Vietnam for 45 days. He is inscribed on the Vietnam Veterans
Memorial, **Panel 14W, Line 64**.

### "Your actions saved us at your own sacrifice"

Fifty-three years after the fact, on December 31, 2022, **Lt. Glen Beasley** — who
commanded the D Company element with Frierson that day — left a remembrance on the Vietnam
Veterans Memorial Fund's Wall of Faces:

> *"Ken, 53 years ago this month. Your actions saved us at your own sacrifice. God bless you."*

Beasley signed it *Lt*. He was Cat 6 — the element commander in the field on January 25,
1970. His words are the only firsthand account of what happened that day, and they are
unambiguous: Frierson did something that protected the people around him. He died doing it.

The casualty record codes his death as hostile, **died outright** — gun or small arms fire,
ground casualty. A direct-fire engagement. Not a mine, not a mortar. Frierson was in a
firefight, and Beasley, who survived it, has said for the record that it was Frierson's
actions that made that possible.

### Where the battalion was

In January 1970, the 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry was operating in the **western War Zone C
corridor**, conducting the 1st Brigade's interdiction mission along the Cambodian border
under LTC Michael Conrad. The battalion's last confirmed fire support base before this
date is **FB Westphal** (YT380835), which was active January 14–17, 1970 — eight days
before Frierson was killed. By January 25 the battalion had moved, but to where is not
established in reviewed sources. The casualty record gives **Hau Nghia Province**, which
overlaps the southern portion of the XT grid corridor where 2/8 Cav operated.

The ORLL that would cover January 25 — the 1st Cavalry Division's operational report for
the period ending January 31, 1970 — has not yet been reviewed. That document would fix
the battalion's position and activity on this date.

### Kenneth Frierson — from Alcolu, South Carolina

Kenneth Frierson was from **Alcolu**, a small community in Clarendon County, South
Carolina. He was born November 8, 1949. He arrived in Vietnam on **December 11, 1969**,
assigned to D Company as a light-weapons infantryman (MOS 11B10). He was 20 years old
and had been in-country **45 days** when he was killed. He was brought home and buried at
**Union Cypress Cemetery, Manning, South Carolina**.

He is the first D Company soldier to die in 1970. The company would lose five more men
before the end of March — all in the same border corridor, all in the months before the
Cambodian Incursion that the division was preparing.

In his community, his memory has been kept. **George Frierson**, a Vietnam veteran and
local historian in Alcolu, has honored him publicly — including before the Clarendon County
Council. Lt. Beasley's remembrance, posted more than five decades after the fact, is the
other half of that keeping.
