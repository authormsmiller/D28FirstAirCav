## April 22, 1966 — D Company loses a man at An Khe

> **Research page — sparse.** Confirmed: SP4 Terry Allen Rippy, D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th
> Cavalry, was killed by an explosive device on April 22, 1966. The official record classifies
> the death as hostile, died outright. He was 23 years old and had served 144 days. The precise
> location and circumstances are not yet confirmed from primary sources.

On **April 22, 1966**, **SP4 Terry Allen Rippy** — D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry — was
killed by an **explosive device** in the days following the battalion's return from the western
highlands. He was 23 years old. His name is on the Wall at **Panel 6E, Line 128**.

### Where the battalion had just been

The month before Rippy's death had taken D Company across the length of the II Corps highlands.
**Operation Lincoln** (25 March–8 April) was a search-and-destroy in Pleiku Province; 2/8 Cavalry
worked forward LZs across the Chu Pong area — **LZ Typhus** (YA855198), **LZ Boil** (YA950255),
**LZ Pox** (YA975291), and **LZ Wool** (YV856962, the battalion CP during the final phase). D
Company, with the battalion CP, trucked to Oasis from LZ Pox on March 31 as the brigade repositioned.

**Operation Mosby I** followed immediately: a two-brigade reconnaissance-in-force into **Pleiku and
Kontum Provinces along the Cambodian border**, running 11–17 April. 2/8 Cavalry assaulted into
**LZ Amy** (YA693474) and **LZ Kim** (YA663478) on April 11, the operation's first day. The
reconnaissance turned up little enemy contact — the country along the border was largely empty —
and on **16–17 April** the 1st Brigade was airlifted back to An Khe in thirty-seven C-130 sorties.
By the evening of the 17th, D Company was back at **Camp Radcliff** (An Khe, Binh Dinh Province).

### April 22 — five days later

Rippy was killed five days after that return. The official record is clear on the mechanism:
**explosive device**, hostile, died outright. What it does not settle is the exact place. The
**An Khe TAOR was itself heavily mined and booby-trapped** — the base perimeter and the areas
of operation around Camp Radcliff were a persistent hazard — and the **Highway 19 corridor** (the
Pleiku–An Khe road the division's convoys used) was repeatedly flagged in the period's after-action
reports as mine-threatened. Either a base-area device or a road incident is consistent with the
record, though the brigade's airlift home (rather than a convoy move from the forward LZs) makes a
Highway 19 incident on the return leg unlikely. The circumstances are not yet established from
primary sources.

The casualty province of record in the DCAS is "Quang Nam" — an I Corps province on the coast
far from where D Company was operating. This is almost certainly a data-entry error; the 1st
Cavalry Division was in the II Corps highlands in April 1966, not in the Marine TAOR. The Virtual
Wall carries the province as not reported.

### SP4 Terry Rippy

Terry Allen Rippy was from **Hammond, Oregon**, a small community on the north coast of Clatsop
County. He arrived in Vietnam on **November 30, 1965**, among D Company's first men in country.
Airborne-qualified — MOS 11H2P, infantry direct-fire crewman — he had been with the company through
the division's early operations, including the Pleiku Campaign and the highlands sweeps that winter.
He was 23 years old and 144 days into his tour when he was killed.

He is memorialized on the **Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Panel 6E, Line 128**.
