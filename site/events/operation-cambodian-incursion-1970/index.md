## The Cambodian Incursion — D Company in the Fishhook

> **Context page — operation level (umbrella).** Built from the U.S. Army Center of Military
> History's airmobility volume, with the 2/8 Cavalry firebase records and the Army's Illingworth
> account confirming the battalion's role. It frames **D Company's 1970 in and around Cambodia** —
> the year the First Team carried the war across the border. The campaign's shape is thoroughly
> documented at division and brigade level; the company's day-by-day actions, and the specific
> contacts behind the year's casualties, await the battalion staff journal and are documented on
> their own event pages as they are built.

### What it was

The **Cambodian Incursion** (1 May – 30 June 1970) was the **1st Cavalry Division's defining
operation of 1970** — and the first time a large U.S. airmobile force fought outside the borders
of South Vietnam. For nine years the Viet Cong and North Vietnamese had run their war in III and
IV Corps from sanctuaries just inside Cambodia: a network of base camps housing the **Central
Office for South Vietnam (COSVN)** and a vast, truck-supplied logistics complex of warehouse
caches that allied operations like Junction City and Attleboro had never been allowed to reach.
By early 1970 the 1st Cavalry — straddling the infiltration trails out of Cambodia toward Saigon
across a 14,000-square-kilometer area of operations — had pushed its small, hasty fire bases right
up to the border, certain that the warehouses feeding those trails lay just beyond it.

On 26 April 1970, MG **Elvy B. Roberts** was ordered to plan a coordinated attack into the
**"Fishhook"** of Cambodia. The U.S. operation — officially **Operations TOAN THANG 43, 45, and
46** — opened on **1 May** behind B-52 strikes and massed artillery. **Task Force Shoemaker**,
under the division's Assistant Division Commander for Maneuver, BG **Robert M. Shoemaker**, made
the assault: the **3rd Brigade, 1st Cavalry** (with attached mechanized infantry and tanks), the
**3rd ARVN Airborne Brigade**, and the **11th Armored Cavalry Regiment**, with the **1st Squadron,
9th Cavalry** screening. Tactical surprise was complete. Over the next weeks the cavalry overran
**"The City"** (a three-square-kilometer storage depot found by C Co, 1/5 Cav on 5 May) and
**"Rock Island East"** (329 tons of munitions, found by the 2nd Brigade on 8 May), and a chain of
smaller caches. COSVN's headquarters was never found, but the supply system feeding the enemy's
war in the south was gutted: by the time the last U.S. aircraft left Cambodia at **1523 on
29 June 1970**, the division (with attached units) had accounted for **2,574 enemy killed** and
captured over **2,200 tons of rice** and **ten million rounds of ammunition**.

### Where 2/8 Cavalry — and D Company — fit

2/8 Cavalry fought the incursion as a **3rd Brigade** battalion — part of Task Force Shoemaker's
assault force. The ORLL for this period (DTIC AD0514580) documents the battalion's entry: the
3rd Brigade's initial assault (1–4 May) committed the 2-5 Cav, TF 2-7 Cav, 2-34 Armor, and
2-47 Mech into the COSVN Base Area under Task Force Shoemaker. On **20 May**, the 3rd Brigade
assumed OPCON of 2-8 Cav, which displaced from Phuoc Vinh and established **FSB Glider**
(XU807383) inside Cambodia — Phase 3 of the brigade's campaign, directed against enemy Base
Area 350 to the northeast. From 20 May through 29 June the battalion's companies worked a
cache-rich corridor: **A Company** found a large automotive-parts cache on 25 May (XJ776366);
**C Company** found an ammunition cache (XU802368) on 26 May; **D Company** found a
**communications cache at XU803347 on 5 June** — consistent with the CMH airmobility volume's
identification of D Company, 2/8 Cav as discovering an enemy communications-supply depot
between 25 May and 9 June that the North Vietnamese "did not have time to evacuate." The
battalion's firebase records place 2/8 Cav at fire support bases inside Cambodia — **FB Gonder**
(20 May–27 June), **Picatinny East** (a C Company weapons cache, 27 May–2 June), and **FB
Eunice** (opened by C Company 26 June) — which closed on **4 July** when 2-8 Cav withdrew to
Bien Hoa for stand-down. After three days' rest, the battalion reopened **FSB Union** on **7
July** and resumed operations to interdict NVA infiltration routes in Phuoc Long Province.

That places D Company squarely in the operation. The company's **1970 losses bracket it**, in
three phases:

- **The border buildup (winter–spring, War Zone C).** Before the incursion, the division was
  already pressing to the Cambodian border out of Tay Ninh and Binh Long, fighting from small
  hasty fire bases. 2/8 Cav opened **FB Mary Gwen** on 24 February ("Incursion," per the firebase
  data) and held **FB Illingworth**. D Company's dead in this phase ran through the winter and
  early spring: **PFC Kenneth Frierson** (25 Jan), **PFC Henry Rava** (18 Feb), **CPL Kenneth
  Flashner** (28 Feb), **CPL Francis Ware III** (6 Mar), **PFC Thomas Brockmeier** (7 Mar), **CPL
  Michael Jackson** (17 Mar), and **SP4 Stephen Alsup** (date disputed — 18 Mar or 6 Apr).
- **The incursion (May–June, Cambodia).** D Company's communications-depot cache discovery falls
  here, between 25 May and 9 June. Two D Company men died on **19 May** — **SSG James Cooney** and
  **CPL Kenneth Gonder** — at the opening of the cross-border phase. Gonder's death is recorded as
  **non-hostile** (an accident, in Binh Duong Province at the division's rear, not in Cambodia);
  Cooney's classification still needs checking against his own sources. The next day, **20 May**,
  2/8 Cav opened a fire base inside Cambodia named **[FB Gonder](/locations/fsb-gonder/)** — very
  likely in his memory.
- **After the return (autumn, III Corps).** The division pulled back across the border at the end
  of June. **CPL Craig Waterman** (3 Sep) fell that autumn, back in the III Corps area of
  operations.

### The Battle of FSB Illingworth — the battalion's signature fight of early 1970

The one engagement of this period with a full record is **not a D Company action**, but it is
2/8 Cavalry's, and it belongs in the frame. **Fire Support Base Illingworth** — a hasty base in a
dry pond bed about five miles from the Cambodian border, ringed by little more than a low berm and
claymores — was named for **CPL John James Illingworth** of **A Company, 2/8 Cav**, killed 14 March
1970. At **2:18 a.m. on 1 April 1970**, after a twenty-minute barrage, roughly **400 men of the
272nd Regiment, 9th PAVN Division** assaulted the perimeter, defended by a patchwork of about 215
men including **Companies C and E, 2/8 Cavalry** and several artillery batteries. The defenders
held; **25 Americans were killed and 54 wounded.** Two soldiers received the Distinguished Service
Cross, and **SGT Peter C. Lemon** of **Company E (Recon), 2/8 Cav** — fighting on with grenades and
in hand-to-hand combat after his weapons jammed — received the **Medal of Honor**. Illingworth was
the 2/8 Cavalry's bloodiest day of the border buildup, the month before the division crossed into
Cambodia.

### What's still open

The campaign is thoroughly documented at division and brigade level, and D Company's presence in
Cambodia is confirmed by the published history. What is **not** yet pinned is the company's
day-by-day maneuver and, crucially, **which of the year's casualties fell on which action** — the
firebase data gives the fire bases, but the day-of records await the **2/8 Cav daily staff journal
(NARA RG 472)** and the division **ORLLs**. Those records will let us place each 1970 loss inside
or outside the 1 May–30 June Cambodia window, confirm the **FB Gonder** naming, reconcile the
campaign's end date with the firebase note on FB Eunice, and fix 2/8 Cav's brigade assignment
across the year. Those Tier 1 event pages are queued and will wire to this frame as they are built.
