## September 19, 1966 — a patrol pinned down near Bong Son

> **Confirmed and well-documented.** This action is recorded in detail in the Distinguished Service
> Cross citation of LTC Thomas H. Tackaberry, commander of 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry — the rare case
> in this archive where a single day's contact survives in a full first-order narrative. What is *not*
> yet pinned is the exact grid and the identities of the other men in the patrol.

On **19 September 1966**, during **Operation Thayer I** (the 1st Cavalry Division's search-and-destroy
operation in Binh Dinh Province, 13 September – 1 October 1966), a **fifteen-man patrol of D Company,
2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** was caught in an intense firefight with a **reinforced company of the
North Vietnamese Army** near **Bong Son**. At approximately **1500 hours** the patrol's platoon
leader — **2LT Michael Douglas Derosier**, 23, of Fort Walton Beach, Florida — was killed by
small-arms fire, and the patrol was pinned down and in danger of being overrun.

### The battalion commander's intervention

What happened next is the substance of **LTC Thomas H. Tackaberry's second Distinguished Service
Cross**. From the citation (HQ U.S. Army, Vietnam, General Orders No. 6537, 28 November 1966):

> "...At approximately 1500 hours a fifteen man patrol was engaged in an intense fire fight with a
> reinforced company of the North Vietnamese Army. The platoon leader had been killed and the patrol
> was pinned down. Lieutenant Colonel Tackaberry ordered his unarmed command helicopter to land near
> the action. Running through the intense fire, he reached the besieged patrol and assumed personal
> command of the unit. He then called for a reserve force to reinforce his position... he personally
> led the assault on the forward hostile bunkers and succeeded in driving the insurgents from their
> positions... Receiving word to extract his force, the patrol quickly returned to the landing zone
> and established a defensive perimeter. Two of the pickup helicopters were hit by intense fire from
> the rapidly regrouping hostile troops. Despite the threat of an all out insurgent assault on the
> landing zone, Lieutenant Colonel Tackaberry refused to leave until the rest of his men had been
> extracted. Along with two remaining soldiers, he dauntlessly continued to fire on the advancing
> hostile troops until being picked up by the unarmed command helicopter."

Tackaberry — who had taken command of 2/8 Cav only seven weeks earlier, on 30 July 1966 — went on to
become one of the most decorated soldiers in U.S. Army history (three Distinguished Service Crosses,
five Silver Stars) and retired a lieutenant general. This was his second DSC; the platoon leader whose
death set the action in motion was 2LT Derosier.

### 2LT Michael Derosier

Michael Douglas Derosier was a 23-year-old platoon leader (MOS 1542, Infantry Unit Commander) who had
arrived in Vietnam on 3 July 1966, ten weeks before his death. He is the only 2/8 Cav officer killed
on 19 September 1966, which identifies him as the platoon leader the citation describes. He is
buried at Arlington National Cemetery; his name is on the Wall at **Panel 10E, Line 117**. See his
[profile](/soldiers/derosier-michael/).

A note on the record: Derosier's official casualty file lists the province as **Quang Tin** (I Corps),
but that is an error — 2/8 Cavalry was operating in **Binh Dinh** near Bong Son on this date, and the
DSC citation places the action there. The Binh Dinh / Bong Son location is followed here.

### What's still open

The detailed *valor* narrative survives, but the *unit* record does not yet: the exact grid, the other
members of the fifteen-man patrol (the citation makes clear there were further casualties), and the
enemy regiment all await the 2/8 Cav daily staff journal (NARA RG 472) and the Operation Thayer I
after-action report.
