        Graduated Little Rock (Central) High School in 1952, tutored as a football tackle by

    - type: command

      location: "Song So Ha River, RVN/Cambodia border"
      from: "1964"
      to: "1964"
      notes: >
        First Vietnam tour, as a Special Forces adviser. On 30 August 1964, Captain Blagg was
        leading a 30-man South Vietnamese patrol seeking to intercept North Vietnamese soldiers
        crossing the Song So Ha River from Cambodia. The patrol was ambushed at a river bend by
        a battalion-size NVA unit firing from dug-in positions on the Cambodian side; most lead
        elements were hit in the initial contact and Blagg's interpreter was badly wounded.
        Blagg radioed his Special Forces camp for reinforcements, strapped his wounded
        interpreter to his back, and conducted a fighting withdrawal toward the linkup point —
        the NVA pursuing across the river into South Vietnam. The fight became hand-to-hand.
        Blagg was wounded in the face and his right arm was shattered by an enemy bullet, but
        continued fighting his patrol back to the linkup point. Once reinforcements arrived, he
        reversed the tide of the battle by launching an attack that drove the remaining NVA back
        into Cambodia (estimated 6-to-1 enemy casualty rate on bodies, blood trails, and captured
        weapons). Not evacuated for treatment of his near-fatal wounds until after the battle was
        won. Awarded the Silver Star. SOURCE: ASU ROTC Hall of Heroes citation (full text);
        Encyclopedia of Arkansas.
    - type: other

      unit: "United States Military Academy, West Point"
      location: "Indiana University; West Point, New York"
      from: "1969"
      to: "1971"
      notes: >
        After recovering from his 1964 wounds, the Army sent Blagg to Indiana University for a
        master's degree in English. From 1969 he taught English composition and literature at
        West Point, including Chaucer's *The Canterbury Tales*.
    - type: command

      from:

        A coveted assignment regarded as an audition for promotion to brigadier general, under
        commanding general MG John W. Hudachek. When Colin Powell arrived at Fort Carson as
        deputy commanding general and sought Blagg's opinion on delivering unwelcome advice to
        Hudachek, Blagg warned him off: "Don't do it. Because it's not a problem Hudachek can
        discuss or even admit. I'm warning you — you won't help him. And you might hurt
        yourself." (Powell, *My American Journey*, pp. 267–268.) Powell delivered the advice
        anyway and was marked down by Hudachek as a result. Hudachek separately gave Blagg — "the
        Chief," admired by other officers for his performance — an efficiency report Blagg said
        "damned me with faint praise." When seven general officers told Blagg they could get his
        career back on track, he told them: "Forget it. I'm done." Retired as a Colonel in 1982
        after nearly 27 years on active duty.

notes: >
  RANK (RESOLVED 2026-07-27): retired a full Colonel in 1982 after nearly 27 years on active
  duty (Encyclopedia of Arkansas; Gazette obituary). Held LTC — the standard Vietnam-era
  battalion-commander rank — specifically while CO, 2/8 Cav, Jul 1971 – Jun 1972; "Col. Tom
  Blagg" in the Kutter speech/memorial booklet reflects his eventual/final rank, not his rank
  at the time of that command, resolving the previously open question.
  IDENTITY: Thomas Elwood Blagg, born October 20, 1934, in Prairie Grove, Benton County, AR
  (Encyclopedia of Arkansas) — the Gazette obituary instead gives "Prairie View, Arkansas,"
  which does not appear to be an actual place name and is treated as a transcription variant
  of Prairie Grove. Third of eight children of John and Marietta ("Mary") Blagg. Family moved
  to Little Rock, Pulaski County, in the early 1940s (father worked as a butcher and for Dillaha
  Fruit Company); graduated Little Rock (Central) High School, 1952. Arkansas State College
  (now ASU), Class of 1956 — football, journalism major, editor of the school paper, ROTC cadet
  colonel, Distinguished Military Graduate, commissioned 2LT Infantry.
  FAMILY: married Billy J. Blagg (met at Arkansas State); 64 years of marriage; she predeceased
  him. Three daughters: Leah Ash (Jim), Melanie Maxwell (Roy), Aimee Van Ausdall (David). Six
  grandchildren, five great-grandchildren. Surviving siblings at his death: four sisters
  (Annette Ledbetter, Janette Buchanan, Sarah Eudy, Nena Batson); predeceased by two brothers
  (Bob and Ted) and a sister (Susie Page).
  DEATH: died at home, Colorado Springs, CO, January 22, 2023, age 88. "A Celebration of Life"
  was planned for Summer 2023 per the obituary. Tribute from retired LTG Robert T. Clark, a
  junior officer in Blagg's Vietnam battalion: "I never met a better soldier/leader."
  CAREER ARC beyond 2/8 Cav: M.A. English (Indiana University) → taught English at West Point
  (from 1969) → 2/8 Cav command (Jul 1971 – Jun 1972, this site's focus) → ran the military
  segment of Nixon's second inaugural parade (Jan 20, 1973) → U.S. Army War College, Carlisle
  Barracks → brigade commander, 101st Airborne Division, Fort Campbell (alongside fellow brigade
  commander Colin Powell) → Chief of Staff, 4th Infantry Division, Fort Carson, CO, under MG
  John W. Hudachek (with Powell as deputy commanding general) → retired Colonel, 1982, after the
  "damned me with faint praise" efficiency report episode recounted in Powell's memoir.
  POST-SERVICE: moved with Billy from Colorado Springs to Gunnison, CO in the early 1980s;
  directed the Western State College (now Western Colorado University) Foundation, later the
  school's physical plant; returned to Colorado Springs in 1990 and taught English for a decade
  at Pikes Peak Community College (now Pikes Peak State College).
  2/8 CAV DATES: arrived/departed (Jul 1971 – Jun 1972) match this site's independently
  researched CO command chain (see stannard-john.md notes, dubia-christian.md,
  gonder-kenneth's alongside-tier-4 fix, Session 116) and are now corroborated by an
  independent published source (Encyclopedia of Arkansas: "in 1971 he was deployed as commander
  of an infantry battalion in the First Cavalry Division"). Exact month-level start/end are
  still not confirmed by a primary command roster — precision here is "circa," not exact.
