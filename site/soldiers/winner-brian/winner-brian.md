## Brian Carl Winner

**PFC Brian Carl Winner**, D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry, was killed in
action in the Republic of Vietnam; his roster date of death is **June 7, 1968**. He
was from **Detroit, Michigan**, and had arrived in-country on May 13, 1968 —
roughly three weeks before his death. He is inscribed on the Vietnam Veterans
Memorial, **Panel 58W, Line 1**.

> **Research stub.** Identity, decorations, and service dates are sourced from
> Honor States, Virtual Wall, and VVMF Wall of Faces. The combat narrative below
> is a medium-confidence candidate match to a journal-recorded action — the
> journal never names individuals, and the roster date is a *recovery* date,
> not necessarily the date of the action itself.

### What is known

The D/2-8 Cav company daily journal records a bunker-complex contact with NVA
forces on **June 4, 1968**, at grid **YD324393** (1345 hours): the company engaged
with automatic-weapons fire while scout helicopters took fire, resulting in one US
KIA whose body was not recovered — carried missing in action — one US WIA, and one
NVA KIA. A 10th Sapper Battalion command post was assessed nearby at YD323392.

Three days later, on **June 7**, the journal records that a US KIA was found at
grid YD325391 (1105 hours) and identified by the platoon leader as the man missing
since June 4 — his remains partially decomposed. Five NVA KIA from the intervening
5-6 June fighting, and a captured SKS rifle, were found in the same area.

Winner's roster date of death, **June 7**, lines up with this recovery/
identification date rather than June 4, the date of the actual contact. Virtual
Wall independently records the casualty as hostile fire, "died outright," in Quang
Tri Province — consistent with a man killed in the contact itself rather than one
who lingered before dying; the multi-day gap is explained by the delay in
recovering his body, not a delayed death.

PFC Winner's confirmed decoration is the **Purple Heart**. Honor States lists
several additional decorations as probability-based awards common to his era and
MOS; these remain unconfirmed pending a primary source.
