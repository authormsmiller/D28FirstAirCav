SP4 Marvin Dale Miller to his brother **Dan**, 8 March 1971 — the only *in-country* letter in the collection written to a sibling rather than to his mother, and it reads like it: fishing season, a .22 pistol Dan bought off Rudy, trouble starting the Ford, the welder Dan and Buss had gone in on, and a tired-sounding instruction to quit trying to keep him in cameras and film. Signed simply "Marv."

---

8 Mar 71

Hi Dan,

Well Mom said you were getting ready for the fishing season. I guess it is getting about that time of year again.

What make is the 22 pistol you got off of Rudy. I can't ever remember him having one so he must have just got it recently. I'm kinda surprised that Rudy would be allowed to own a pistol in the first place.

Did you have any trouble starting the Ford this winter yet. If you didn't it's a wonder. I had a good bit last winter. I still want to get a new car when I get home. I wish I knew a little more about how to get one though. Or what kind to get for that matter.

Mom told me about the welder Buss and you got. I guess it would come in pretty handy if you were building something out of metal.

Don't bother sending another camera. It's to much of a hassle. I doubt whether I'll take any more pictures anyway. If you can't find the right film for the camera I got just forget it. When I get back to the rear maybe I'll have a little more time for picture taking.

Well I can't think of much else to say so I'll close.

Marv

### Transcription Notes

- Blue ballpoint, two pages. Some lens flare / blue blur along the lower-right edge of both pages, but all text is legible.
- **Salutation and signature differ from his usual.** This letter opens "Hi Dan," (not "Dear Mom,") and closes simply "Marv" — no "Love." It is the only *in-country* letter in the surviving collection addressed to a sibling rather than his mother (a separate, undated letter to Dan survives from Marvin's training days), and the register is noticeably more casual.
- "What make is the 22 pistol" — Marvin wrote "is," appears to have started to strike it through, then left the sentence as is; transcribed as written. "22 pistol" is his shorthand for a .22-caliber pistol.
- His own usages preserved: "to much" (for "too much"), "kinda," and the question marks dropped after "this winter yet" and "for that matter."

### Archivist Notes

**A letter to a brother — the only one from Vietnam.** Every other surviving *in-country* letter is to his mother (a separate, undated letter to Dan survives from Marvin's training days back in the States). This one is to **Dan**, one of his older brothers, and it shows a different side of Marvin: no reassurance about the war, no money sent home, no roll-call of the nieces and nephews — just brother-to-brother talk of guns, cars, fishing, welding, and cameras. He even signs it "Marv" rather than the "Love, Marvin" he uses with his mother. Written **8 March 1971**, it sits squarely inside the early-March cluster: one day after the **7 Mar 71** letter and one day before the **9 Mar 71** letter, in which Marvin sends his income-tax paperwork home for "Dan or Buss" to fill out — the same two brothers, the welder partners, named here.

**"Don't bother sending another camera."** The standout passage, and part of a thread that runs the length of the tour. The family had been trying to keep Marvin supplied with a working camera and the right film, and here — sounding worn down by it — he tells Dan to stop: "It's to much of a hassle. I doubt whether I'll take any more pictures anyway… When I get back to the rear maybe I'll have a little more time for picture taking." He did not, in fact, stop: a **Kodak 124** turns up in the **13 Apr 71** letter, and through the summer he is developing rolls and mailing prints home (**12 Jul**, **26 Jul**, **4 Aug 71**). The photographs that survive in this archive exist despite the very frustration he voices here.

**The Ford, and a new car when he gets home.** "Did you have any trouble starting the Ford this winter yet… I still want to get a new car when I get home." The family's old Ford is a recurring worry — its new transmission is the subject of the faint, content-dated **~Feb 1971** letter ("when Dan gets it out"), and the maintenance plan traces back to the **10 Jan 71** letter. The wish for a new car of his own, once he's back, is one of the small future-tense hopes that surface across the correspondence.

**The welder — Dan and Buss.** "Mom told me about the welder Buss and you got." Marvin's brothers **Dan** and **Don ("Buss")** had gone in on a welder together. It fits the steady picture of the brothers working on metal and on the family property — the same household of projects that produces Buss's porch (proposed **7 Mar**, photographed by **18 Oct 71**) and the long rebuild of the family home.

**Rudy and the .22.** "What make is the 22 pistol you got off of Rudy… I'm kinda surprised that Rudy would be allowed to own a pistol in the first place." A glance at family/neighborhood life back in Kittanning — Dan had bought a .22 pistol off a man named **Rudy**, and Marvin's surprise that Rudy "would be allowed to own a pistol" hints at some history not spelled out here.

**Location:** Unnamed, but in this stretch Marvin was at **FSB Fontaine** — the move he names two days later in the **9 Mar 71** letter ("I moved to a new base called Fontaine").
