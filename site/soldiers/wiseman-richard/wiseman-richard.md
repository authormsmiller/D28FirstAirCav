      He was assigned to Headquarters & Headquarters Company (HHC), 2nd Battalion, 8th
      Cavalry; the unit honor roll lists him as attached to D Company — typical of the
      battalion assets (RTOs, forward observers, recon, commo, medics, heavy weapons) sent
      out to a line company in the field. He had been in-country only one month. He was
      21 years old.
      He is near-certainly one of the casualties of the confirmed D Company, 2/8 Cavalry
      contact in the "Dog's Head" that day. The 1st Cavalry Division's official Operational
      Report (DTIC AD0512505) logs the action as "181110 Mar XT045833" — 18 March, 11:10 hrs
      — D Co receiving small-arms, automatic-weapons, and B-40 rocket fire, the contact
      breaking fifteen minutes later with 14 US wounded and no killed. Wiseman's "died of
      wounds" classification fits a man wounded there who died of those wounds; the date,
      unit, province, and the fragmentation wounds (a B-40 is a fragmentation weapon) all
      line up. The one step short of name-confirmation is the 2/8 Cav daily staff journal
      (NARA RG 472), which would list the wounded by name. See
      /events/contact-dogs-head-1970-03-18/.
