notes: >
  Command stub (status: researching) — Bacon/Blagg model. LTC Thomas H. Tackaberry,
  commander of 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry during Operation Thayer II; relieved by LTC
  John C. Dashiell on 7 Feb 1967 (source: 1st Cav Div ORLL period ending 30 Apr 1967,
  DTIC AD0385642). He was the battalion CO at the time of the 28 Jan 1967 D Company
  action that killed CPL Keller and CPL Yates — linked to both as a chain-of-command
  "Served Alongside" (Tier 3) connection. He assumed command of 2/8 Cav from LTC John A. Hemphill on
  30 July 1966 (per the 22 Nov 1966 1st Cav Div ORLL command roster), so his tenure ran 30 Jul 1966 –
  7 Feb 1967. Arrival date and other service details unknown.
  BIOGRAPHY (Wikipedia / Congressional Record 19 May 2017): Thomas Howard "Tom" Tackaberry,
  b. 6 Sep 1923, Los Angeles; d. 3 Apr 2017, Fayetteville NC; buried Arlington National Cemetery.
  WWII, Korea, and Vietnam veteran; one of very few soldiers to earn THREE Distinguished Service
  Crosses, plus five Silver Stars. NOTABLE FOR 2/8: his second DSC was earned in 1966 as a lieutenant
  colonel near Bong Son, leading a search-and-destroy operation — when a 15-man platoon was pinned
  down and its leader killed, he landed his unarmed command helicopter, ran through enemy fire to take
  command, and led an assault on enemy bunkers that forced a larger NVA force to retreat. PINNED DOWN
  (2026-06-27): the citation (HQ USARV GO No. 6537, 28 Nov 1966) dates this action to 19 September
  1966 near Bong Son — i.e., Operation Thayer I — and the platoon leader killed was 2LT Michael D.
  Derosier (D Co 2/8), who is profiled here and wired to Tackaberry as chain-of-command. He called up
  a reserve force, personally led the assault on the NVA bunkers, and on extraction — with two pickup
  helicopters hit — refused to leave until all his men were out, firing alongside the last two soldiers
  until the command ship retrieved them. FULL ACCOUNT (with the citation in full): event page
  contact-bong-son-1966-09-19 — the single landing place for this action. Career: rose to Lieutenant General;
  commanded the 196th Infantry Brigade (1969, 3rd DSC), the 82nd Airborne Division, and XVIII Airborne
  Corps / Fort Bragg (1979–81); retired 1981 after 38 years. Remembered as a "grunt's angel."
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tackaberry ; https://valor.militarytimes.com/recipient/recipient-5898/
