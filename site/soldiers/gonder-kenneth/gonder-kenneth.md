    body: >
      CPL Kenneth Gonder began his tour with the 1st Platoon, D Company, 2nd Battalion,
      8th Cavalry (Airmobile), 1st Cavalry Division, Republic of Vietnam. A 1969 economics
      graduate of Wilkes College, he had been drafted that fall, trained at Fort Dix and Fort
      Lewis, and shipped overseas the weekend of March 1, 1970. By his family's account he
      disagreed with the war and had hated guns since childhood, but felt it his duty to go —
      resolving that he would not kill anyone.

    headline: "Died — non-hostile (Allied land mine), May 19, 1970"
    body: >
      CPL Kenneth Gonder was killed instantly on May 19, 1970, in Binh Duong Province when he
      stepped on an Allied Forces land mine while walking with his platoon — the circumstance his
      sister records in her tribute, and the reason the casualty is classified **non-hostile** in
      the Honor States and Virtual Wall records. He was 23, and had been in Vietnam a little over
      two months. His death came during the 1st Cavalry Division's Cambodian Incursion, while
      2/8 Cav was operating across the border; one day later, on May 20, the battalion opened a
      fire support base inside Cambodia named **[FB Gonder](/locations/fsb-gonder/)** — very
      likely in his memory. He was survived by his parents, Walter and Birtie, and two sisters,
      JoAnn and Robin. His name is inscribed on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Panel 10W, Line 64.
