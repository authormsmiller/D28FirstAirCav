timeline:
  - date: "1968-01-19"
    phase: in-country
    type: combat
    tags:
      - { type: c, label: "Silver Star" }
    headline: "Silver Star — Quang Tri Province, January 19, 1968"
    body: >
      Serving as a Platoon Sergeant with Company C, 1st Battalion, 3d Marines, SGT
      Bonnelycke's platoon was ordered to assist a friendly unit that had become heavily
      engaged with a numerically superior enemy force north of the Cua Viet River.
      The Marines were immediately pinned down by heavy small arms and automatic weapons
      fire from numerous fortified positions. Bonnelycke exposed himself to the fire,
      maneuvered across twenty-five meters of fire-swept terrain, and assaulted an
      enemy bunker — silencing it with a grenade. His actions helped his unit account
      for twenty-three enemy soldiers confirmed killed. He was awarded the Silver Star
      (Navy Award) for this action.

  - date: "1969-07-13"
    phase: in-country
    type: combat
    tags:
      - { type: c, label: "Silver Star" }
    headline: "Silver Star — Tay Ninh Province, July 13, 1969"
    body: >
      Now serving with D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry (Airmobile), SGT Bonnelycke
      was acting as Platoon Leader of the Second Platoon during a search and clear mission
      in Tay Ninh Province when the point element was ambushed with mines, machine-guns,
      and small arms fire. The initial contact wounded several men in his platoon. With
      no base of fire from which to mount a counter-attack, Bonnelycke moved forward to
      his wounded machinegunner, picked up the weapon, and laid down suppressive fire,
      enabling the platoon to attack. When his ammunition was spent, he seized grenades
      and silenced an enemy bunker with accurate throws. Five wounded soldiers were
      evacuated and the ambush was broken. He was awarded the Silver Star with Bronze
      Oak Leaf Cluster (2nd Award) for this action. General Orders No. 10412,
      HQ 1st Cavalry Division, August 26, 1969.

  - date: "1969-08-12"
    phase: in-country
    type: combat
    tags:
      - { type: c, label: "Silver Star" }
    headline: "Silver Star — Tay Ninh Province, August 12, 1969"
    body: >
      One month later, SSG Bonnelycke's base came under an intense rocket, mortar, and
      ground attack. He left the safety of his own bunker and went to a bunker that had
      taken a direct hit, pulling wounded soldiers to safety. He then raced to the berm
      and placed suppressive fire on the enemy with a machine gun; when it ran out of
      ammunition, he moved to a second gun and continued firing. Despite his own wounds,
      he repeatedly returned to the berm to engage the enemy and helped carry the wounded
      to safety. He was awarded the Silver Star with Second Bronze Oak Leaf Cluster
      (3rd Award) for this action. General Orders No. 5044, HQ 1st Cavalry Division,
      April 4, 1970.

notes: >
  PASSED AWAY: November 16, 2024, Lytle, Texas. Source: War Memorial article (warmemorial.org),
  "In Service to One's Country," which includes a photo of his headstone. Middle name confirmed
  as LEE (full name: Clyde Lee Bonnelycke) per same article. Address himself as "MSG B."
  BIOGRAPHY (Doug Sterner interview, Vietnam magazine April 2022 / HistoryNet):
  Born ~1939-1940 (was 28 on 19 Jan 1968). Hometown: Tacoma, Washington.
  Retired in Lytle, Texas (as of 2022).
  Enlisted USMC ~1957-1958; served 10+ years before discharge summer 1968.
  After USMC discharge, re-enlisted Army within weeks. Assigned to 3rd Infantry
  Division, Germany — not Vietnam as requested. USMC Silver Star tracked him down
  there and was presented in Germany. Kept requesting reassignment to Vietnam.
  "I wanted to go back where the fighting was at." — Bonnelycke to Sterner.
  Returned to Vietnam 1969, D Co, 2/8 Cav, 1st Cav Div.
  Earned two Army Silver Stars in 30 days (Jul and Aug 1969).
  Retired MSG (E-8) May 1, 1988. Total service: ~29 years (10 USMC + 19 Army).
  Full decorations: Silver Star ×3, Bronze Star ×4, Purple Heart ×2 + campaign medals.
  MCSN/ASN: 1806071/1806021 — DO NOT PUBLISH (service number).
  Rank: SGT (USMC, Jan 1968); SGT acting as Platoon Leader (USA, Jul 1969);
  SSG (USA, Aug 1969); MSG at retirement. Published rank for profile: SSgt (combat rank).
  Platoon: Range. Nickname: Sergeant B.
  HISTORICAL DISTINCTION (sourced — publishable): Doug Sterner, curator of the Military
  Times Hall of Valor database and Army Vietnam veteran, researched the record and told
  Bonnelycke: "I could find no record of any other man receiving Silver Stars while
  serving with the Marine Corps and then the Army." Bonnelycke's reply: "Really? Can I
  hand the phone to my wife and have you tell her that?" The distinction is framed as
  "possibly" by HistoryNet — that qualifier is appropriate and should be retained in
  published text. Source: Vietnam magazine, April 2022; HistoryNet, March 21, 2022.
  HISTORYNET ERROR: Sterner's article lists his Army unit as "Company C, 2nd Battalion
  (Airmobile), 8th Cavalry." This is incorrect — both GOs confirm D Company. Confirmed
  as D Company by primary sources and researcher (Michael Miller). Do not propagate
  the "Company C" error.
  PRIVATE NOTE: Bonnelycke was the individual who fired on CPL Craig Waterman (KIA 3 Sep
  1970) in a friendly fire incident — Waterman had gotten outside the wire at night and
  tripped a flare near Bonnelycke's squad position. This information is held in private
  research notes and is NOT published. See: site/soldiers/waterman-craig/waterman-craig.md
  and site/events/contact-binh-tuy-1970-09-03/index.md (admin notes only).
