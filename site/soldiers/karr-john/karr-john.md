timeline:
  - date: "1969-03-12"
    phase: in-country

    tags:

    headline: "Arrived in Vietnam — March 12, 1969"
    body: >
      1LT John Karr began his tour with D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry
      (Airmobile), 1st Cavalry Division, Republic of Vietnam, taking over as leader of
      White Skull (1st Platoon) after 1LT Doug Magruder was wounded a second time and did
      not return. Per CPT Henry Colavita's memoir, Karr had completed on-the-job training
      with Alpha Company beforehand; tall, blond, and confident, he made a strong,
      immediate impression on Colavita, who introduced him to fellow platoon leaders 2LT
      Bob Babas and 2LT David Spingath. A separate photo caption elsewhere in the same
      book confirms Karr's own radio callsign directly: "White Skull 6."

  - date: "1969-05-25"
    phase: in-country
    type: combat
    tags:
      - { type: c, label: KIA }
    headline: "Killed in action — May 25, 1969"
    body: >
      On May 25, 1969, 1LT John Karr — leader of D Company's Skull Platoon, radio
      callsign "White Skull" — was killed in Hau Nghia Province when a single
      command-detonated B-40 rocket, rigged in trees above a trail, was set off over
      the platoon. The blast killed Karr together with his radio-telephone operator,
      CPL Wayne Garven, and CPL Richard White; his casualty record gives the cause as
      an "other explosive device." Remembered by a comrade as a VMI graduate and,
      possibly, a new father, Karr had been in Vietnam barely ten weeks. See
      /events/trail-ambush-hau-nghia-1969-05-25/ and the family account left by the
      son of fellow RTO Ray Clooney.
