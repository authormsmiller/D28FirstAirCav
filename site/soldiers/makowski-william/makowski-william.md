# ── BIO ───────────────────────────────────────────
bio: >
  William "Bill" Makowski grew up in Buffalo, New York, the only son in his family, with two sisters.
  He graduated from Kensington High School and worked at Arcata Graphics before his service.
  He volunteered for the draft — he did not seek deferment. Assigned to D Company, 2nd Battalion,
  8th Cavalry (The Angry Skippers), 1st Cavalry Division, he served as M-60 Machine Gunner in
  Range (2nd) Platoon. He was 20 years old — five days past his twentieth birthday — when he
  was killed in action.

    label: Killed in Action — Nui Ba Mountains, Binh Tuy Province
    notes: >
      At approximately 6 p.m., Bill's squad — under Squad Leader Doug Hilts, with RTO Jean
      "Dizzy" Blais — conducted the final security patrol around Range Platoon's Night Defense
      Perimeter. Fresh cuttings had been found in the jungle that day. The enemy ambushed the
      squad with AK-47s, an RPG, and detonated a claymore mine. Bill stayed up to return fire
      with his M-60 and was hit in the neck by shrapnel, severing his jugular vein. Hilts and
      Blais dragged him behind cover of two fallen trees and carried him back to the perimeter
      (~70 yards). A medic treated him but could not stop the bleeding. Bill died among his
      platoon mates. His body was extracted that evening by helicopter — flown by battalion
      commander Col. Tom Blagg, who refused to accept the medevac's refusal to fly. A ceremony
      was held for Bill at Firebase Jefferies the following day, with the company in formation,
      before his helmet, rifle, and boots.
      Note: DoD DCAS lists cause as "small arms fire." Booklet and Kutter speech confirm
      cause was shrapnel (claymore/RPG) — DoD classification uses "hostile action" as the
      overriding category.
