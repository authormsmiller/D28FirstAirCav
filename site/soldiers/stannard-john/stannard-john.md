  Command profile (status: researching) — Tackaberry model. LTC John Edward Stannard commanded
  2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry in Vietnam in 1967, including the Battle of Tam Quan (6–20 Dec 1967),
  part of Operation Pershing. He is the battalion CO at the time of the 11 Dec 1967 D Company night
  ambush (BS 926048) that killed SP4 Allan Follett and SP4 John Paulson — linked to that contact
  event and the Tam Quan operation page as chain-of-command context.
  COMMAND CONFIRMED (two independent sources): the West Point AOG memorial (USMA 1946, No. 15850)
  states he "went to Vietnam in 1967, first to command the 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry; then as brigade
  commander, 1st Brigade, 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile)"; and the Eager Arms 2/8 Cav veterans'
  accounts place him commanding 2/8 Cav at Tam Quan (Dec 1967) and through the move to Quang Tri.
  COMMAND TIMELINE (Eager Arms "Tet Offensive / Deployment to Quang Tri"): Stannard commanded 2/8 Cav
  until early February 1968, when he was promoted to Colonel and replaced Col. Donald "Snapper" Rattan
  as CO, 1st Brigade. His 2/8 successor was LTC Howard Petty (KIA by a mortar at LZ Betty, 15 Mar
  1968 — see petty-howard notes for LZ Betty vs LZ Sharon source discussion), then LTC John V.
  Gibney (16 Mar 1968). 2/8 CO chain: Hemphill (Sep 1965 → Jul 30, 1966) → Tackaberry
  (Jul 30, 1966 → Feb 7, 1967) → Dashiell (Feb 7, 1967 → Jul 30, 1967) →
  Stannard (30 Jul 1967 → early Feb 1968) → Petty (→ KIA 15 Mar 1968) → Gibney (16 Mar 1968
  → Oct/Nov 1968) → Dubia (Oct/Nov 1968 → Jul 1969) → Conrad (Jul 1969 → Apr 27, 1970)
  → Kingston (Apr 27, 1970 → Jul 1970) → Lytle (Jul 1970 → Oct 7, 1970) → Moore (7 Oct 1970 →)
  → Bacon (16 Feb 1971 →) → Blagg (~Jul 1971 →).
  SOURCE (Dashiell→Stannard date): ORLL AD0386215, command roster, period ending 31 Jul 1967.
  SOURCE (Moore→Bacon date): ORLL AD0518422, command roster, period ending 30 Apr 1971.
  At Tam Quan the 1st Bde CO was Rattan (signed the AAR, DTIC AD390613).
  BIOGRAPHY (West Point AOG memorial, Assembly Sep '94): John Edward Stannard, b. 3 Feb 1922 in
  Kenmare, North Dakota; raised in Williston, ND; d. 31 Oct 1993 (heart attack), Sumner County,
  Tennessee, aged 71; buried Arlington National Cemetery. Enlisted in the North Dakota National Guard
  (164th Infantry) in high school; the 164th was federalized Feb 1941 and became part of the Americal
  Division — the first U.S. Army division to fight in the Pacific, at Guadalcanal — where he earned a
  Purple Heart and Silver Star (he later wrote *The Battle of Coffin Corner and Other Comments
  Concerning the Guadalcanal Campaign*). Won an Americal Division appointment to West Point; USMA Class
  of 1946, commissioned 2LT Infantry. Korea 1952 with the 23rd Infantry, 3rd Division (company
  commander / battalion operations officer; 2nd CIB). Later: 508th & 325th Airborne Regiments, Airborne
  School instructor, C&GSC, West Point Admissions XO (NYU MBA), Armed Forces Staff College, National
  War College (1964), War Plans Division DCSOPS. VIETNAM 1967: CO 2/8 Cav, then CO 1st Brigade, 1st Cav
  Div — earning a 2nd and 3rd Silver Star and his 3rd CIB. A C Company 2/8 platoon leader (Gerald
  Saylor) recalled Stannard, after the company was caught in an NVA ambush, visiting each platoon at
  the night position and organizing a volunteer party that recovered the missing — including the dog
  handler and his dog — an action for which he received a Silver Star. One of very few soldiers to earn
  the Combat Infantryman Badge in three wars → inducted into the Infantry Hall of Fame, Fort Benning.
  Post-Vietnam: International Security Affairs (OSD); promoted brigadier general 1970; Asst Chief of
  Staff for Plans & Policy, HQ Allied Forces Southern Europe; CINCPAC staff (Hawaii); retired 1975.
  Survived by wife Llewellyn (Fowler), sons Dennis and Fred, daughter Llewellyn.
