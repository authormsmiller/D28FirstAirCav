  POSSIBLE D CO PREDECESSOR — FLAGGED, NOT CONFIRMED (2026-07-28): a new profile,
  soldiers/guest-james, documents CPT James A. Guest commanding a D Co, 2/8 Cav company
  in 1966-67 — the tour immediately preceding Scholes's own 1967-68 D Co command — with
  a strikingly parallel career leading up to it (both North Georgia College graduates;
  both held consecutive-generation postings at 10th SFG, Bad Tölz, Germany, just before
  their respective Vietnam tours: Guest 1962-65, Scholes 1963-66). This suggests Guest
  may have directly preceded Scholes as D Co CO, which would extend this archive's
  "Angry Skipper" D Co commander chain back one more tour. Not confirmed: neither
  Guest's nor Scholes's source material names the other or documents a direct handoff.
  This profile's "earned the nickname ... from his commander in the 1st Cavalry
  Division" line most likely refers to a battalion or brigade CO, not Guest
  specifically — do not read it as evidence of a Guest-to-Scholes nickname origin. See
  soldiers/guest-james admin notes for the full reasoning.
  SIGNIFICANCE — THE "ANGRY SKIPPER" NAME: Scholes is the origin point of the
  Angry Skipper Association's name (and, by extension, this archive's working
  title/README header, "Angry Skipper"). Per his obituary: he "earned the
  nickname Angry Skipper 6 from his commander in the 1st Cavalry Division due to
  his prickly disposition and immense desire to keep his men safe. The Angry
  Skipper Association survives today, representing the soldiers who served from
  1968 through 1972, based on Ed's insistence to live by this creed." D Company's
  own radio callsign was "Skipper" (see the Skipper Journal, D/2-8 Cav's 1968
  company daily log); Scholes, as company commander, was "Skipper 6"; "Angry" was
  personal to him specifically. Featured prominently in his own timeline/service
  record above per Michael's request (2026-07-16) — this should not be buried in
  admin notes alone.
  D CO COMMAND SEQUENCE: Scholes commanded D Co as "Angry Skipper 6" in 1967-68 —
  immediately preceding CPT Rodney Grannemann, who held D Co command (as "Skipper
  6," without "Angry") by 24 May 1968 (Silver Star action) through at least 8 Jun
  1968 (see soldiers/grannemann-rodney). The two are back-to-back or near
  back-to-back D Co commanders; exact handoff date between them not yet sourced.
  Worth a cross-link once confirmed.
  IDENTITY: born 16 Aug 1939, Copperhill, Tennessee; raised McCaysville, Georgia,
  son of Marie and Alvin Scholes. Died 23 Oct 2024, Franklin, Tennessee, age 85.
  Survived by wife Elva (Bussey) Scholes (married 63 years — the day of his
  commissioning), daughters Kim and Tracy, son Mike, multiple grandchildren and
  great-grandchildren. To be interred Arlington National Cemetery. NOTE: the ASA
  Master Roster working checklist (_private/asa-roster-checklist.md, line 295)
  spells her name "Elba" — obituary gives "Elva (Bussey)"; not corrected in that
  file per standing practice of leaving the ASA roster untouched.
  EDUCATION: B.S. (cum laude), Physics, Distinguished Military Graduate, North
  Georgia College and State University (now Univ. of North Georgia), Dahlonega,
  GA; M.S., Operations Research, Naval Postgraduate School, Monterey, CA; U.S.
  Army War College, Carlisle, PA; USMC Command and Staff College; General Officer
  Capstone Course; NATO Senior Officer Course.
  CAREER ARC: commissioned 2LT Infantry 1961 → XO/platoon leader, Ft Benning
  (1961-63) → CO, ODA, 10th Special Forces Group, Bad Tölz, Germany (1963-66) →
  CO (Angry Skipper 6), D Co 2/8 Cav + S-3 1/12 Cav, Vietnam (1967-68, 1st tour) →
  Senior Advisor, I Corps Ranger Command/BDQ, Vietnam (1970-71, 2nd tour) →
  Executive Assistant to Comptroller of the Army, Pentagon (1971-74) → USMC
  Development and Education Command rep, Quantico (1975-76) → Bn Cmdr, Korea
  (1976-77) → Cmdr, 2nd Training Bn, Ft Benning (1977-79) → Deputy G-3, XVIII
  Abn Corps (1980-83) → Cmdr, Infantry Training Bde, Ft Benning (1983-85; ~38,000
  Infantry soldiers trained) → Deputy Cmdr/Chief of Staff, Third U.S. Army/ARCENT
  (1985-88) → ADC, 82nd Airborne Div (1988-89) → Chief of Staff, JTF-South/XVIII
  Abn Corps — Operation Just Cause, Panama (1989-90) → Chief of Staff/Deputy Cmdr,
  XVIII Abn Corps — Desert Shield/Desert Storm, Saudi Arabia (1990-91; Forward
  Cmdr for the first 3 weeks; 50,000 troops) → Deputy Cmdr, XVIII Abn Corps/Ft
  Bragg (1991-93) → Deputy Cmdr/Cmdr, U.S. Army Forces, NATO Land Southeast
  Command, Izmir, Turkey (1993-95) → retired Major General, 1996, Ft Benning.
  Also led the XVIII Abn Corps Hurricane Andrew response (1992). Post-retirement:
  Program General Manager, Vinnell/Northrop Grumman Corp., Saudi Arabian National
  Guard training contract (1996-2002, six and a half years in Saudi Arabia); then
  private defense consultant (Iraq, Afghanistan, Jordan, Algeria, Colombia, Peru,
  Bolivia) from 2002. Ranger Hall of Fame (2007); Georgia Military Veterans' Hall
  of Fame, for Valor (2015); DAR (Atlanta) Medal of Honor (2009); Honorary Aide de
  Camp to Georgia Gov. Zell Miller (1991); North Georgia College Alumni Hall of
  Fame.
