    body: >
      On October 8, 1969, CPL Albert Altizer drowned during a river crossing on the Song Be
      River, in Phuoc Binh Province. A platoon-sized patrol was crossing in two boats;
      Altizer — a member of Third Squad, with fellow squad members Joseph Benson and Jerome
      Taylor — boarded the first boat in full combat gear, with twelve other men. About fifty
      feet out the boat began taking on water as it accelerated; the lieutenant in charge
      ordered the men to drop their packs, but the craft capsized. The second boat ferried
      survivors to the Song Be Bridge. A head count found three men missing. The shoreline
      search that afternoon recovered Benson and Taylor, but not Altizer; only some of his
      equipment was found downstream, and the search for him went on for days. Later
      intelligence reported an American killed and buried in the vicinity of Binh Duong — a
      possible match to Altizer that was never confirmed. He was listed Killed, Body Not
      Recovered, and was promoted to Corporal while still listed missing. He remains
      officially Missing in Action, memorialized among the missing at the Courts of the
      Missing, National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific (Honolulu Memorial), and on Panel
      17W, Line 49 of the Wall. He was 20 years old and had been in Vietnam about nine weeks.
      See /events/river-crossing-song-be-1969-10-08/.
