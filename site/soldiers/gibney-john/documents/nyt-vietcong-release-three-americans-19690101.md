# VIETCONG RELEASE THREE AMERICANS

**By Joseph B. Treaster — The New York Times, January 1, 1969, Page 1**

*G.I.'s Freed at 2d Meeting in Field — U.S. Major Held 5 Years Also Returns.
Vietcong Free Three Americans at Second Meeting in the Field.*

> Transcript of the digitized New York Times archive article, saved by Michael
> Miller (2026-07-15) from a TimesMachine capture. Preserved here as the
> primary-source account of the January 1, 1969 release LTC John Gibney led.

---

SAIGON, South Vietnam, Wednesday, Jan. 1 — Three American prisoners of war
were released this morning by the Vietcong. The release came during the
second meeting between representatives of the United States and the National
Liberation Front, or Vietcong, at a battlefield about 50 miles northwest of
Saigon. The first meeting, on Christmas Day, ended in a procedural stalemate
after more than two hours.

The three soldiers freed by the Vietcong are Pfc. Donald G. Smith of Akron,
Pa., Specialist 4 James W. Brigham of Ocala, Fla., and Specialist 4 Thomas N.
Jones of Lynnville, Ind., all 21 years old and all captured more than a year
ago.

About 24 hours before the release, a United States Army Special Forces
officer was recovered in the Mekong Delta. The officer, Maj. James N. Rowe of
McAllen, Tex., had been held captive by the Vietcong for more than five
years. It was not immediately clear whether he had escaped or been released
by his captors. A South Vietnamese officer was picked up with him.

When the Vietcong presented the three men, they were wearing bluish-gray
uniforms and yellow shower sandals. Each of them carried a rice bag that
appeared to contain personal effects. "Today you are allowed to retire to your
native land," the leader of the Vietcong delegation told the prisoners
through an interpreter. "We wish you will lead a happy life."

A United States military spokesman said that four of the five Vietcong
representatives at the meeting this morning had participated in the first
talks. Forty minutes after the talks began they were recessed for nearly an
hour. After about 15 minutes of discussion, the three prisoners were turned
over to the American delegation and then were put aboard a helicopter and
flown to the 24th Evacuation Hospital at Longbinh, 12 miles northeast of
Saigon. Newsmen at Longbinh reported that the three prisoners looked thin but
healthy. They all wore open-toed sandals and carried rice bags that appeared
to hold their personal belongings. All had moustaches and slightly long hair.
"Glad to be back," one of them said.

Major Rowe, the 31-year-old officer who was found by Government troops during
a sweep near the U Minh Forest on the Gulf of Siam, was a lieutenant when he
was captured and had been promoted automatically. He was reported to be in
satisfactory condition at an Army hospital near Saigon. He is a 1960 graduate
of West Point.

The prisoner talks with the Vietcong were held in a special corridor about 50
miles northwest of Saigon. The allies had said it would remain free of combat
until three hours after the meeting. The corridor, a little more than a mile
wide and extending six miles to the Cambodian border, became a necessity when
the allies decided to break the pattern of the last four years and not
observe the enemy's 72-hour cease-fire for the New Year. The allies acted for
the reason that the enemy had ignored such arrangements in the past.

As the truce, which was to end at 1 A.M. the next day, approached the midway
point the day before, a South Vietnamese Government spokesman said that the
enemy forces had violated their own cease-fire at least 10 times. A United
States military spokesman said that two helicopters had been shot down. Two
Americans were wounded in one of the crashes.

At the prisoner talks the five-man United States delegation consisted of four
officers who participated in the Christmas meeting and a Signal Corps
lieutenant who is skilled in photography. The lieutenant, Joseph V. Tourtelot
of River Forest, Ill., replaced Capt. Ronald R. Tesch, a doctor from Denver.
The leader was **Lieut. Col. John V. Gibney of Clearwater, Fla.**, and his
deputy and senior interpreter was Maj. Jean A. Sauvageot of Akron, Ohio. The
other members were Capt. Melvin R. Chatman, an interpreter, from Chicago, and
Capt. Burke O. Buntz, a communications officer and photographer, from
Defiance, Ohio.
