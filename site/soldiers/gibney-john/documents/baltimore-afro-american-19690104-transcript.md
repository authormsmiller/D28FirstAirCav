LANDING ZONE LIBERTY, Vietnam (UPI) — Freedom is a tiny tear in the corner of
a young soldier's brown eyes, a shaky hand-pat on the cold metal nose of a
helicopter, a chance for a moment to forget feet swollen by miles of forced
marching.

So it was, at 10:42 a.m. New Year's Day, when three 21-year-old Americans who
wondered whether they would ever see home again walked through a desolate,
scrubby little field and crossed the boundary between captivity and liberty.

A Viet Cong leader droned out their names in a singsong monotone — "Spec. 4
James W. Brigham... Spec. 4 Thomas N. Jones... PFC Donald G. Smith..." But
they seemed not to hear.

Above their heads waved a blue flag with its gold star [the NLF banner], but
they did not look up. To their front, five Communists and five U.S. military
representatives carefully, formally intoned the rites of official prisoner
release. But the young Americans stared straight ahead, or at the ground,
and almost did not seem to hear.

Then, "Dustoff," **Lt. Col. John V. Gibney, 38, of Oakdale, N.Y.**, head of
the U.S. delegation, curtly called — the signal for a helicopter to land to
pick up the three men.

Jim Brigham, of Ocala, Fla., had been a prisoner of the Viet Cong since he
was captured on a road-sweeping mission Sept. 13 [1967]. Jones, of Lynnville
[Lynville in the scan], Ind., was last seen driving a truck last Aug. 25
[1967]. Smith, of Akron, Pa., had been in Communist hands since last May
[1967].

"We came a long way through marshes and woods," said Brigham, who [appeared]
healthiest [of the three]. "I'm just happy to be released and hoping to get
home to my family." The other two returning soldiers did not [speak with]
newsmen.

The New Year's Day release followed [the] first U.S.-Viet Cong [meeting] on
Christmas [Day, which had ended without a release].

In Ocala, Fla., [Mrs.] Brigham — Jim's mother — sobbed with joy when she
heard of her son's release. "My prayers have been answered. I want to see my
boy just as soon as [possible]," she said. Mrs. [Mary Louise] Brigham, a
domestic worker with 11 children, broke into sobs during the phone interview
in which she learned the news.

## Notes for the profile

- Gives Gibney's age at the time (38) and a hometown that **conflicts** with
  the Washington Star and New York Times accounts: this UPI wire story says
  "of Oakdale, N.Y.," while both other contemporary sources say "of
  Clearwater, Fla." Recorded as an open discrepancy, not resolved here.
- New concrete detail: Gibney personally called "Dustoff" — the medevac/pickup
  call sign — as the operational signal that closed out the release, once the
  formal exchange was complete.
- Independently corroborates (a third contemporary source, after the two NYT
  pieces) that the Jan. 1 release was preceded by a first, unsuccessful
  Christmas Day meeting — consistent with Operation CHICAGO FIRE.
- Adds approximate capture dates/circumstances for the three freed POWs
  (Brigham: Sept. 13, road-sweep; Jones: Aug. 25, driving a truck; Smith: since
  last May) — not previously in the profile, included here for context though
  these three men are not part of the D Company roster this site otherwise
  tracks.
- The mother's story is affecting but is about the Brigham family, not
  Gibney; not built out further here, kept only as scene-setting context for
  the release itself.
