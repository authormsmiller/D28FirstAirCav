    body: >
      CPL Eskridge was killed by hostile action (small arms fire) on a search-and-destroy
      patrol in Binh Long Province, Republic of Vietnam (casualty records list Tay Ninh —
      see admin notes), while serving with D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry. He and
      SP4 Carl Pipher died in the same engagement, walking into a prepared enemy bunker
      complex on a three-platoon front; the fight continued through the night, with the
      enemy staying in their bunkers rather than pressing the attack. Company commander CPT
      Henry Colavita has given two independent firsthand accounts of this fight — a 2016
      oral history and his own memoir, which names Eskridge directly. CPL Eskridge was 21
      years old. Full account: events/operation-sheridan-sabre-1969-01-28.

  - date: "post-1983"
    phase: post-service
    type: personal
    tags:
      - { type: s, label: "Remembered" }
    headline: "A postwar visit to Tangier Island"
    body: >
      CPT Henry Colavita and two fellow "Angry Skipper" veterans, Ed Regan and Mike
      McGhie, made a postwar visit to Eskridge's home, Tangier Island, Virginia,
      hitching a mail/supply boat ride from Crisfield, Maryland. They laid a wreath at
      his grave and met his family, including his twin brother, then serving as the
      island's mayor. During the visit, Eskridge's sister showed Colavita a condolence
      letter that carried his own unsigned signature block but had actually been
      ghostwritten by the company's XO, Lt. Karl Derums — standard practice for the
      role at the time. Colavita disputed a claim in the letter that Eskridge had
      "walked into an ambush," a characterization he says doesn't match how Delta
      Company operated. Exact date not given in the source; placed here only as
      sometime after Colavita's 1983 retirement. See soldiers/derums-karl.
