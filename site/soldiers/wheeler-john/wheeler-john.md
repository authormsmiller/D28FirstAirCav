## John Clark Wheeler

**PFC John Clark Wheeler**, D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry, was killed in
action on **June 12, 1968**. He was from **Eugene, Oregon**, and had arrived
in-country on May 14, 1968. He is inscribed on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial,
**Panel 57W, Line 5**.

> **Research stub.** Identity, decorations, and service dates are sourced from
> Honor States, Virtual Wall, and VVMF Wall of Faces. Unlike three other D
> Company losses from this same month, no incident-level narrative is
> available yet — the gap is in the source, not the research.

### What is known

Virtual Wall records Wheeler's death as hostile fire, **an explosive device** —
not small-arms fire — in Quang Tri Province, and classifies it as died outright
rather than died of wounds.

The only Delta Company narrative source reviewed for this period so far is a
veteran-transcribed company daily journal covering 3 June-3 July 1968. It has an
acknowledged transcription gap from **June 10 to June 18**, during which the
company was on base defense at LZ Betty and then LZ Pedro. Wheeler's date of death,
June 12, falls squarely inside that gap — so this source has nothing to say about
the specific circumstances of his death.

The nearest single-casualty action in the transcribed record is June 5 (grid
YD325393, addressed on [Jacob Weldin's](/soldiers/weldin-jacob/) page) — seven days
before Wheeler's date of death, and a small-arms fire casualty rather than an
explosive-device one. That both the date offset and the cause of death diverge
rules out this action as a match, rather than merely leaving it uncertain.

An explosive-device cause is at least consistent with a base-defense setting — a
mine or booby trap on the perimeter, for instance — though no source yet confirms
the specific incident.

PFC Wheeler's confirmed decoration is the **Purple Heart**. Honor States lists
several additional decorations as probability-based awards common to his era and
MOS; these remain unconfirmed pending a primary source.

### Next source needed

The **2/8 Cav daily staff journal** (NARA RG 472, 10-18 June 1968) is the specific
gap this source leaves open, and the logical next document to pull — it may
describe the mine or booby-trap incident directly.
