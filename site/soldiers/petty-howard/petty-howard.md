  IDENTITY: Howard Palma Petty; born 9 Oct 1930; KIA 15 Mar 1968, age 37. Hometown:
  Pulaski, Giles County, Tennessee. Burial: Maplewood Cemetery, Pulaski, Giles County, TN.
  Wall: Panel 44E, Line 61. Tour start: 20 Jul 1967 (Virtual Wall). MOS: 1542. Service ID:
  O84703 (Regular Army officer — admin notes only, do not publish). Length of service:
  18 years (Virtual Wall) → entered service ~1950.
  FAMILY: Wife Edith (both Howard and Edith graduated University of Alabama, Tuscaloosa).
  Daughter: Pamela Anne Parsons. Parents: Mr & Mrs Roy Petty, Chattanooga, Tennessee.
  COMMAND: Succeeded LTC John Stannard as CO 2/8 Cav in early Feb 1968; KIA 33 days into
  command. Succeeded by LTC John V. Gibney (16 Mar 1968). Eager Arms confirms: Stannard
  "promoted to Colonel and replaced Colonel Donald 'Snapper' Rattan as Commander of the
  1st Brigade" in early February; Petty was named as "our new Battalion Commander."
  CASUALTY LOCATION — LZ BETTY vs LZ SHARON: Two sources conflict.
  (1) Eager Arms Bravo Company history: battalion was at "a newly established base called
  LZ Betty, located to the west of Quang Tri." Explicitly: "During the night of 15 March,
  the enemy attacked LZ Betty with mortars and rockets. A mortar round landed on the tent
  occupied by our Battalion Commander, LTC Howard Petty, and our Battalion Operations
  Officer, Major Barney Darby. LTC Petty was killed immediately and Major Darby was
  evacuated with serious wounds. LTC John V. Gibney assumed command of our battalion on
  16 March."
  (2) Wall of Faces (L.J. Adams, self-described S-3 NCO, posted 12/20/2007): "Co A 2/8th
  Cav 1st Cav LZ Sharon Quang Tri 1968 — I was his S-3 NCO, with him at LZ Sharon the
  night he was killed by a mortar round dropped on the officers bunker. The spotter was in
  the church tower, the VC mortar team was killed."
  ASSESSMENT: LZ Betty is the more reliable location. The Eager Arms account names the
  base explicitly in a unit-history narrative. Adams' "LZ Sharon" header likely describes
  his own company's home LZ, not the battalion TOC. Both sources agree: mortar attack,
  night of 15 March, Petty killed in his tent/bunker. The S-3 NCO (Adams) detail about
  a church tower spotter and the mortar team being killed is not corroborated elsewhere
  but is plausible; it may describe the follow-up action after the attack.
