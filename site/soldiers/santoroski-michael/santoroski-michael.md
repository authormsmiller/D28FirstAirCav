    body: >
      On September 6, 1967, seven weeks into his tour, PFC Santoroski stepped on a
      land mine in Vietnam and sustained severe wounds — metal fragment wounds to his
      right ankle, left thigh, and both legs — requiring surgical amputation of both
      legs below the knees. He was evacuated from the field to a hospital. Writing to
      his parents four days later, he was characteristically resolute: "Mom, please
      have courage. It's hard for me and you can make it easier if you are strong.
      God had planned for me. I accept it for I have my whole life ahead of me and
      I'm determined not to let this get me down."

  - date: "1967-09-26"

    headline: "Died of wounds — Army Hospital, the Philippines"
    body: >
      PFC Michael Paul Santoroski died of his wounds at an Army hospital in the
      Philippines on September 26, 1967 — twenty days after being wounded. He was
      20 years old. His last letters home mentioned that he expected to be transferred
      first to Tokyo for further treatment, then to Walter Reed Army Hospital in
      Washington. He did not survive to make that journey. He is remembered on the
      Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Panel 27E, Line 16.

  September 26, 1967 — twenty days after wounding. DCAS cause: "Hostile, Died of
  Wounds, Other Explosive Device, Ground Casualty." Wall: Panel 27E/16.
  MOS: 11C10 (Indirect Fire Infantryman — mortar crew). Grade E3.
  DOB: January 25, 1947, New York City. Hometown: Kingston, Ulster County, New York.
  Parents: Joseph F. and Marie E. (Lickvar) Santoroski, 95 Millers Lane, Kingston, NY.
  Siblings: Robert G., Stephen C., Thomas G. (brothers), Barbara (sister), all of Kingston.
  Paternal grandmother: Mrs. Barbara Santoroski, 763 Carson Street, Hazleton area.
  Maternal grandfather: Michael Lickvar, former resident.
  Funeral: Bonin Funeral Home → St. Stanislaus Church (high mass of requiem) → parish
  cemetery, Hazleton, Pennsylvania. Source: Hazleton-area newspaper obituary (exact
  paper title not confirmed; family copy).
  PROVINCE NOTE: VVMF lists casualty province as "PR & MR UNKNOWN." This is consistent
  with the newspaper account — he died at a U.S. Army hospital in the Philippines, outside
  Vietnam, so no Vietnamese province is recorded.
  BOLLING NOTE: Although Santoroski's casualty date (Sep 26) falls within the Operation
  Bolling window (Sep 19 – Oct 14, 1967, when 2/8 Cav was OPCON to the 173rd Airborne
  Brigade west of Tuy Hoa), his WOUNDING incident occurred on September 6 — before Bolling
  — in the Pershing AO in Binh Dinh. He was already evacuated and hospitalized when Bolling
  began. He is a Pershing casualty, not a Bolling casualty.
