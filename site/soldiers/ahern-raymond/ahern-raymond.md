notes: >
  EDITORIAL STANCE (2026-07-17, per Michael): CPT Colavita, Ahern's own
  company commander, considered him part of Delta Company — his memoir
  counts Ahern's death among D Company's own losses, not as a supporting
  artilleryman who happened to be nearby. This archive follows that lead:
  Ahern is presented here primarily as a D Company soldier (platoon: HQ,
  service_record lists his D Co service first), not as an outside
  attachment with a footnote. His administrative parent unit (C Battery,
  2/19 Artillery) and MOS (13E40) are still recorded, for accuracy, but
  are treated as secondary to how his own commander — and now this
  archive — counted him.
  CORRECTION / PUBLISHED (2026-07-17): this profile was held as a draft
  because its "D Co, 2/8 Cav" roster line was suspected to be a clerical
  artifact — the division-level ORLLs place his battery, C/2/19 Arty, in
  direct support of 1st Brigade in Tay Ninh, while D Co 2/8 Cav was a 2nd
  Brigade battalion in the Fishhook. That suspicion is now resolved: two
  independent sources — the Virtual Wall's own 07 Dec 2002 tribute ("Prior
  to his death on LZ Rita, Ray was attached to Delta Company, 2nd
  Battalion, 8th Cavalry as the Forward Observer's RTO. Ray spent most of
  his tour of duty with D 2/8 Cavalry...") and CPT Henry Colavita's memoir,
  "Company Grade: Memoir of an Angry Skipper" (Colavita commanded D Co at
  the time) — both independently confirm Ahern was attached to D Company as
  an artillery FO/RTO team for most of his tour, reassigned to his post on
  LZ Rita one day before his 26 Nov 1968 death. His parent battery's
  official 1st Brigade/Tay Ninh assignment and his attached, in-the-field
  service with a 2nd Brigade maneuver company are not contradictory — this
  is standard practice for artillery FO/RTO teams, and explains both the
  correct 1cda "D Co, 2/8 Cav" roster line and the "Tay Ninh Province"
  quirk on his official casualty record. Un-drafted and published on this
  basis. Decorations updated from the Virtual Wall's own confirmed listing
  (Bronze Star w/ Valor, Purple Heart, Air Medal, NDSM, VSM, VCM),
  replacing the earlier Honor-States-probability guesses. New cross-links:
  soldiers/hackett-larry (a D Co friend who left a 2005 tribute, reproduced
  in full as its own document) and soldiers/colavita-henry (his company
  commander at the time). See also the operation-sheridan-sabre-1968 event
  page and the fsb-rita location page, both updated to match.
  ORIGINAL RESEARCH LOG (predates the above correction):
  KIA 1968-11-26 — Died through hostile action — artillery rocket mortar, South Vietnam, Tay Ninh province.
  DOB 1948-01-14. Hometown: Philadelphia, Philadelphia County, PA. Wall: Panel 38W/63.
  Start tour: 1968-05-21 (Virtual Wall). MOS: 13E40 · Cannon Fire Direction Specialist.
  Unit (per sources): C Battery, 2nd Battalion, 19th Artillery, 1st Cavalry Division.
  Service ID 52814241 — DO NOT PUBLISH (Army-era IDs are SSNs).
  Decorations_unconfirmed are Honor States probability-based —
  move to confirmed when a primary source is located. Note: "Combat Infantryman
  Badge" is a Honor States probability inference and is unlikely for a 13E40
  artilleryman — flagged for review rather than assertion.
  UNIT RECONCILIATION: Honor States and Virtual Wall both record C Battery, 2/19
  Artillery, 1st Cav Div. The 1st Cavalry Division Association (1cda) KIA roster
  lists him under "D Co, 2nd Bn, 8th Cav" (AHERN RAYMOND JOSEPH JR SP4 19681126).
  The 13E (Cannon Fire Direction) MOS reconciles the two: he was most likely a
  fire-direction/forward-observer man from the direct-support artillery battery
  operating with D/2-8 Cav — the reason he appears on this archive's roster.
  Treat the 2/8 Cav association as inferred pending a primary source.
  OPERATIONAL CONTEXT (researched 2026-06-26, confirmed from primary sources): Ahern
  was killed 3 weeks into the 1st Cav Division's arrival in III Corps. The division
  moved 570 mi from I Corps to the Cambodian-border provinces NW of Saigon in late
  Oct–Nov 1968 (Operation Liberty Canyon) and launched Operation SHERIDAN SABRE (7 Nov
  1968 – 4 Apr 1969), its first major III Corps operation, under II Field Force's
  Operation Toan Thang II — interdiction of NVA infiltration from Cambodia. See event
  operation-sheridan-sabre-1968.
  AHERN'S PLACE IN IT (per the 1st Cav DivArty ORLL ending 31 Jan 1969, DTIC AD502415,
  PDF in this folder): 2/19 Artillery was in DIRECT SUPPORT of the 1st BRIGADE in the
  NE Tay Ninh AO, and his battery — C/2/19 — occupied LZ MUSTANG (XT 371820). So his
  "Tay Ninh province" casualty record is CORRECT: he was with the 1st Brigade's
  artillery in Tay Ninh. His death by hostile INDIRECT fire on a 13E40 fire-direction
  man fits a standoff shelling of a firebase, not a maneuver firefight. The division
  reports list no named action for 26 Nov at division/DivArty level. UPDATE
  (2026-07-17): the specific incident IS now pinned via Colavita's memoir and the
  Virtual Wall tribute — a night rocket attack on LZ Rita (not LZ Mustang) that scored a
  direct hit on Ahern's bunker. A division/DivArty-level primary source (2/19 Arty daily
  journal or 2/8 Cav S-3 journal, NARA RG 472) would still firm this up further, but the
  who/what/where/when is no longer an open question.
  UNIT MISATTRIBUTION (explicit) — SUPERSEDED, SEE 2026-07-17 CORRECTION ABOVE: this
  paragraph originally concluded Ahern was MISATTRIBUTED to D Company, 2nd Battalion,
  8th Cavalry on the 1cda KIA roster, and was NOT a D/2-8 infantryman — reasoning that
  the Division ORLL (AD502597) places 2/8 Cav in the 2nd BRIGADE (Fishhook/Binh Long AO),
  while the DivArty ORLL (AD502415) places his battery, C/2/19, in direct support of the
  1st BRIGADE in Tay Ninh. That reasoning about the two ORLLs is still accurate as far as
  it goes — but it did not account for attached-duty FO/RTO teams, which serve in the
  field with a maneuver company regardless of their parent battery's own administrative
  brigade assignment. Two independent sources (see the correction note above) now confirm
  Ahern was exactly that: a C/2/19 artilleryman attached to D Co, 2/8 Cav, for most of his
  tour. The "D Co 2/8" roster line was NOT a clerical artifact after all. (This paragraph
  itself had already superseded an earlier, even more mistaken draft note that inferred
  2/8 was 1st Brigade and used Ahern to support the Dec 4 cluster's Tay Ninh records —
  2/8 was 2nd Brigade, so the Dec 4 men's "Tay Ninh" records remain likely DCAS errors,
  unrelated to Ahern.)
  RESEARCH OPEN (as of 2026-07-17, the firebase/incident and unit attribution below are
  RESOLVED — see correction note above; remaining open items only): a division/DivArty-
  level primary source for the 26 Nov 1968 LZ Rita rocket attack (2/19 Arty or 2/8 Cav
  daily journal, NARA RG 472) would still firm up the account beyond Colavita's memoir
  and the Virtual Wall tribute; the other ~10 men 2/8 Cav lost in
  Nov 1968 (incl. PFC M.M. Canfield Jr., B/2-8, KIA 25 Nov) are tracked on the event
  page — note the 25 Nov LZ Ann fight is attributed to 1/7 Cav, so Canfield's LZ Ann
  tie is unconfirmed.
