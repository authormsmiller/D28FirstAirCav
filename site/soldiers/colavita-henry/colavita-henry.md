        First location in command was LZ Joe (Oct 1968; not yet located in
        the gazetteer), then LZ Rita (FSB Rita, XT505795, Binh Long Province/
        the Fishhook — see soldiers/pipher-carl, soldiers/eskridge-warren,
        and events/operation-sheridan-sabre-1969-01-28) through the
        battalion's relief there on 30 Jan 1969. The battalion CP then moved
        to LZ St. Barbara — a base NOT belonging to 1st Cav Division, where D
        Company did ambushes only and no perimeter defense (coordinates not
        yet confirmed; see admin notes on a likely false-match candidate).

        **The December 4, 1968 "Chicken Valley" ambush:** while linking up
        with Charlie Company at a captured enemy supply/document cache — the
        march during which the men nicknamed the area "Chicken Valley" —
        Charlie Company reported a group of NVA under observation who
        appeared to want to surrender (Chieu Hoi). Delta's White Skull
        platoon was ordered to close in and take them prisoner; the NVA had
        no intention of surrendering and ambushed the approaching soldiers,
        killing three (SP4 Willie Jones, SP4 Donald Stoltz, SSG William
        Williams — see soldiers/jones-willie-gerald, soldiers/stoltz-donald,
        soldiers/williams-william) before the ambushing NVA group was itself
        wiped out. A joint Delta/Charlie perimeter then held through a
        massive all-night mortar and ground assault — command had speculated
        the position might be the COSVN headquarters — with no further KIA
        to either company, before the site was struck by a B-52 Arclight the
        next day. See events/operation-sheridan-sabre-1968-12-04 and the
        full verbal account: colavita-henry-verbal-account-dec4-1968.

        As an Infantry captain in command, living in the field with his
        company for weeks at a time and leading roughly 100 men, carried the
        radio callsign "Angry Skipper-6" — the same D Company callsign held
        by CPT Edison Scholes (1967-68), CPT Rodney Grannemann, and CPT
        Audis Garner before him (see soldiers/garner-audis). He
        proposed full-company ambushes, setting ambush sites along trails and
        trying to find a new one every night, with resupply only once every
        few days. Per his obituary, Colavita and his soldiers "pioneered
        never-before seen company sized ambush tactics that required strict
        discipline and attention to every detail," and per his own account
        the company became "exceptionally good" at it — its main mission
        became supply interdiction: hitting enemy logistics personnel and
        finding caches. A Goodreads reviewer who read the memoir adds that he
        deliberately kept his ambush tactics from his own superiors because
        they weren't "by the book" — consistent with an account of once
        feigning radio trouble when a general ordered the company to move
        positions for the night, running a successful ambush from the
        original position instead, and never hearing anything more about it.
        After his D Company command, he was asked to develop an ambush
        seminar (unit/location/date not yet sourced).

        **The January 28, 1969 bunker-complex fight:** while on a
        search-and-destroy patrol out of LZ Rita, the company walked into an
        unmapped bunker complex and immediately returned fire; they formed a
        perimeter (moving with platoons abreast and the CP in the center —
        "circle the wagons" — rather than single-file) and Colavita called in
        Army helicopter gunship support rather than Air Force or artillery,
        judging it more accurate at close range. The company stayed in
        contact all night despite heavy air support ("Death from Above"),
        took wounded who could not be evacuated until the next day, and the
        enemy broke contact and disappeared at first light — consistent with
        this being the same action that killed SP4 Carl Pipher and CPL
        Warren Eskridge (see soldiers/pipher-carl, soldiers/eskridge-warren,
        and events/operation-sheridan-sabre-1969-01-28), previously
        undocumented beyond the bare casualty record. See the full verbal
        account: colavita-henry-verbal-account-jan28-1969.

  AMBUSH DOCTRINE / COMPANY REPUTATION: per Michael's transcription,
  Colavita proposed full-company ambushes, set sites along trails and tried
  for a new one every night, resupplied only once every few days, and his
  company became "exceptionally good" at it — the main mission shifting to
  supply interdiction (hitting logistics personnel, finding caches). This
  corroborates (with much more specificity) the obituary's "pioneered

  reviewer's note that he kept some of these tactics from his own
  superiors. Also per Michael: once feigned radio trouble when a general
  ordered the company to displace for the night, ran a successful ambush
  from the original position instead, and said he never heard anything more
  about it. After leaving D Company, he was asked to develop an ambush
  seminar — unit, location, and date not yet sourced.
