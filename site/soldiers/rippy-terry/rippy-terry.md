    body: >
      SP4 Terry Rippy was killed by an explosive device on April 22, 1966 — a hostile death,
      "died outright," recorded as a ground casualty. He had been in Vietnam about five months,
      among D Company's earliest losses of the war. His death falls just after **Operation Mosby
      I** (16–17 April 1966, a two-brigade reconnaissance-in-force in Pleiku and Kontum) — the
      operational arc that ran Jim Bowie → Lincoln → Mosby I, before Operation Crazy Horse in May.
      During Lincoln, D Company's forward position was **LZ Cotton** in the Chu Pong / border
      country — roughly 120 km from An Khe — so the battalion did not convoy home: the 1st Brigade
      was **airlifted back to An Khe by C-130 on 16–17 April**, staged through the Oasis. By the
      22nd the company had been back at **An Khe (Camp Radcliff)** for several days, which is the
      likeliest place of his death by explosive device — the An Khe TAOR was heavily mined and
      booby-trapped — with a Highway 19 incident a secondary possibility. The exact place and
      action are not yet confirmed, and his casualty province of record ("Quang Nam," I Corps) is a
      DCAS error — the 1st Cavalry was in the II Corps highlands, not the I Corps coast, in April
      1966. His name is inscribed on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Panel 6E, Line 128.
