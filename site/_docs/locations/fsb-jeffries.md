## 2. Origin of the Name

In keeping with U.S. military tradition in Vietnam, firebases were frequently
named or renamed to honor fallen comrades.

On April 24, 1971, an Army UH-1H Huey (tail 69-15692) belonging to A Company,
229th Assault Helicopter Battalion, 1st Cavalry Division, suffered a catastrophic
engine failure immediately upon takeoff from an existing firebase. The crash
killed the aircraft commander, CPT Martin Vincent Fanning, and his co-pilot,
WO1 Gabriel Augustus Jeffries Jr.

Shortly thereafter, the 3rd Brigade dismantled a nearby cramped outpost along
Highway QL-1 — originally named FSB Fanning — and moved operations to a more
modern, geographically sound location. The new installation was named Fire
Support Base Jeffries in honor of the fallen co-pilot.

**Note:** The McGrew calendar (August 1971) corroborates this sequence:
- Aug 11: "Convoy To Fanning" — unit at FSB Fanning
- Aug 13: "CA to New Loc" — combat assault to new location
- Aug 14: base crossed out / no entry — consistent with FSB Jeffries established this date

## 4. Physical Layout and Life on the Base

The 3rd Brigade constructed FSB Jeffries along a strict geometric triangle
design to maximize defensive lines of fire against potential ground overruns.
The center of the base featured an RC-292 omnidirectional antenna — the primary
communication link between base commanders and infantry platoons on long-range
jungle patrols.
