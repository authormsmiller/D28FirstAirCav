## 1. Overview

FSB Fontaine was Delta Company 2/8 Cav's primary operational base during the
spring of 1971. It was established in approximately February 1971 and remained
the unit's center of gravity through the April 24 crash and into early May.

Miller, Marvin Dale drew a map of Fontaine for a colonel — referenced in a
letter dated approximately February 1971 as "the new firebase." This places him
at Fontaine during or shortly after its establishment, and establishes that he
knew the base well enough to sketch it from direct knowledge.

## 3. Critical Note on Grid Zone — Fontaine vs. Fanning

Fontaine is grid zone **YS**. FSB Fanning is grid zone **YT** (YT 525 155).
These are not neighboring coordinates — they are in different geographic sectors.

- **Fontaine (YS 804 953):** ~15 miles southeast of Xuân Lộc, near Bình Tuy border
- **Fanning (YT 525 155):** Northeast of Xuân Lộc, near QL-1/QL-56 intersection

This strongly suggests that FSB Fontaine and FSB Fanning were **distinct
positions**, not the same base renamed. The existing open question in fsb-fanning.md
("rename vs. new position") is likely answerable once these coordinates are
verified against operational maps: if both coordinates hold, the rename theory
is effectively ruled out.

⚠️ *Coordinate verification remains a prerequisite before treating this as
resolved. If the YS coordinate for Fontaine is wrong, the discrepancy question
reopens.*

Additional corroboration: Miller, Marvin Dale drew a map of Fontaine for a
colonel in approximately February 1971, then returned from Relay Mountain to
the unit at Fontaine in mid-April 1971. McGrew's calendar shows "CA to Fontaine"
on May 2, 1971 — eight days after the crash. The unit's presence at Fontaine
before and after the crash, with Fanning appearing only in May, is consistent
with two separate bases.

## 6. Physical Layout

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced — partially corroborated by D Co. veteran accounts (Garvin
and others cited by Gemini as sources, but specific account documents not yet
identified in this archive).*

**Artillery:** Full battery of six 105mm howitzers. Veteran records indicate
the crews could reliably deliver defensive fire within five minutes of an
infantry call for emergency cover.

**Ammunition storage:** Heavily sandbagged powder charge pits; deep central
ammunition bunker engineered to withstand direct mortar hits.

**Access road:** Unlike other area FSBs, Fontaine could be reached by ground
vehicle — convoys turned off QL-1 at a small rural village onto an unpaved
dirt road to the base.

**Morning mine sweeps:** A squad from Delta Company conducted a foot sweep of
the access road every morning before convoys moved. VC sappers regularly
targeted the road overnight.

**The laundry incident:** Veteran accounts describe one convoy mine strike that
destroyed a trailer of fresh army fatigues — laundry scattered across the brush,
no casualties. *(Anecdotal — source not yet identified in archive.)*

## 7. Tactical Environment

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced.*

Fontaine sat directly in the path of NVA/VC logistics lines running out of the
May Tao Secret Zone. Infantry platoons operating around the base regularly
uncovered sophisticated enemy bunker complexes under jungle canopy. As U.S.
forces drew down, contacts became more frequent and closer — sudden ambushes
at close quarters were common in the dense brush around the perimeter.

## 8. The Map

Miller, Marvin Dale drew a map of FSB Fontaine for a colonel, referenced in
approximately his February 1971 letter. The map itself may have been enclosed
with the letter or retained separately. This artifact — if it survives as a
scan or physical document — would be among the most significant items for this
location page: a hand-drawn primary source of the base layout by a soldier
who was there.
