## 1. Names and Aliases

This location appears under multiple names across primary and secondary sources.
All of the following refer to the same geographic feature:

| Name | Source / Context |
|---|---|
| **Relay Mountain** | Soldier nickname — used by D Co. veterans including Miller, Marvin Dale (primary) |
| **The Rock** | Soldier nickname — anecdotal, secondary sources |
| **Núi Chứa Chan** | Vietnamese name; appears on military and civilian maps |
| **Hill 837** | U.S. military grid designation — summit elevation 837 meters |
| **Gia Ray Mountain** | Name used by CPT Neal in his account of 4/20/71 (primary) |
| **Chứa Chan** | Modern name — now a tourism and recreation area |

The name "Relay Mountain" reflects the installation's communications function
(see Section 3). "Gia Ray" likely derives from the nearby town of Gia Ray in
Long Khánh Province. CPT Neal's use of "Gia Ray Mountain" in a primary document
dated April 20, 1971 corroborates the Wikipedia-listed alias independently.

## 2. Geographic Location

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Summit elevation | 837 meters (2,740 ft) |
| Province | Long Khánh Province, Military Region 3 (III Corps) |
| General position | Northeast of Xuân Lộc; northwest of the town of Gia Ray |
| Relationship to FSB Jeffries | FSB Jeffries was positioned on higher ground toward the base of this mountain |
| MGRS | Not yet confirmed |
| Coordinates | Not yet confirmed |

The mountain is described by veterans and secondary sources as a massive,
solitary granite monolith rising sharply from an otherwise flat plain. From the
summit, visibility is extraordinary: Miller, Marvin Dale observed the former
location of FSB Silver (already dismantled by his garrison rotation) and could
see as far as the South China Sea.

**Line of sight to FSB Silver:** Gemini-sourced coordinates place FSB Silver at
MGRS YT 829 043, approximately 18–20 miles east-southeast of the summit, in
gently rolling lowland brush near the Long Khánh / Bình Tuy provincial border.
The surrounding terrain is flat, leaving the sightline unobstructed.
⚠️ *YT 829 043 is Gemini-sourced — verify against operational maps or aviation
logs before treating as confirmed.*

**Line of sight to the South China Sea:** The coastline near Vũng Tàu and Bình
Tuy Province sits approximately 35–40 miles south-southeast of the summit. The
theoretical horizon from 2,700 feet of elevation is roughly 63 miles (standard
atmospheric geometry: 1.22 × √2700 ft ≈ 63 miles) — the coast falls well
within that range. No higher terrain intervenes between the mountain and the
sea. This aspect of dad's observation is geometrically sound and does not depend
on Gemini sourcing.

## 3. Role and Function

Relay Mountain was not a firebase. It was a permanent, heavily fortified signal
and communications relay installation, operated primarily by the 53rd Signal
Battalion.

**Why the location mattered:** VHF tactical radios used by infantry platoons are
line-of-sight and cannot penetrate dense jungle canopy or terrain. The summit of
Chứa Chan was the highest ground in Military Region 3 and provided unobstructed
relay coverage across Long Khánh Province and beyond. Infantry units on long-
range patrols — including Delta 2/8 Angry Skipper — bounced radio transmissions
up to the summit relay detachment, which forwarded them back to tactical
operations centers on the ground.

⚠️ *The following operational details are Gemini-sourced and have not been
verified against primary sources. Treat as plausible but unconfirmed.*

- Summit accessible only by helicopter via a narrow landing pad cut from the peak
- Cleared 50 meters down each slope; ringed with concertina wire, trip-flares,
  machine-gun bunkers, and mortar positions
- Radio masts and tropospheric scatter antennas operated continuously
- Living conditions markedly different from lowland FSBs: frequent cloud cover,
  wind, cold nights, and constant generator noise
- The installation was a high-priority enemy target due to its communications role

*Source citation "104 Sig Sqn" appears in Gemini output — possible unit or
document reference, not yet traced.*

## 4. Delta Company / Angry Skipper Connection

D Co., 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry ("Angry Skipper") was regularly called on to
provide security garrison at Relay Mountain. This was a recurring operational
assignment, not an incidental visit.

**Miller, Marvin Dale** was on garrison duty at Relay Mountain in early 1971.
Based on his letters, this rotation occurred sometime in the March–April 1971
window. He returned to the unit at FSB Fontaine in mid-April 1971, where he
encountered CPT Neal (newly arrived). The garrison rotation at Relay Mountain
therefore predates April 14–18, 1971 (approximate return date).

**McDonald, Norm ("Range")** was wounded by shrapnel at Relay Mountain.
Date and circumstances not yet confirmed. McDonald's calendar is a primary source
for FSB Donna; his WIA at Relay Mountain is anecdotally reported.

⚠️ *McDonald WIA — needs verification against military casualty records.*

## 6. Relationship to Other Locations

- **FSB Fontaine** — Dad returned from Relay Mountain garrison duty to Fontaine
  in mid-April 1971; the April 24 crash occurred approximately one week later.
- **FSB Jeffries** — Positioned at the base of the mountain; Relay Mountain
  served as the communications backbone for Jeffries (and likely Fanning before it).
- **FSB Silver** — Visible from the summit during dad's rotation; already
  dismantled by that point. Gemini places Silver at YT 829 043, ~18–20 miles
  east-southeast. If the coordinate holds, the sightline claim is geometrically
  confirmed. Silver predates the garrison rotation (est. March–April 1971).
