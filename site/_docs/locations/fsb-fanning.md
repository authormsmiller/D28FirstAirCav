## 1. The Namesake: CPT Martin V. Fanning

FSB Fanning was named in honor of CPT Martin Vincent Fanning, aircraft
commander of A Company, 229th Assault Helicopter Battalion, 1st Cavalry
Division.

On April 24, 1971, UH-1H tail number 69-15354 (A Co., 229th) suffered total
engine failure immediately upon clearing the tree line during a takeoff in Long
Khanh Province. The crash killed CPT Fanning and his co-pilot, WO1 Gabriel
Augustus Jeffries Jr. (See also FSB Jeffries.)

When the 3rd Brigade (Separate) established a new artillery position in the
immediate weeks following the crash, they named it Fire Support Base Fanning to
honor the fallen pilot.

**⚠️ Discrepancy note:** The existing fanning-martin.md timeline entry states
"FSB Fontaine was subsequently renamed FSB Fanning." The source material here
describes FSB Fanning as a *new position established* in Fanning's honor, not a
renaming of FSB Fontaine. These may describe the same event from different
angles (Fontaine decommissioned and replaced by Fanning), or they may be
genuinely different bases. Needs verification against operational maps.

## 2. McGrew Calendar Corroboration (Primary Source)

McGrew's personal field calendar (D Co., 2nd Bn., 8th Cav — "Angry Skipper")
provides primary-source confirmation of FSB Fanning's existence and active
dates. Key entries:

| Date | Entry | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| May 2, 1971 | "CA to Fontaine" | Unit at FSB Fontaine — 8 days after crash |
| May 7, 1971 | "Returned to Fanning" | **Earliest FSB Fanning entry — base already named by May 7** |
| May 13, 1971 | "Booked to Fanning" | Unit operating from FSB Fanning |
| June 2, 1971 | "CA to Fanning Mission" | Continued presence |
| June 7, 1971 | "Fanning" | At base |
| June 21, 1971 | "CA to Fanning and to Vung Tau" | |
| June 24, 1971 | "Back to Fanning CA to Mission" | |
| July 7, 1971 | "Back to Fanning" | |
| July 10, 1971 | "Convoy to Fanning" | |
| July 20, 1971 | "CA to Fanning / Truck to Old ARVN Base" | |
| July 22, 1971 | "Truck to Fanning from Cabin" | |
| Aug 11, 1971 | "Convoy To Fanning" | **Last FSB Fanning entry in calendar** |
| Aug 13, 1971 | "CA to New Loc" | Move to FSB Jeffries begins |
| Aug 14, 1971 | (crossed out — no entry) | FSB Jeffries established |

**McGrew-documented window: May 7 – August 11, 1971**

May 7 reads "Returned to Fanning" — implying at least one prior visit, so the
base was already operational before that date. Given the crash was April 24 and
the 3rd Brigade would have moved quickly to honor Fanning, establishment in
early-to-mid May 1971 is well supported. McGrew's calendar is treated as the
primary source for these dates.

Note: McGrew was on leave July 23 – late July (calendar shows "Chock to Bien
Hoa for leave" Jul 23, "To Camp Alpha / Tan Son Nhut" Jul 26, "Saigon / Home
on Leave" Jul 29), so the July 23 – Aug 10 gap in Fanning entries reflects his
absence, not a change in base.

## 3. Geographic Location and Strategic Purpose

**Source: Military Grid Reference System registries**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| MGRS | YT 525 155 |
| Latitude / Longitude | 10°59'11"N, 107°18'42"E |
| Decimal degrees (Google Maps) | 10.986389, 107.311667 |
| Province | Long Khánh Province, Military Region 3 (III Corps) |

**Modern landmark:** Just north of the intersection of Highway QL-1 and
Highway QL-56, slightly northeast of the city center of modern-day Xuân Lộc.
In 1971 this was dense brush and the outer fringes of the massive
French-established rubber plantations. FSB Fanning was established to protect
the northern approaches to the regional capital of Xuân Lộc.

**Strategic purpose (mid-1971):** Following the 1970 Cambodian Incursion, NVA
forces attempted to slip back through rubber plantations and thick jungle in
Long Khanh to stage rocket attacks against Saigon. FSB Fanning was placed
directly into this terrain as an airmobile jumping-off point, allowing infantry
companies — including Delta 2/8 "Angry Skipper" — to conduct daytime clearing
patrols and night ambushes under the base's artillery umbrella.

## 4. Structural Setup and Life on the Base

Standard 1st Cavalry Division late-war outpost configuration:

**Weapons:** Split battery of 105mm towed howitzers (typically 2–4 pieces),
arranged in tight perimeter for 360-degree fire.

**Infrastructure:** Conex containers (steel shipping boxes) buried in dirt and
reinforced with sandbags, serving as the TOC, ammunition bunkers, and sleeping
quarters. Designed to withstand NVA 82mm mortar and rocket counter-battery fire.

**Logistics:** Entirely air-supplied — water, C-Rations, and ammunition flown
in by CH-47 Chinooks and Huey supply ships via the central LZ.

## 5. Transition to FSB Jeffries

By August 1971, the tactical footprint of FSB Fanning had become redundant and
overly exposed to enemy surveillance from surrounding high ground. On August 14,
1971, the 3rd Brigade officially relocated assets to a more secure position
nearby, naming the new installation **Fire Support Base Jeffries** — ensuring
the names of both pilots who died in the same cockpit on April 24 remained tied
together on the maps of Long Khanh Province until the unit stood down.
