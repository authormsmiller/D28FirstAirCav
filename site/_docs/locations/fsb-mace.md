## 1. Overview

FSB Mace was the semi-permanent, battalion-level anchor base for 1st Cavalry
Division operations in Long Khánh Province. Unlike the temporary satellite
positions (Silver, Fanning, Jeffries) that opened and closed as the tactical
situation shifted, Mace was a large, heavily fortified installation that remained
the fixed point on the map throughout the 1970–71 drawdown period.

Originally built by the 199th Light Infantry Brigade, Mace passed to 1st Cavalry
Division operational control in September 1970 — approximately two months before
Miller, Marvin Dale arrived in-country.

## 3. Geographic Relationships

Mace is the southernmost of the Long Khánh cluster and the base from which the
other positions in this area radiated:

| Location | MGRS | Relationship to Mace |
|---|---|---|
| FSB Mace | YT 570 085 | — baseline anchor, southern foot of Relay Mountain |
| FSB Fanning | YT 525 155 | ~4 miles northwest, along QL-1 corridor |
| FSB Jeffries | YT 545 225 | ~4 miles north, closer to mountain base |
| Relay Mountain (summit) | unconfirmed | Directly above — signal station looked straight down into Mace |
| FSB Silver | YT 829 043 *(unverified)* | ~18 miles east-southeast |

**The operational triangle:** Mace (base of mountain, heavy artillery anchor),
Jeffries (approach to mountain, infantry staging), Relay Mountain (summit,
communications relay). The signal detachment on the peak had direct line of sight
into Mace's layout. Soldiers on guard at Mace looked up at the silhouette of the
relay station every day.

## 4. Strategic Role

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced — plausible, unverified.*

Mace served two overlapping functions:

**Heavy artillery platform:** Housed batteries from the 1st Cav providing
continuous fire support for infantry companies — including Delta 2/8 "Angry
Skipper" — operating in surrounding rubber plantations and jungle toward the
May Tao Secret Zone.

**Staging and extraction hub:** As smaller FSBs opened and closed around it,
Mace was the fixed point where troops were shuffled, rearmed, and extracted.
When Silver was dismantled and operations shifted to Fanning, then Jeffries,
Mace remained the constant.

## 5. The "Red Road" Convoys

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced — cites "daily logs from the 720th Military Police Battalion."
This is a specific, named unit with records — researchable at NARA.*

Because of its scale, Mace could not be sustained by helicopter resupply alone.
Daily ground convoys ran from Long Binh along Route QL-1 — nicknamed the
"Red Road" for its choking red clay dust and its danger rating.

The 720th Military Police Battalion escorted the convoys. The road was heavily
contested: VC sappers mined it regularly, and ambushes (small arms and rockets)
along the highway were common. The convoy escort mission was a grinding,
daily operational constant for the MP battalion.
