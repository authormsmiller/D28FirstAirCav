## 1. Overview

Vũng Tàu was the primary in-country Rest and Recuperation (R&R) destination
for U.S. and allied combat troops throughout the Vietnam War. A French-influenced
coastal resort city on a peninsula approximately 60 miles southeast of Saigon,
it offered a jarring contrast to the operational reality of Long Khánh Province:
real beds, hot showers, beaches, cold beer, and several days without a weapon
in hand.

For D Co. 2/8 Cav soldiers, Vũng Tàu was the closest thing to a pause in the
war. It sits just over the horizon from Relay Mountain — your dad, standing at
the summit in spring 1971, was looking directly toward it when he saw the South
China Sea.

## 2. Geography

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Location | Peninsula, ~60 miles southeast of Saigon |
| Province | Phước Tuy Province (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu today) |
| Character | Coastal resort city; French colonial architecture; beaches |

The city was considered among the safest in South Vietnam due to its peninsula
geography and heavy allied military presence.

⚠️ *The following detail is Gemini-sourced and unverified:* Vũng Tàu was
reportedly treated as an informal "open city" by both sides — neither U.S. forces
nor the VC wanted combat drawn into the resort zone. VC operatives reportedly
used the city covertly alongside American GIs under an unwritten non-engagement
understanding.

## 3. The 1970–1971 Status Shift

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced — specific enough to pursue, not independently verified.*

**Before September 1970:** Vũng Tàu operated as an open R&R city. American
soldiers could move freely in civilian clothes and stay in local hotels.

**September 1970:** Military command officially changed Vũng Tàu's status to
a "closed city" for U.S. personnel, citing the acceleration of Vietnamization
and an increase in black-market activity.

**Dad's window (December 1970 onward):** He arrived after the closed-city order.
His experience of Vũng Tàu — if and when he went — would have been the
restricted version: authorized R&R passes, MP patrols enforcing uniform codes,
no civilian hotels.

---

## 4. The R&R Experience

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced.*

Soldiers flew directly from forward operating bases to Vũng Tàu Army Airfield.
Standard R&R rotation: 3–5 days.

- **R&R Center:** Run by the U.S. Army's 1st Logistical Command. Real beds,
  hot showers, air-conditioned mess halls, steak, ice cream, cold beer.
- **Back Beach:** Swimming, sunbathing, outdoor beach clubs including the
  Beachcomber.
- **Bar Street (Phan Thanh Giản):** Bars, music, neon — the off-duty strip.

## 5. The Strip — Bar Names and Landmarks

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced. This list is intended as a memory aid and photo
identification tool — not a verified historical registry. Names may be
approximate or misremembered across sources.*

**Phan Thanh Giản Street ("The Strip")** was the main commercial bar district
downtown. Narrow storefronts with steel sapper screens, tiled facades, and
jukeboxes. Common visual: neon signs, open doorways, groups of GIs in fatigues.

**Downtown / classic town bars:**

Tommy's Bar · The New York Bar · The Texas Bar · The Lucky Strike Bar ·
The Playboy Bar · The Miami Bar · The San Francisco Bar · The California Bar ·
The Tiger Bar · The Chicago Bar · The Soul Bar · The Rex Bar · The Blue Star Bar ·
The Top Hat Bar

*The Soul Bar was noted as heavily frequented by Black service members and
featured Motown, Stax, and R&B on the jukebox. The Tiger Bar commonly featured
hand-painted Tiger Beer murals.*

**Coastal and Aussie-frequented spots:**

The Beachcomber · The Pacific Bar · The Kangaroo Bar · The Oasis Bar ·
The Splendid Bar

*The Beachcomber was a large open-air pavilion on Back Beach, operated by
1st Logistical Command. The Kangaroo Bar was a noted meeting point for
Australian diggers and American GIs.*

### Memory Prompts for Photo Identification

When showing veterans photos of Vũng Tàu — especially doorway or street shots
where the bar name isn't visible — these sensory details may help place the
image:

**Music:** Creedence Clearwater Revival ("Run Through the Jungle," "Have You
Ever Seen the Rain"), Jimi Hendrix, Three Dog Night. Almost universally
remembered from the jukeboxes.

**Drinks:** Ba Mười Ba (33 Beer — "thirty-three," distinct skunky taste),
San Miguel, Tiger Beer. Typically served in glasses with chunks of block ice
cracked on the sidewalk.

**The smell:** Cheap perfume, stale beer, red clay dust, and nước mắm
(fish sauce) from nearby street food. Veterans consistently cite this specific
combination as immediately evocative.

## 6. The Australian Connection

⚠️ *Gemini-sourced. The 1st Australian Task Force (1ATF) presence at Vũng Tàu
is historically documented and not controversial — the detail below about
informal fraternization is anecdotal.*

Vũng Tàu was the main logistics and headquarters base for the 1st Australian
Task Force. American GIs and Australian/New Zealand soldiers shared the city's
rest facilities. Veteran accounts describe informal socializing between the
allied forces — dayrooms, bars, trading stories.

## 3rd Brigade (Separate) R&R rotation — 1971 (primary source)

The 1971 brigade commander's debriefing report puts a primary-source date and structure on
Vũng Tàu's role for D Co's battalion. In his Senior Officer Debriefing Report (period 10 April –
13 December 1971; DTIC AD0520447), BG Jonathan R. Burton records that the **First Team R&R
Center opened at Vũng Tàu in March 1971**, and that on a **45-day rotation each rifle company and
recon platoon was sent there for a three-day standdown** — fitted between roughly fifteen days
in the bush and five days of firebase security. Burton calls "Vung Tau R&R" a privilege "much
sought after by all Skytroopers" and "a major positive morale factor," giving troops "an
excellent opportunity to wind down after six weeks in the field." Operating the center required
26 people. This corroborates and dates the (Gemini/McGrew-sourced) context above with a primary
unit record.

See `/events/3rd-brigade-separate-garryowen-1971/` (which hosts the report for download).

**Source:** 3rd Brigade (Separate), 1st Cavalry Division — Senior Officer Debriefing Report of
BG Jonathan R. Burton, "Personnel Management" (DTIC AD0520447).
