## The observation

Across the entire D Co. KIA list (1965–1972, 107 dated entries), **May 1969 is the
second-deadliest month** — six KIA — behind only March 1967 (seven). The May 1972 Chinook
crash (21) is a single mass-casualty event and is set aside as an anomaly, not sustained
combat.

What makes May 1969 stand out further is the run-up: **March 1969 = 0, April 1969 = 0, then
May 1969 = 6**, with one more on June 12. The company went from a quiet spring to its worst
month in two years.

## Discipline — what we are NOT concluding yet

These are **separate dates and almost certainly separate actions**. Same company and the same
general area of operations (the 1st Cav's 1969 Tay Ninh / War Zone C campaign) make a shared
*operational* context plausible, but that is **not** evidence of a single battle, and the
records of record carry at least one province discrepancy (May 25 = "Hau Nghia," which
conflicts with the unit's documented Tay Ninh AO). Do **not**:
- merge these into one event page;
- build a "May 1969 cluster" narrative tying the deaths to a single action;
- assume Neil Brown's or Marchand's province without confirmation.

The honest current statement is: *D Company suffered an unusually heavy run of hostile losses
in May 1969, at least two of them (May 12–13) in Tay Ninh Province and plausibly within one
operation; the operational picture should be established from unit records before any
connection is drawn.*

## What the 1st Cav Division ORLL (AD0506273) establishes

Read 2026-06-15 from the report Michael supplied (1st Cav Div Operational Report - Lessons
Learned, period ending 31 July 1969; 73 pp., declassified). It **confirms the operational frame
and the intelligence-driven character** we hypothesized — while staying at division level (no
D Co / platoon detail, no named men, no description of the specific May 2 / 12-13 / 25 actions).

Confirmed from the report:

- **2/8 Cav was in the 1st Brigade** this quarter (Task Organization, TAB A: 1st Bde = 2-7,
  **2-8**, 1-12 Cav). The 1st Brigade's AO was **central/southern War Zone C, Tay Ninh
  Province** — operating from LZs Grant, Carolyn, St Barbara, Ike, Jamie, and White against
  enemy lines of communication and **Base Area 355** / the Saigon River approaches.
  (Division-wide AO this quarter: Tay Ninh, Binh Long, Binh Duong, Phuoc Long.) This supports
  the theory that the May cluster sits in the Tay Ninh AO and **sharpens the May 25 "Hau Nghia
  vs. Tay Ninh" question** — 1st Bde / 2-8 Cav was operating in Tay Ninh, not Hau Nghia.
- **Which operations 2/8 Cav (1st Brigade) was under — careful reading:** the division ran
  several concurrent operations by different brigades, all under Toan Thang III. The ones in
  **War Zone C, where 1st Brigade (2/8 Cav) operated**, were:
  - **Montana Raider** — a three-brigade effort (+ 11th ACR), **13 Apr - 13 May 1969**,
    throughout War Zone C; 1st Brigade worked AO RED / "the Crescent" against the 95C Regiment,
    1st NVA Division, exploiting massed B-52 strikes. Brackets the **May 2 and May 12-13** window.
  - **Montana Scout** — 1st & 2nd Brigades, **1 Apr - 23 Jun 1969**, along the southern frontier
    of War Zone C (interdiction; small-unit patrols and saturation ambushes vs Base Area 355).
    The ongoing 1st-Brigade operation; since Montana Raider ended 13 May, **May 25 most likely
    falls here.**
  - **Comanche Warrior** (14 May - 1 Jun) and **Creek II** (19 May - 22 Jun) were **3rd Brigade**
    operations in **War Zone D** (Long Khanh / Bien Hoa, against the 5th VC Division) — a
    different AO; **2/8 Cav was not in them.** (This corrects an earlier read that placed May 25
    under Comanche Warrior.)
  - Kentucky Cougar (from 24 Jun) — 1st Brigade continued, shifting north toward Phuoc Long.
  Bottom line (highly likely, not certain): the May cluster sits in **1st Brigade's War Zone C
  operations — Montana Raider through ~13 May, then Montana Scout** — consistent with the Tay
  Ninh province of record for George Brown and Dunkle. Which specific operation/day each death
  belongs to still needs the battalion journal.
- **Intelligence-driven operations — the hypothesis, in the report's own words:** "As visual
  reconnaissance and usually reliable intelligence disclosed enemy concentrations in lower Base
  Area 355, the 1st Brigade initiated a two-battalion seal-search-and-destroy operation with
  the **2/8 Cav**, 2/12 Cav, and one tank company..."; the brigade repeatedly "reorient[ed] its
  forces" "responding to intelligence and ground contacts"; with "maximum emphasis ... upon
  **ambush operations**" and "**saturation ambushes** to interdict enemy movement into Base Area
  355." The division also ran an active **Chieu Hoi / Hoi Chanh** program (127 ralliers this
  quarter) — the prisoner/rallier-derived intel mechanism described in the hypothesis.
- 2/8 Cav-specific: the report credits **the 2/8 Cav with establishing LZ Becky**.

**What the ORLL does NOT give** (still required): the specific May 2 / 12-13 / 25 actions, which
D Co platoon, grids, and any documented action-to-action causation. That lives in the **2/8 Cav
daily staff journal / AAR (NARA RG 472)** — Section 2 below. Until those show one action led to
the next, links stay **`operational-period`** (now well-supported: same campaign, same
intel-driven Tay Ninh AO), with the refinement that the May cluster falls under **1st
Brigade's War Zone C operations** (Montana Raider through ~13 May, then Montana Scout), **not**
the 3rd-Brigade Comanche Warrior / Creek II.

## Modeling approach — a chain of linked events (the April 1971 Fontaine model)

When we build this out, treat May 1969 the way the **April 1971 FSB Fontaine series** was
handled: **separate, self-contained event pages linked as a chain — NOT one merged narrative.**

Working hypothesis (to be confirmed from records, not assumed): the May deaths are the product
of an **intelligence-driven sequence** — a captured prisoner, a Chieu Hoi rallier, or an agent
report points to an enemy location; the company air-assaults or patrols there; a contact or
ambush results; that action in turn surfaces the next objective. A chain of cause and effect,
not a single battle.

How the repo already chains events (the `related_events:` front-matter array of
`{ slug, relationship, note }`). Observed relationship vocabulary, from the Fontaine series:
- `causal` — one event directly precipitated another (the 4/20 contact -> the 4/24 crash,
  "occurred in the context of post-contact operations").
- `operational-period` — same operation / AO / window, **no causal claim** (the May 1971 Chieu
  Hoi escort -> the 4/20 contact: "No causal relationship - loose operational linking only").
- `commemorative` — a later memorial tied to an event.

Candidate May 1969 pages (only once records justify them):
- a **May 2** action (Neil Brown) — hostile;
- a **May 12-13** action in Tay Ninh (George Brown, Dunkle) — probably a single fight;
- the **May 25** command-detonated ambush (Karr/Garven/White) — already drafted as
  `trail-ambush-hau-nghia-1969-05-25`, which becomes the chain's existing anchor.

**Linking discipline:** until the ORLL/AAR actually shows an intel-driven sequence, link these
with `operational-period` (the honest "same campaign, same AO" claim), **not** `causal`.
Upgrade a specific link to `causal` only when a record documents that one action led to the
next. This keeps the chain faithful to the Fontaine precedent, where causal vs. loose linking
is stated explicitly for each pair — and keeps us from drawing the straight line before the
records support it.

## What each record can give — and the chain we probably won't get

**Veteran-facing principle first:** the public pages **never lead with operation names.** A
trooper remembers the trail, the cache, the day a friend was killed — not "Operation Montana
Scout." Operation pages are background scaffolding; the soldier profiles and the tactical-event
contribution prompts stay anchored on the men, the place, and the day (the low-barrier
invitation used on the May 25 event).

Record hierarchy — from campaign frame, to the chain, to human truth:
- **Division ORLL (have it — AD0506273):** the campaign frame only. A quarterly summary
  ("usually reliable intelligence disclosed enemy concentrations ... the brigade initiated a
  seal-search-and-destroy"). It never names D Co or a specific day.
- **2/8 Cav daily staff journal / duty officer's log (NARA RG 472 — not yet obtained):** the one
  record where the chain might actually live. Time-stamped running entries (PW report ->
  patrol dispatched -> cache found -> next-day contact -> casualties). It will not editorialize
  causation, but consecutive entries can let the chain be reconstructed. Terse and sometimes
  incomplete — but this is where to look.
- **First-person accounts (the contribution prompts):** the human truth the records can't give.

**The "smoking gun" we want, and probably won't get in full:** a cleanly documented chain — a
captured VC or a Chieu Hoi rallier names a trail; D Co / 2-8 is sent; they find the trail and a
cache; a follow-on patrol makes contact; KIA result. The ORLL confirms this *kind* of
intelligence-driven sequence was the brigade's routine that quarter (and that 127 ralliers came
in), but it does not chain a specific capture to a specific D Co contact. The daily staff journal
is the best — and probably only — shot at the specific links; expect partial, not perfect.
