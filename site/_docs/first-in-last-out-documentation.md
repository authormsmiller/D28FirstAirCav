# "First In, Last Out" — the documentary record for 2/8 Cav

**Purpose:** back the 1st Cavalry Division's "first in, last out" claim — and D Company, 2/8
Cav's place in it — with the actual Army records, not just the association narrative. Supports
the unit-history section "The First Team Goes Home — The Brigade Stays."
**Created:** 2026-06-16.

The claim, stated plainly and accurately: the 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile) was the **first
full U.S. Army division committed to Vietnam (1965)**, and a brigade carrying its colors — the
**3rd Brigade (Separate)** — was among the **last U.S. Army ground combat units to leave (1972)**.
**2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry sits at both ends of that span:** in country by September 1965 and
inactivated 28 June 1972. So D Company's record runs the full length of the division's war.

---

## FIRST IN — deployment, 1965

**What happened.** The **1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile)** was activated **1 July 1965** at Fort
Benning, Georgia, formed from the assets of the **11th Air Assault Division (Test)** and elements
of the **2nd Infantry Division** (the 1st Cavalry Division colors were brought from Korea; the
2nd Infantry Division colors went the other way). Reorganized and equipped as the Army's first
airmobile division — roughly **428 organic helicopters** — it deployed by sea within weeks. Its
**2nd Brigade landed at Qui Nhon on 14 September 1965**; the division established its base at
**An Khe (Camp Radcliff)** in the Central Highlands. It was the **first fully committed U.S.
division of the war**, and the **first U.S. division to earn the Presidential Unit Citation** —
for the **Pleiku Campaign** (Oct–Nov 1965), its first major action.

**Where 2/8 Cav fits.** Per its lineage, the **2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** had been assigned to
the 1st Cavalry Division on **1 September 1963** (the 8th Cavalry had been a 1st Cav Division
regiment since 13 September 1921). It deployed with the division and was in country by
**September 1965** — and it fought in the Pleiku Campaign that earned the division its PUC
(see the Operation All the Way / Pleiku pages and the casualty cluster of 4 November 1965).

**Documentation that shows it:**
- **CMH, *Airmobility 1961–1971*** (LTG John J. Tolson; U.S. Army Center of Military History),
  Chapter IV, "The First Airmobile Division and the Buildup, 1965" — the official Army account of
  the activation and deployment.
- **1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile), *Interim Report of Operations, 1 July 1965 – 31 December
  1966*** — the division's own primary after-action report covering the deployment and first year.
- **Lineage and Honors, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** (U.S. Army Center of Military History) — the
  constitution/assignment chain (1866 → 1st Cav Div 1921 → 2/8 Cav assigned 1 Sept 1963 → Vietnam).
- **"The Pleiku Campaign,"** HQ 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile) after-action study — **DTIC
  AD0855112** (already held in this archive; basis for the Operation All the Way page).
- **DA General Orders** establishing the 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile), 1 July 1965, and the
  **Presidential Unit Citation** for the Pleiku Campaign — the formal instruments (exact GO numbers
  to pull; see Open items).

## LAST OUT — redeployment, 1971–72

**What happened.** As the U.S. drew down, the **1st Cavalry Division colors redeployed to Fort
Hood, Texas in 1971** (where the division reorganized as a TRICAP — "Triple Capability" —
division). To continue operations in country, the **3rd Brigade, 1st Cavalry Division (Separate)**
— the **"Garryowen Task Force"** — was **organized and activated 30 April 1971**. It remained as
one of the **last two major U.S. Army ground combat units in Vietnam**. The brigade **stood down
in a ceremony on 20 June 1972** (attended by COMUSMACV General Creighton Abrams) and its colors
departed for Fort Hood, arriving **29 June 1972**; **1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry (Task Force Garry
Owen)** stayed on until roughly **August 1972**.

**Where 2/8 Cav fits.** 2/8 Cav was one of the 3rd Brigade (Separate)'s maneuver battalions in
this final period. The battalion **left Vietnam in 1971 and was inactivated 28 June 1972** — i.e.
2/8 Cav was present to the very end of the brigade's tour. This is why D Company kept taking losses
in 1971–72 after the division proper had gone home: the **FSB Fontaine actions (April 1971)**, the
**Núi Bà contact (October 1971)**, and the **Chinook crash of 10 May 1972** — D Company, 2/8 Cav's
single costliest day — all fall inside the Garryowen period, just weeks before stand-down.

**Documentation that shows it:**
- **3rd Brigade, 1st Cavalry Division (Separate) — Operational Reports / Lessons Learned (ORLLs),
  1971–72** — the brigade's own quarterly reports (DTIC and the Texas Tech Vietnam Center &
  Archive).
- **Lineage and Honors, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** — records the battalion's 1971 departure and
  **inactivation 28 June 1972**.
- **DA General Orders** organizing the 3rd Brigade (Separate), 1st Cavalry Division (30 April
  1971) and inactivating its elements in June 1972 — the formal instruments (exact GO numbers to
  pull; see Open items).
- Stand-down of 20 June 1972 (Gen. Abrams) — documented in unit and press records of the brigade's
  departure.

## The through-line for D Company

Put the two ends together and D Company, 2/8 Cav embodies the whole claim: **in country with the
first full division in September 1965** (Pleiku, Hill 732 — Coffey, Hamill, Hill) and **still in
the field with the last brigade out in 1972** (the Chinook crash, 10 May 1972), the battalion
inactivating 28 June 1972. Few units bracket the American ground war as cleanly.
