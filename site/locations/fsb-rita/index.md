## Overview

**FSB Rita** was a fire support base near the **Cambodian border in the Fishhook**, northwest of An Loc, built to interdict North Vietnamese supply routes running from Cambodia toward Saigon. It was established by the **1st Infantry Division** in October 1968 during **Operation Toan Thang II**, and is best known for the fierce battle fought there on **1 November 1968**. Six days later, on **7 November 1968**, the base passed to the **1st Cavalry Division's 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** as **Operation Sheridan Sabre** opened, and 2/8 Cav held Rita through late January 1969.

For the d281 archive, Rita matters as the firebase from which **D Company, 2/8 Cav** operated in the winter of 1968–69 — the period in which **[SP4 Carl Pipher](/soldiers/pipher-carl/)** and **[CPL Warren Eskridge](/soldiers/eskridge-warren/)** were killed (28 January 1969).

## The battle (1 November 1968)

In the dark early morning of **1 November 1968**, an NVA sapper battalion assaulted FSB Rita, which was then held by **1st Infantry Division** troops — two artillery batteries (**1st Battalion, 5th Field Artillery** and **Battery C, 8th Battalion, 6th Artillery**), a cavalry troop, and an infantry company. Sappers threw Bangalore torpedoes across the wire and penetrated the perimeter within the first confused minutes; the fight ran all night. Artillerymen fired **beehive and canister rounds** point-blank "right down their barrels," and an AC-47 "Puff the Magic Dragon" gunship worked the perimeter from above.

The ground commander, **Lt. Col. Charles C. Rogers** (commanding 1/5th FA), was wounded three times rallying the defense and was awarded the **Medal of Honor** — he later retired a major general and remains the highest-ranking Black soldier to receive the award. **1SG Ira Whitaker** (Battery C, 8/6th Arty) earned a **Distinguished Service Cross**. **Twelve U.S. soldiers were killed** in the fighting before the attack petered out at daylight.

## The 1st Cav takeover and Sheridan Sabre (Nov 1968 – Jan 1969)

As the 1st Cavalry Division moved into III Corps (Operation Liberty Canyon, late October 1968), **2/8 Cav occupied Rita on 7 November 1968** — the same day Operation Sheridan Sabre is recorded as beginning. As a **2nd Brigade** battalion, 2/8 Cav worked the Fishhook and the Saigon River infiltration routes; its direct-support artillery was the **1st Battalion, 77th Artillery (-)**, with **A/2/19** on Rita.

Rita stayed under fire. The 1st Cav DivArty ORLL for the period ending 31 January 1969 records the base taking **735 incoming rounds** — 671 x 82mm mortar, 52 x 107mm rockets, 2 x 122mm rockets, and 3 over-caliber "Trashcan" rockets — though it notes that **casualties and damage on Rita "remained light."** 2/8 Cav was **relieved by 2/5 Cav at 300600H January 1969**. A re-survey logs the base at **XT499802** with B/1/30th Artillery (155mm) in February 1969, and that position was **attacked again on 23 February 1969**.

## The 26 November 1968 rocket attack

Less than three weeks after **2/8 Cav** occupied Rita, **SP4 Raymond Ahern** — the RTO for the
artillery Forward Observer supporting D Company, and counted by his own company commander as one
of D Company's own men — was killed when his bunker took a direct hit from a rocket during the
night of **26 November 1968**. He had been reassigned
to his post on Rita only one day before. Per a fellow D Company soldier, Larry Hackett, who left a
tribute to Ahern years later: the company had relieved the 1st Infantry Division at Rita on 9
November, spent several days rebuilding bunkers under continuing mortar and rocket fire, swept the
jungle in a wide arc for about ten days, and had just returned to defend the perimeter when Ahern
was killed. See **[SP4 Raymond Ahern, Jr.](/soldiers/ahern-raymond/)** and
**[the event page](/events/operation-sheridan-sabre-1968-11-26/)** for the full account, sourced
from CPT Henry Colavita's memoir and Hackett's own tribute.

## The 28 January 1969 cluster

**SP4 Carl Pipher** and **CPL Warren Eskridge** (D Co, 2/8 Cav) were killed by small-arms fire on **28 January 1969**, two days before the battalion was relieved at Rita. Both 1st Cav ORLLs (Division **AD502597**, DivArty **AD502415**) place 2/8 Cav in the Rita/Fishhook AO on that date but **do not itemize the contact** — the division's significant-actions chronology runs from 24 January straight to the 30 January relief, so two KIA by small arms fell below its reporting threshold. The men were therefore likely killed on a **patrol or ground contact in the surrounding AO** rather than on the base itself; FSB Rita is recorded here as their **operating base**, framed within Sheridan Sabre, pending a lower-echelon source (NARA RG 472 — 2/8 Cav daily staff journal / S-3 AAR) that could fix the exact action.

## Location

The firebase gazetteer fixes Rita at **XT505795 (11.5741, 106.3764)** for the 7 Nov 1968 occupation, with a later survey at **XT499802 / 499803 (11.5805, 106.3710)**. The Medal of Honor record independently places the 1 Nov 1968 battle at **11°34′44″N 106°22′30″E (11.579, 106.375)** "near the Fishhook" — the same spot within roughly a kilometer, which is what confirms the two "Ritas" are one base. The position sits near the Cambodian border in the **Fishhook salient** (Binh Long border country); note that 2/8 Cav casualty records of this period list **Tay Ninh Province**, the same province-record quirk documented elsewhere in the archive.
