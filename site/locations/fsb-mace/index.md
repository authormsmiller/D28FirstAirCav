## Overview

**FSB Mace** was the large, semi-permanent **regimental and battalion anchor base** for 1st Cavalry Division operations in **Long Khánh Province** (Military Region 3, III Corps) during the 1970–71 drawdown. Unlike the temporary satellite firebases that opened and closed as the tactical situation shifted — **Silver, Fanning, Jeffries, Fontaine** — Mace stayed put: the fixed point on the map from which the other positions radiated and through which troops were staged, rearmed, and extracted. Jim Garvin, who served with **D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry ("Angry Skipper"), Range Platoon ("Range 1")**, called it "the Regimental HQ and a very large installation."

Garvin documented Mace twice: his company first flew in by **C-7 Caribou on 14 December 1970**, and he returned to it in early 1971 when **LT Druener** — the former Range Platoon leader, by then acting Executive Officer — made him **D Company mail clerk**, a thank-you for having been point man longer than any other man in the platoon. His Mace chapter runs **23 February – 15 April 1971**.

## Location

Research notes place Mace at **MGRS YT 570 085 (10.9272, 107.3527)**, about nine miles east of Xuân Lộc on the **QL-1** corridor, at the **southern foot of Núi Chứa Chan** — the granite monolith the soldiers knew as **Relay Mountain** or "Signal Mt." These coordinates are **Gemini-sourced and not yet verified** against operational maps. Garvin's photographs, however, independently corroborate the *geography*: his company-area frame shows the hooches set against the steep flank of the mountain, his "Signal Mt" frame looks down on the base from a Loach, and the **Gia Ray airstrip and village** sit just outside the wire — exactly the placement the notes describe.

## Layout

From Garvin's captions: the **D Company area** was sandbagged hooches and bunkers below the mountain. Rear-area permanent staff lived in **tents with partitions and bookshelves built from discarded ammo crates**. Crews built **overhead cover** for the sleeping quarters out of steel plates over a wooden frame, topped with several layers of sandbags and a tent — Garvin remarked that materials were hard to find and that he "didn't want to be under there at all" during incoming. Mace was **very dusty**, with no paving and a constant churn of helicopters and trucks. A **hooch maid named Lang** swept the company office and did laundry for the rear-area personnel; local Vietnamese were not allowed on base until after 8:00 AM and had to clear security before dark each night.

## What the deck shows

- **The base itself** — the company area against Relay Mountain, Mace from the air, and the overhead-cover construction.
- **Rear-area life** — Garvin as mail clerk with the day's mailbags, his ammo-crate quarters, and Lang the hooch maid.
- **A March stand-down** — field gear stored at the company AO while the company went to Vung Tau; men loading trucks at Mace bound for the Gia Ray airstrip (Doug Hilts and Joe "Meatball" Fairchild among them).
- **The mountain** — "Signal Mt" (Relay Mountain) on the bluff above Mace, shot from a Loach.

## Relay Mountain

Directly above Mace stood **Núi Chứa Chan (Hill 837)** — variously "Relay Mountain," "Signal Mt," "Gia Ray Mountain," or "The Rock" — a 1st Cav signal/communications relay installation on the summit (documented separately in the archive's research notes as **relay-mountain**). The signal detachment on the peak had direct line of sight into Mace's layout; guards at Mace looked up at the relay station's silhouette every day.
