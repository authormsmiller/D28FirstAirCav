## Overview

Before the 1st Cavalry Division fought a single action in Vietnam, it had to get there — and in
1965 that meant a sea voyage. The division's **1st Brigade**, which included the **2nd Battalion,
8th Cavalry** and so **D Company**, crossed the Pacific together aboard the troop ship **USNS
Geiger**, marching ashore at **Qui Nhon on 20 September 1965** and moving inland to the new base at
An Khe.

This matters for how the archive reads the early war. The men who made this crossing went over
**as a unit, together** — the same ship, the same month at sea. The replacements who filled the
company's ranks in the years that followed almost always arrived **individually, by air**, dropped
into an established war one or two at a time. The voyage of the Geiger is one of the few experiences
the original cohort genuinely shared, and it is worth marking as a place of its own.

## The passage

The 1st Brigade boarded in **Savannah, Georgia** in the latter half of August 1965 and sailed for
Vietnam. By a brigade veteran's account the crossing ran about **32 days**, with a roughly
twelve-hour stop at **Honolulu**, a call at **Guam**, and **one hurricane** along the way, before
the brigade disembarked at **Qui Nhon on 20 September 1965**. From the pier the troopers moved
straight inland to An Khe (Camp Radcliff) — loaded onto trucks at the dock and then onto Chinooks
for the short flight up. That last detail comes from an anonymous account of the crossing — *"we
were stacked on a truck and off loaded onto a Chinook for a quick flight to An Khe"* — and squares
with the unit record's "moved inland by air." It corroborates how the troops got from Qui Nhon to
An Khe; the source can't be tied to a particular soldier, or confirmed as D Company.

## Who was aboard

The archive can already place several D Company men on this crossing by their arrival dates. Among
them: **Roger Coffey, Wright Hamill, and Eddie Hill** — three of the company's earliest combat
deaths, killed near Hill 732 on **4 November 1965**, barely seven weeks after stepping off the
ship — along with **Paul Malec** and **David Wilson**. Their presence aboard is strong
circumstantial evidence (their service begins with this movement), not yet individually confirmed;
the soldiers list below is labeled accordingly.

If you or someone in your family crossed on the **USNS Geiger** in 1965 — or remembers the
boarding at Savannah, the stop at Honolulu, the hurricane, or coming ashore at Qui Nhon — we'd like
to hear it. A name, a photo, or a memory of those weeks at sea all help complete the record, and
can confirm who was aboard.
