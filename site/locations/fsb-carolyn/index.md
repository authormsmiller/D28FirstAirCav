## Overview

**FSB Carolyn** was a 1st Cavalry Division fire support base in **Tay Ninh Province, War Zone C** (Military Region 3, III Corps), opened by the **2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** on **20 April 1969**. It sat roughly 10km north of the "French Fort" site associated with [FSB St. Barbara](/locations/fsb-st-barbara/), matching CPT Henry Colavita's own account of D Company's move "about 10km due north" that same month. The base recurs across at least three documented occupancy cycles through March 1970, the last date any source on file places activity there.

> Built from three research passes: the division and division-artillery ORLLs already in the archive, a gazetteer/geography cross-check against already-published site pages, and a single contributed photograph — the first image of the base itself. Full research log at `site/_docs/locations/fsb-carolyn.md`.

## The 6 May 1969 battle

On the night of **6 May 1969**, **Companies C and E, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry**, defending FSB Carolyn, took a mortar and rocket attack followed by a **regimental-size North Vietnamese Army ground assault**. The division ORLL's significant-activity log describes it:

> "6 May: Companies C and E, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry defending LZ CAROLYN received a mortar and rocket attack followed by a regimental size ground attack by NVA forces. The ground assault was launched simultaneously from the southwest and north at 0200 hours; breaching a portion of the perimeter and destroying a 105mm howitzer ammunition area at 0315 hours. Fighting continued throughout the early morning, with U.S. forces utilizing all available support. Counterattacks reestablished the perimeter and the enemy force began withdrawing at 0415 hours, with contact finally breaking at 0600 hours. At first light three company sized units were air assaulted to block and interdict avenues of escape to the north and west. Final results of the NVA assault were 198 enemy KIA, 30 PW[s]... Friendly casualties were 10 US KIA and 73 US WIA."

The companion division-artillery ORLL describes the same night from the gun line's side — over 200 incoming rounds (122mm rockets, 75mm recoilless rifle, 82mm and 60mm mortars), a counter-mortar radar sited at the base itself acquiring enemy firing positions, and the ammunition storage area taking two direct hits — but gives slightly different figures: **9 US KIA, 64 US WIA**, and **101 NVA KIA**, against the main ORLL's 10 KIA / 73 WIA and 198 NVA KIA. Both describe the same single engagement; the discrepancy is treated here as a normal artifact of two different reporting chains with different cutoff times, not two different fights — both figures are quoted rather than silently reconciled to one.

The gazetteer's own entry compresses this into two words — "overrun 6May69." The ORLL narrative describes something more specific: a **partial breach, followed by a successful counterattack that reestablished the perimeter** — the base was not permanently lost that night.

This was **Companies C and E**, not D Company, so it sits slightly outside this archive's usual D-Company focus; see the admin notes above on why it's narrated here rather than split into its own event page.

## A recurring position, not a single occupation

The base opened 20 April 1969 and was still being "maintained and upgraded" alongside FSBs Ike, Grant, and St. Barbara as of the division ORLL covering through 31 July 1969 — but no source on file gives an exact date that first occupancy ended. By October 1969 it's described as being reinforced (three 105mm tubes airlifted in, secured by C Troop, 1st Squadron, 12th Cavalry, from 15 October) and then explicitly **"reopened"** on 22 October by the **ARVN 11th Airborne Battalion**, part of a Vietnamization "buddy" pairing between U.S. cavalry squadrons and ARVN Airborne battalions. That reopening confirms at least one full close/reopen cycle.

The last dated activity found anywhere is **11 March 1970**, when artillery platoons shuttled out of Carolyn to FSBs Beverly and St. Barbara — five to six weeks after Garry Bruckner's own overnight stay at the base in early February 1970, arriving to join D Company in the field. The March 1970 grid (XT271783) sits about 860m from the original 1969 gazetteer grid (XT278788) — read here as re-survey drift on the same recurring position, not two different bases.

## Bruckner's account, February 1970

**[Garry Bruckner](/soldiers/bruckner-garry/)**, newly arrived in D Company, spent a night at FSB Carolyn in early February 1970 — diverted here after the company he'd been sent to find had "just moved to Tay Ninh." He recalled the artillery running fire missions through the night before he joined the company in the field by log bird the next day, working alongside the **11th Armored Cavalry Regiment** near the Cambodian border. That 11th ACR detail is independently corroborated: the already-published [Dog's Head contact](/events/contact-dogs-head-1970-03-18/), six weeks later and in the same corridor, confirms Alpha Troop, 1st Squadron, 11th ACR operating alongside 2/8 Cav in this exact AO that spring. Full account: `documents/bruckner-garry/bruckner-garry-account-1970-02/`.

## A 1970 photograph

A single photo, courtesy of **[Mike Lomker](/soldiers/lomker-michael/)** (jointly credited to John Williams, who does not yet have his own site profile), shows a howitzer position at Carolyn in 1970 — ammunition crates stacked into a protective wall around the gun pit, two soldiers working the piece, a second gun position visible across the field. No one in frame is identifiable. This is the first photographic evidence of the base found so far.
