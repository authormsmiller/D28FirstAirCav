## Overview

**FSB King** was a 1st Cavalry Division fire support base in the Long Khanh / Binh Tuy area (Military Region 3, III Corps), held by **D Company, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry** in late spring 1971. It was **split off from FSB Fanning** on or about **24 May 1971**: the battalion divided Fanning into two firebases of **three 105mm howitzers each**, and King took the unusual shape seen in Jim Garvin's aerial -- adopted, with the reduced strength, in the hope it would be easier to defend. The jungle sat uncomfortably close to the wire. The firebase gazetteer logs 2/8 Cav at King from **20 May to 1 June 1971** (battalion-level), and McGrew's field calendar records the base being **dismantled at the start of June** — a short-lived position. Garvin's Range Platoon photos are dated 24 May; the 20 May battalion start likely reflects an earlier platoon, with Range arriving a few days later.

A newspaper clipping in Garvin's collection gives King its reason for being: it was **built as the fire-support base for [Operation Thunder](/events/operation-thunder-1971-05/)** — a combined 2/8 Cav / Binh Tuy Regional Forces raid on the **Nui Be Mountains** (the MR-7 Rear Service Group), roughly **20–28 May 1971**. D Company staged from Ham Tan airfield and air-assaulted into the mountains under King's guns, returning on 28 May; King was torn down on 1 June once the operation closed. King's whole short life maps onto Thunder.

## What the deck shows

- **The firebase** -- an aerial showing King's distinctive shape and only three gun-position rings.
- **Logistics** -- a typical **resupply "log"** staged at King before going out to the company: boxes of rations, green bags of clothes, red bags of mail.
- **People** -- **Ray Graham** (heading to the field, rucksack beside the ration boxes) and **Lt. Guidara** at the log; and the battalion commander, **Col. Bacon**, in conversation with **Lt. Guidara** on the base.
- **Air lift** -- a Sky Crane working the base.
