*The following has been paraphrased for clarity and punctuation from Poore's own handwritten notes. The content and voice are otherwise his own.*

Thinking back to the April ambush: Stan Dillon had come back to Range Two, and he, Stan Sargent, and I moved up — and then the explosion. As I could see people, I pointed them back up the creek.

I clearly remember the hole in Stan Dillon's hand.

As it quieted down, I heard movement, then saw it was Bill Holtsclaw, our RTO. I went to him and covered for him. He assessed himself and waved, and I grabbed his hands. I could see a soldier dead on either side of me. They looked so at peace.

Bill H. and I got back across the creek — Bill H. wounded, and the other wounded were taken off. I stayed with Stan Sargent, who I knew was dying but wasn't dead yet. I think for a while we were the front line, alone.

I heard at a reunion, behind my back, people asking why I didn't leave him and go help others. I guess, besides what I've already said, it could have been exhaustion.

I declined to help recover the bodies — I was afraid they wouldn't be there, or wouldn't be as I remembered seeing them.

I went on to walk point a lot for the new guys ("cherries") with C Company, 1st Battalion, 8th Cavalry, during stand-down with the 1st Cav. I took the job and it gave me confidence — I actually preferred it. I wish, sometimes, that I'd been on point that day.
