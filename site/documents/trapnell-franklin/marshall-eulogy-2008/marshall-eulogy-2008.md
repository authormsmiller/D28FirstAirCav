A first-person remembrance written by **[Franklin "Buck" Trapnell](/soldiers/trapnell-franklin/)** — the wartime Recon Platoon Leader and later D Company XO — for the Angry Skipper Association newsletter (June 2008), reporting on the funeral of **[MSG Clint R. Marshall](/soldiers/marshall-clint/)**, his Reconnaissance Platoon Sergeant in D Company, 2/8 Cavalry.

## What it covers

Trapnell recounts meeting Marshall at Fort Benning in **early 1965**, when they were Platoon Leader and Platoon Sergeant of the **Recon Platoon, Company D, 1st Battalion–511th Infantry, 11th Air Assault Division** — the unit soon redesignated **Company D, 2nd Battalion, 8th Cavalry**. He sketches Marshall's life: born and raised in **Snyder, Oklahoma**; an older brother killed by a kamikaze off Okinawa in WWII; enlistment in **1948** at age 19; jump school and service with the **82nd** and **101st Airborne**, and in **Korea and Germany**; marriage to Jo Ann Hines and seven children; and **~22 years in the Army**, retiring in 1970.

The two men reconnected by telephone in **November 2007** after forty-one years out of touch, and "talked over old times and in particular our **battle on 4 November 1965 in the Ia Drang Valley**" — Trapnell explicitly points readers to the after-action report (the same 2/8 Cav AAR held in this archive). They planned to meet at the May 2008 Angry Skipper reunion in Salt Lake City, but Marshall died **10 February 2008** before that could happen.

Trapnell drove to Snyder, attended the burial "with full military honors" on **13 February 2008**, and was asked to speak at the graveside. Of Marshall he said he "had the magic of small unit leadership," and he reflected on "the gift that small-town America continues to provide the nation's military by way of its sons and daughters."

> *"He used to tell people that when he was leading he had never lost a man."*
