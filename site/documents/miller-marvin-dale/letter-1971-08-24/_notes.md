# Archivist Notes — Letter 24 August 1971 (MDM-LETTER-19710824)

## Date Context and Sequencing

August 24, 1971 — written eleven days before the September 3 letter and exactly twenty days after Marvin's promotion to E-5 on August 4. He does not mention the promotion in this letter.

Letter sequence established:
- May 22, 1971 — first letter after April 20 contact
- August 24, 1971 — this letter (11 days before September 3)
- September 3, 1971 — FSB Jeffreys letter

There is a gap of approximately three months between May 22 and August 24. Whether letters were written during this period and lost, or whether Marvin wrote less frequently during the summer months, is unknown. The July 1971 personnel rotation, his promotion on August 4, and the transition to a new role as squad leader may have affected his writing rhythm.

## "I haven't been too busy lately" — Operational Tempo

August 1971 was a period of reduced operational activity for many US units as Vietnamization accelerated and American forces drew down. The fact that Marvin was "not too busy" in late August may reflect the broader operational picture — fewer patrols, less field time, more base time. The September 3 letter confirms the move to FSB Jeffreys happened around this period, and a base transition often meant a period of settling in with reduced patrol tempo.

## The Photography Logistics

This letter contains the most specific information in the collection about Marvin's photography operation. He was sending exposed film to a commercial photo developing firm for processing, and switching to a cheaper one — "about half as much to develop them there." The problem: the new firm did not accept COD payments, which was how he had apparently been paying the first firm. Proposed solutions in order:

1. Get money orders — possible if he could access Bien Hoa (the rear)
2. Have his mother send him US dollars ("greenbacks") directly

"Greenbacks" is significant. US paper currency was officially prohibited in Vietnam under the MPC system. Marvin asking his mother to send him actual dollars if necessary suggests either that this rule was loosely enforced in practice, or that he was aware of the workaround and comfortable using it as a fallback.

Switching firms to save money suggests Marvin was developing film regularly enough that the cost differential mattered — consistent with the 50–60 photographs from his tour that survive. He was an active, systematic photographer.

## Bien Hoa as "The Rear"

"Maybe I can get the money orders if I go back to the rear (Bien Hoa)" — he parenthetically identifies Bien Hoa as "the rear," confirming it as the battalion's administrative base. The fact that he says "if I go back" rather than "when I go back" suggests access to Bien Hoa was irregular from the field.

## "Mothers are about the only really consistent writers"

One of the most direct and unguarded observations in the letters. Marvin was getting a letter from his mother approximately every three days — roughly ten per month, sustained across his entire tour. He notes this in the plural — "we fellows," "we read" — speaking for a group of men sharing an experience of mail call. Letters from home were a communal morale resource as much as a personal one.

"It's usually the first letter we read too." He's telling his mother, in the understated way he tells her everything, that what she's been doing matters.

## Transcription Notes

- Marvin spells "develop" as "develope" and "accept" as "except" — his natural spelling, preserved as written.
- "Mothers are about the only really consistent writers we fellows have over here" — "have" appears written above the line as an insertion, added after he wrote the sentence. Shows him re-reading as he wrote.
- Letter ends mid-close — "Well it's getting too dark to write anymore / Love / Marv" — suggesting he was writing by natural light and the day was ending.
- Faint mirror-writing visible through the page from a previous letter in the same stack — not part of this letter.
