# Archivist Notes — Letter 3 September 1971 (MDM-LETTER-19710903)

## Date Context

September 3, 1971 — this letter is the direct primary source confirmation of the movement timeline entry: "3 Sep 1971 — Moved to FSB Jeffreys, not far from a Vietnamese village." Marvin had approximately three months remaining in-country (DEROS December 2, 1971).

As of this date, Marvin had been a Sergeant for exactly 30 days (date of rank: August 4, 1971). He does not mention the promotion.

## Short-Timer Psychology

The opening paragraph is the most psychologically revealing passage of any letter in the collection. "The days keep chugging by. I don't like to think about it too much, the time doesn't go as fast then if you're thinking about it all the time." This is a precise description of the short-timer's cognitive strategy — deliberately not counting the days, because counting makes the waiting unbearable. The later November 24 letter describes this explicitly as a "short-timer's attitude," but here in September it is already present, undeclared.

## Birthday

"I guess my birthday this month too" — characteristic understatement, almost an afterthought. Marvin turned 22 on September 15, 1971, twelve days after this letter, while stationed at FSB Jeffreys in Long Khanh Province. He would be home by December 2.

## FSB Jeffreys — Confirmed and Named

This letter is the primary source document establishing that FSB Jeffreys existed and that Delta Company was there on September 3, 1971.

**The spelling:** Marvin writes "Jeffreys" — his phonetic rendering of the name as he heard it in the field. WO1 Gabriel A. Jeffries Jr., for whom the base was almost certainly named, has his surname recorded as "Jeffries" on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial Wall. The variant is not an error; it's how the name circulated verbally among soldiers.

**The proximity to a village:** "It's not very far from a Vietnamese village." This detail has potential geographic value — FSB locations near villages can sometimes be identified on period maps using village locations as reference points.

## The PS and the Financial Arrangement

"I didn't know if I had any money left or not. So go ahead and use it for the film or anything else you need."

Marvin kept approximately $10 per month for personal use and sent the remainder of his pay home to his mother, who managed it on his behalf. He was not tracking his own balance — she held it. An E-5 infantryman in Vietnam in 1971 earned approximately $350–400 per month including combat pay and hazardous duty pay. If Marvin kept $10 and sent the rest, he was sending approximately $340 per month.

He explicitly names "film" as the first suggested use — photographic film, consistent with the August 24 letter's fuller account of his switching developing firms.

## Personnel Context — July 1971 Rotation

Based on accounts from Dale Tincher and Val Romani, a rotation of personnel occurred in July 1971. This is consistent with the broader Vietnamization drawdown. Wayne Robinson, listed as Marvin's squad leader in April 1971, may have rotated out during this period, which would have opened the squad leader position Marvin subsequently filled upon his E-5 promotion in August.

## Transcription Notes

- "Hope [crossed out word] everyone is feeling alright" — Marvin wrote a word then crossed it out before writing "everyone." The crossed-out word appears to be "all" or "an" — he likely started "Hope all" and corrected himself. The strikethrough is deliberate.
- "Jeffreys" — preserved exactly as Marvin wrote it; see spelling note above.
- "develope," "except" — Marvin's natural spelling, preserved as written.
- All other text is clearly legible.
