# Event — Contact, November 4, 1965 (Pleiku Campaign)
## Archive ID: MDM-EVENT-19651104
## Status: PARTIAL — three D/2-8 KIA confirmed; direct unit account of the action still pending (NARA RG 472)
## Compiled: June 2026

---

### Summary

On November 4, 1965, three soldiers of Delta Company, 2nd Battalion (Airborne),
8th Cavalry were killed in action during the Pleiku Campaign in the Central
Highlands of South Vietnam — Sergeant Richard Coffey, Corporal Eddie Hill, and
Private First Class Wright Hamill. All three were classified "hostile, died
outright," ground casualties. They are memorialized together on the Vietnam
Veterans Memorial: Coffey and Hamill on Panel 3E, Line 17; Hill on Panel 3E,
Line 18.

At this date the battalion was operating under the 1st Brigade, 1st Cavalry
Division (Airmobile) during Operation *All the Way* (27 October – 9 November
1965), the search-and-destroy pursuit that followed the siege of Plei Me and
preceded the Ia Drang battles. The operational record currently in hand — the
1st Battalion, 8th Cavalry daily staff journal for the day, and the 2nd
Battalion's own after-action report for 9–10 November — frames the operation,
the area, and the chain of command, but does **not** yet contain the direct,
hour-by-hour account of the action in which these three men died. That record
is expected in NARA Record Group 472 (see Open Questions).

### The Three Killed — November 4, 1965

- **SGT Richard Coffey** — Sergeant, D/2-8 Cav. MOS 11F4P, Infantry Operations
  and Intelligence Specialist (Airborne qualified). Born October 21, 1941; home
  of record Los Angeles, California. Age 24 — he had turned 24 just two weeks
  before his death. Hostile, died outright; ground casualty. Vietnam Veterans
  Memorial, Panel 3E, Line 17.
- **CPL Eddie Hill** — Corporal, D/2-8 Cav. Born July 7, 1940; home of record
  Mobile, Alabama. Age 25. Hostile, died outright; ground casualty. Vietnam
  Veterans Memorial, Panel 3E, Line 18.
- **PFC Wright Hamill** — Private First Class, D/2-8 Cav. MOS 62E2P, Heavy
  Construction Equipment Operator (Airborne qualified). Born August 26, 1946;
  home of record Albany, Oregon. Age 19. Hostile, died outright; ground
  casualty. Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Panel 3E, Line 17.

The shared casualty date, the adjacent Wall panel lines, and the identical
casualty classification ("hostile, died outright") are consistent with all
three falling in a single action, though the documents in hand do not yet
confirm that they died in the same engagement. The three came from across the
country — Los Angeles, Mobile, and Albany, Oregon — and ranged in age from 19
to 25.

### Operational Context

**The campaign.** The 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile) had arrived in Vietnam in
September 1965 and was based at An Khe. By late October the division was
committed to the Pleiku Campaign in the Central Highlands. The 1st Brigade ran
Operation *All the Way* from 27 October to 9 November 1965 — a search-and-destroy
pursuit of People's Army of Vietnam forces withdrawing after the failed siege of
Plei Me, across roughly 1,500 square miles north and west of Plei Me. November 4
falls squarely inside this operation.

**The base.** Both 8th Cavalry battalions were operating from a forward base
designated **STADIUM** (recorded as grid ~YA 201342; the 1/8 journal renders the
grid as ZA 202342 — OCR/transcription variance). The 2/8 after-action report
places its battalion at STADIUM, and the 1/8 journal repeatedly references
companies moving "in route to stadium" and "back to stadium location" on
November 4.

**The chain of command (from the 2/8 AAR).** Per **1st Brigade Frag Order
Number 135 of 7 November 1965**, the 2nd Battalion (Abn), 8th Cavalry was
operating under 1st Brigade in early November and became attached to the 3rd
Brigade on 9 November, then moved by CV-2 and C-123 aircraft from STADIUM to the
division base at An Khe on 10 November. This confirms — from the battalion's own
paperwork — that on **November 4 the battalion was a 1st Brigade unit.** The
same report records the battalion's dispositions as of 9 November: Company D on
the northern half of the STADIUM perimeter, Company B on the southern half,
Company C as battalion reserve, and Company A detached to the 1st Battalion, 8th
Cavalry to secure the Plei Me airfield on 8 November.

**What the 1/8 journal shows for November 4.** The sister battalion (1/8 Cav)
conducted a first-light air assault with its B and D Companies, swept the
objective area, and reported contact — including a company in contact at the
northwest corner of the landing zone (one enemy killed, one weapon and two
grenades recovered) and "2 KIA — A Co" logged at 0955. The journal also records
that "C 2/8 is attached to 1/8 as of 0940 to protect [the] Hard Anvil element."
These entries establish that 4 November was an active, lethal day in the immediate
area and that 1/8 and 2/8 elements were interlaced — but the companies maneuvering
in this journal are 1/8's own (the journal tags the attached 2/8 company explicitly
as "C 2/8"), so this is **not** a direct record of the D/2-8 action.

### What the Records Do and Don't Show

The documents currently in hand let us place these three deaths in time, in
campaign, in brigade, and in general area with confidence. What they do not yet
provide is the direct, contemporaneous account of where D Company, 2/8 was
operating on 4 November and how these men were killed. The 1/8 journal logs
1/8's companies; the 2/8 after-action report does not begin until 9 November.
The record that would close this gap is the **2/8 (or 1st Brigade) daily staff
journal / after-action report for 1–8 November 1965**, held in NARA Record Group
472 (Records of U.S. Forces in Southeast Asia) and not part of the digitized
online holdings. A narrow reproduction request for that date range is the logical
next acquisition.
